Summary

This report is intended to provide Members of Congress and their staff with information and resources regarding federal funding for programs that may support local emergency communications projects. The report provides a brief overview of selected grant and loan programs organized by agency. Agencies covered include the U.S. Department of Homeland Security's (DHS's) Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), the U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC), the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), and the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT). For each program identified, information is included on eligible applicants and allowable costs. Table A-1 includes links to each program's website and most recent Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO). Additionally, the report includes selected resources from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), in DHS, on identifying emergency communications programs and funding and sustaining emergency communications projects.

Introduction

This report is intended to provide Members of Congress and their staffs with information and resources regarding federal funding programs to support local emergency communications projects. According to the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), during an incident, "emergency communications may include alerts and warnings; directives about evacuation, curfews, and other self-protection actions; and information about response status, family members, available assistance, and other matters that impact response and recovery."1

This report provides a brief overview of selected grant and loan programs organized by agency. Agencies covered include the U.S. Department of Homeland Security's (DHS) Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), the U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC), the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), and the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT). For each program identified, this report includes the program's website, its Assistance Listing Number,2 and information on program eligibility. Table A-1 includes links to each program's website and most recent Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO).

This report also highlights resources from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), within DHS, on identifying emergency communications programs and funding and sustaining emergency communications projects.

Information in this report is intended to assist with a variety of potential emergency communication circumstances. Thus, the report identifies and describes an array of programs and funding sources that interested parties could assess in the context of particular situations and needs. The listed programs and funding sources typically detail the purposes and availability of the grants to foster such assessments.

Agency Programs

U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS), Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)

FEMA's preparedness grants "support our citizens and first responders to ensure we work together as a nation to build, sustain and improve our capability to prepare for, protect against, respond to, recover from and mitigate terrorism and other high-consequence disasters and emergencies."3 Many of FEMA's preparedness grant programs list interoperable communications equipment as an allowable expense.4

Grants vary in funding levels and mechanisms. For some grants, FEMA allocates funds to states and territories on a formula basis. In these instances, every state and territory receives funding, but funding varies across jurisdictions based on an assessment of risk of terrorism or disaster. For other grants, applicants compete for funding based on certain pre-set criteria.

Eligibility also varies across programs. In some cases, FEMA awards grants directly to states. States then award funding to subrecipients, typically local entities and agencies. For certain grants, FEMA pre-selects or pre-determines eligible subrecipients. In other cases, tribal or local entities (e.g., fire departments, transit agencies) apply directly. Typically, these grants are reviewed, rated, ranked, and selected on a competitive basis.5

FEMA's Preparedness Grants Manual provides interested parties with information on "policies and procedures for managing preparedness grants; guidance specific to each grant; [and] rules and regulations that guide the proper management of FEMA grants."6

Below are high-level descriptions of various FEMA preparedness grants that could potentially be used to fund emergency communications projects. The cited sources contain additional details on the purposes and availability of the grants.

Homeland Security Grant Program (HSGP)

The HSGP includes three individual grant programs aimed at helping states, local governments, tribes, and territories in "preventing, protecting against, mitigating, responding to and recovering from acts of terrorism and other threats."7 These programs fund "a range of preparedness activities, including planning, organization, equipment purchase, training, exercises, and management and administration across all core capabilities and mission areas."8

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/homeland-security
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.067
  • Eligible applicants: state administrative agencies (SAAs)9

There are three grant programs under HSGP:

  • 1. State Homeland Security Program. These grants "support the implementation of risk-driven, capabilities-based State Homeland Security Strategies to address capability targets."10 Funding is provided to states, which are required to pass through at least 80% of the funds to tribal or local entities.11 Allowable expenses include personnel training; exercises and simulations; and procuring technology or equipment to support surveillance, communications, and data analysis.12
  • 2. Urban Area Security Initiative. This program "provides funding to enhance regional preparedness and capabilities in designated high-threat, high-density areas."13 Funding is provided to states, which are required to pass through at least 80% of the funds to tribal or local entities.14 Allowable expenses include personnel training; exercises and simulations; and procuring technology or equipment to support surveillance, communications, and data analysis.15
  • 3. Operation Stonegarden. This program "provides funding to enhance cooperation and coordination among state, local, tribal, territorial, and federal law enforcement agencies to jointly enhance security along the United States land and water borders."16 States submit applications on behalf of eligible subrecipients ("county-level or equivalent governments and federally recognized tribal governments in states bordering Canada or Mexico, or those with international water borders").17 Allowable expenses include interoperable data systems, such as computer-aided dispatch systems and records management systems.18

Assistance to Firefighters Grants

This program helps "firefighters and other first responders obtain critically needed equipment, protective gear, emergency vehicles, training and other resources necessary for protecting the public and emergency personnel from fire and related hazards."19

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/firefighters/assistance-grants
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.044
  • Eligible applicants: local fire department and nonaffiliated emergency medical services organizations (i.e., those that are not part of hospitals)20
  • Allowable expenses: purchase of a variety of communications equipment, including base stations, headsets, mobile and portable radios, and mobile data terminals21

For additional information on this and other programs supporting fire departments, see CRS In Focus IF12806, Connecting Constituents with Federal Programs for Firefighters and Fire Departments, by Carol Canada.

Emergency Management Performance Grant Program

This program "provides state, local, tribal and territorial emergency management agencies with the resources required for implementation of the National Preparedness System."22

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/emergency-management-performance
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.042
  • Eligible applicants: SAAs or state emergency management agencies23
  • Allowable expenses: interoperable communications equipment24

For more information on the National Preparedness System, see CRS Report R46696, National Preparedness: A Summary and Select Issues, by Shawn Reese and Lauren R. Stienstra.

Emergency Operations Center (EOC) Grant Program

The EOC program "is intended to improve emergency management and preparedness capabilities by supporting flexible, sustainable, secure, strategically located, and fully interoperable EOCs with a focus on addressing identified deficiencies and needs."25

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/emergency-operations-center
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.052
  • Eligible applicants: Only SAAs "on behalf of state and local units of government, with identified projects"26
  • Allowable expenses: interoperable communications equipment that "directly support[s] the function and operation of the EOC"27

Intercity Bus Security Grant Program

This program "helps protect surface transportation infrastructure and the traveling public from acts of terrorism and increase the resilience of transit infrastructure."28

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/intercity-bus-security
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.057
  • Eligible applicants: "private operators providing intercity over-the-road bus transportation that have also completed a vulnerability assessment and developed a security plan, which the Secretary of Homeland Security has approved as described in Section 1531 of the Implementing Recommendations of the 9/11 Commission Act of 2007 (9/11 Act) (P.L. 110-53) (6 U.S.C. §1181)"29
  • Allowable expenses: interoperable communications equipment, including alert and warning capabilities for intercity bus systems30

Intercity Passenger Rail—Amtrak Program

This program "provides funds to protect critical surface transportation infrastructure and the traveling public from acts of terrorism and increase the resilience of the Amtrak rail system."31

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/intercity-passenger-rail-amtrak
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.157
  • Eligible applicants: only Amtrak32
  • Allowable expenses: interoperable communications equipment, including alert and warning capabilities for rail systems33

Next Generation Warning System Grant Program

This program "enhances alert and warning capabilities and improves resiliency of emergency alert and warning systems through effective services, solutions, and technology to improve the timely delivery of public safety information for state and tribal partners" in collaboration with the FEMA Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS).34 One of the objectives is to "integrate effective, reliable, available, and timely alert and warning capabilities to enhance public emergency communications."35

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/practitioners/integrated-public-alert-warning-system/ngwsp
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.138
  • Eligible applicants: states or Indian tribes (tribal nations) as defined in 2 C.F.R. §200.136
  • Allowable expenses: "installation of IPAWS-compatible sirens and/or 'giant-voice systems'"37

For additional information on IPAWS—the national alerting system that state, local, and tribal entities can use to distribute alerts—see CRS Report R48363, The Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS): Primer and Issues for Congress, by Brian E. Humphreys.

Nonprofit Security Grant Program

This program "provides funding support for target hardening and other physical security enhancements and activities to nonprofit organizations that are at high risk of terrorist attack."38

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/nonprofit-security
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.008
  • Eligible applicants: "All states, territories and FEMA identified high-risk urban areas SAAs are deemed the only applicants eligible to apply to FEMA for funding. Nonprofit organizations are eligible as subapplicants to SAAs. Nonprofit organizations may NOT apply directly."39
  • Allowable expenses: such communications equipment as handheld or mobile public address systems, public notification and warning systems, portable radios, paging systems and services, and intercom systems40

For additional information, see CRS In Focus IF13201, Soft Targets and the Nonprofit Security Grant Program, by Shawn Reese.

Port Security Grant Program

This program "provides funding to state, local and private-sector partners to help protect critical port infrastructure from terrorism, enhance maritime domain awareness, improve port-wide maritime security risk management, and maintain or reestablish maritime security mitigation protocols that support port recovery and resiliency capabilities."41

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/port-security
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.056
  • Eligible applicants: port authorities; facility operators; and state, local, and territorial government agencies42
  • Allowable expenses: procuring technology or equipment to support surveillance, communications, and data analysis43

State and Local Cybersecurity Grant Program

This program "provides funding to eligible entities to address cybersecurity risks and threats to information systems owned or operated by, or on behalf of, state, local, or tribal governments."44

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/state-local-cybersecurity-grant-program
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.137
  • Eligible applicants: "All 56 states and territories" as codified in 6 U.S.C. §101(17)45
  • Allowable expenses: "Investments in emergency communications systems and equipment that meets applicable SAFECOM Guidance on Emergency Communications Grants recommendations"46

Transit Security Grant Program

This program "provides funding to eligible public transportation systems (which include intra-city bus, ferries and all forms of passenger rail) to protect critical transportation infrastructure and the travelling public from terrorism, and to increase transportation infrastructure resilience."47

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/transit-security
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.075
  • Eligible applicants: transit authorities including passenger rail, intra-city bus, and ferry systems48
  • Allowable expenses: acquisition of interoperable communications equipment, including alert and warning capabilities49

Tribal Cybersecurity Grant Program

This program "provides funding to eligible entities to address cybersecurity risks and threats to information systems owned or operated by, or on behalf of tribal governments."50

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/tribal-cybersecurity-grant-program
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.156
  • Eligible applicants: federally recognized Indian/Native American/Alaska Native tribal government51
  • Allowable expenses: "investments in emergency communications systems and equipment [that meets] applicable SAFECOM Guidance on Emergency Communications Grants recommendations"52

Tribal Homeland Security Grant Program

This program provides tribal nations resources "to enhance planning, training, equipment, and operational coordination that directly supports prevention, protection, mitigation, and response capabilities."53

  • Program website: https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/tribal-homeland-security
  • Assistance Listing Number: 97.150
  • Eligible applicants: federally recognized Indian/Native American/Alaska Native tribal government54
  • Allowable expenses: procurement of technology or equipment to support surveillance, communications, and data analysis55

U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)

USDA administers several grant and loan programs that may be used to enhance public safety communications. Typically, eligibility is limited to entities in rural areas. For additional information on eligibility as well as programmatic guidance, applicants can contact their USDA Rural Development state offices.56

Community Connect Grants

This program "provides financial assistance to eligible applicants that will provide broadband service in rural, economically-challenged communities where service does not exist."57 A rural area is any area that "is not located within: A city, town, or incorporated area that has a population of greater than 20,000 inhabitants; or an urbanized area contiguous and adjacent to a city or town that has a population of greater than 50,000 inhabitants" (7 C.F.R. §1739.3). In some cases, previous Community Connect awardees used the funding to expand connections to public safety opportunities.58

  • Program website: https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/telecommunications-programs/community-connect-grants.
  • Assistance Listing Number: 10.863
  • Eligible applicants: incorporated organizations; federally recognized Tribes; state and local government units; and other legal entities, including cooperatives, private corporations, or limited liability companies organized on a for-profit or not-for-profit basis59
  • Allowable expenses: "These grant funds will be made available to eligible applicants to construct broadband networks that provide service on a community-oriented connectivity basis in rural areas."60

For additional information on federal assistance for broadband, see CRS In Focus IF12859, Connecting Constituents with Federal Assistance for Broadband, by Rachael D. Roan.

Community Facilities Direct Loan and Grant Program

Also referred to as Community Facilities Loans and Grants,61 this program

provides affordable funding to develop essential community facilities in rural areas. An essential community facility is defined as a facility that provides an essential service to the local community for the orderly development of the community in a primarily rural area, and does not include private, commercial or business undertakings.62

Examples of community facilities include "those providing or supporting overall community development such as hospitals, fire stations, child care facilities, food recovery and distribution centers; assisted-living facilities; group homes, mental health clinics, and shelters; and education facilities."63

  • Program website: https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/community-facilities/community-facilities-direct-loan-grant-program
  • Assistance Listing Number: 10.766
  • Eligible applicants: public bodies, community-based nonprofit corporations, and Federally Recognized Tribes.64 Borrowers must be located in "rural areas including cities, villages, townships and towns including Federally Recognized Tribal Lands with no more than 20,000 residents according to the latest U.S. Census Data."65
  • Allowable expenses: Telecommunication or video surveillance, among other uses.66 Direct loans may be used for procurement of "E-Connectivity end-user equipment to include distance learning equipment, telemedicine equipment, and health information technology equipment as well as internal wiring and publicly available Wi-Fi capability within a facility."67

Telecommunications Infrastructure Loans and Loan Guarantees

Also referred to as Rural Telecommunications loans,68 this program "provides financing for the construction, maintenance, improvement and expansion of telephone service and broadband in rural areas."69 For 911 and emergency communications purposes, rural area is defined as "any area of the United States, its territories and insular possessions … not included within the boundaries of any incorporated or unincorporated city, village, or borough having a population exceeding … 20,000" inhabitants.70

  • Program website: https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/telecommunications-programs/telecommunications-infrastructure-loans-loan-guarantees
  • Assistance Listing Number: 10.851
  • Eligible applicants: "State and local government entities; Federally Recognized Tribes; non-profits, including Cooperatives, and limited dividend or mutual associations; [and] for-profit businesses (must be a corporation of limited liability company)"71
  • Allowable expenses: "the improvement, expansion, construction, acquisition, and operation of telephone lines, facilities, or systems to furnish and improve Telecommunications, 911 and Emergency Communications services in rural areas"72

U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC)

The National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) in DOC has administered public safety grant programs in the past, including the $1 billion Public Safety Interoperable Communications grant.73 Previously, NTIA also administered two 911 grant programs with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.74 NTIA does not currently administer any public-safety-specific grant programs. However, NTIA does administer several large broadband programs, including the Tribal Broadband Connectivity Program described below, which may be used to support public safety communications and connectivity.

DOC, through NTIA, also oversees the First Responder Network Authority (FirstNet) network, a nationwide broadband network for public safety use.75 While there are no grants affiliated with FirstNet, revenue earned from subscriber fees is reinvested back into FirstNet.76 Public safety agencies using FirstNet can work with state officials to convey coverage and service needs to FirstNet and its partner, AT&T.77

For additional information on FirstNet, see CRS Report R48923, First Responder Network (FirstNet) Authority: Reauthorization and Selected Issues, by Colby Leigh Pechtol.

Tribal Broadband Connectivity Program

These grants are "to be used for broadband deployment on tribal lands, as well as for telehealth, distance learning, broadband affordability, and adoption."78 In previous rounds of funding, some award recipients used funding to improve access to public safety.79

  • Program website: https://broadbandusa.ntia.gov/funding-programs/tribal-broadband-connectivity
  • Assistance Listing Number: 11.029
  • Eligible applicants: "(i) a Tribal Government; (ii) a Tribal College or University; (iii) the Department of Hawaiian Home Lands on behalf of the Native Hawaiian Community, including Native Hawaiian Education Programs; (iv) a Tribal organization; or (v) an Alaska Native Corporation"80
  • Allowable expenses: "deployment and adoption of broadband service on Tribal Land and for programs that promote the use of broadband to access 'remote learning, telework, or telehealth resources'"81

U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ)

DOJ administers grants that may fund communications equipment and capabilities for law enforcement, the courts, and criminal justice entities.

Coordinated Tribal Assistance Solicitation

This program is "designed to provide funding directly to federally recognized tribes and tribal consortia to develop a comprehensive and coordinated approach to improve public safety and victim services in tribal communities."82

  • Program website: https://cops.usdoj.gov/ctas
  • Assistance Listing Numbers: 16.710 (Purpose Area 1: Public Safety and Community Policing Tribal Resources Grant Program)
  • Eligible applicants: Federally recognized Tribal governments or consortia consisting of two or more federally recognized Indian Tribes. Tribal designees are eligible for certain activities related to the Children's Justice Act Partnership for Indian Communities.83
  • Allowable expenses: computer hardware and software, mobile data terminals, radios, and communications systems such as those used for crime reporting and accessing national databases84

Grants to Improve the Criminal Justice Response Program

This program "assists state, local, and tribal governments, courts, and victim service providers to improve the criminal justice response to domestic violence, sexual assault, dating violence, and stalking as serious violations of criminal law, and to seek safety and autonomy for victims."85

  • Program website: https://www.justice.gov/ovw/grants-improve-criminal-justice-response-program-fact-sheet
  • Assistance Listing Number: 16.590. Part of DOJ's Grants to Encourage Arrest Policies and Enforcement of Protection Orders Program.
  • Eligible applicants: various government entities (including state governments, Indian tribal governments, units of local government, and state and local courts) and nonprofit entities (including state or territorial domestic violence coalitions, sexual assault coalitions, tribal domestic violence and sexual assault coalitions, and victim service providers)86
  • Allowable expenses: "to develop and install data collection and communication systems, including computerized systems, and training on how to use these systems effectively to link police, prosecutors, courts, and Tribal jurisdictions for the purpose of identifying and tracking protection orders and violations of protection orders, in those jurisdictions where such systems do not exist or are not fully effective"87

School Violence Prevention Program

This program provides "awards directly to States, units of local government, or Indian tribes to improve security at schools and on school grounds in the jurisdiction of the grantee through evidence-based school safety programs and technology."88

  • Program website: https://cops.usdoj.gov/svpp
  • Assistance Listing Number: 16.071
  • Eligible applicants: "States, units of local government, Indian tribes, and their public agencies including: School districts, including public charter schools and school districts with a single school; School boards; [and] Law enforcement agencies"89
  • Allowable expenses include "acquisition and installation of technology for expedited notification of local law enforcement during an emergency."90

U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT)

Selected DOT grants allow for spending on technologies, including communication technologies, that may improve transportation safety. The following grants are targeted at various modes of transportation and could potentially support communication projects that are aimed at enhancing public safety.

Consolidated Rail Infrastructure and Safety Improvements Program

This program "provides funding for projects that improve the safety, efficiency, and reliability of intercity passenger and freight rail."91

  • Program website: https://railroads.dot.gov/grants-loans/consolidated-rail-infrastructure-and-safety-improvements-crisi-program
  • Assistance Listing Number: 20.337
  • Eligible applicants: States, federally recognized Tribes, selected railroads and rail carriers, Amtrak, Transportation Research Boards, and other entities. For a complete list, see pages 4-5 of the FY2025-2026 NOFO.92
  • Allowable expenses: deployment of railroad safety technology, including communications equipment93

Safe Streets and Roads for All Grant Program

This program "funds regional, local, and Tribal initiatives through grants to prevent roadway fatalities and serious injuries."94

  • Program website: https://www.transportation.gov/grants/SS4A
  • Assistance Listing Number: 20.939
  • Eligible applicants: "1. a metropolitan planning organization (MPO); 2. a political subdivision of a State or territory; 3. a federally recognized Tribal government; and 4. a multijurisdictional group of entities made up of any of the aforementioned three types of entities"95
  • Allowable expenses: "programs or technologies used by public safety agencies (e.g. … improved emergency communications) to prevent, respond to, and/or reduce the severity of roadway crashes"96

Tribal Transportation Program Safety Fund

This program addresses "transportation safety issues identified by federally recognized Indian tribes through a competitive, discretionary program. Projects are chosen whose outcomes will reduce fatal injuries in transportation related incidents."97

  • Program website: https://highways.dot.gov/federal-lands/tribal/safety/funds
  • Assistance Listing Number: 20.205. This is part of the Federal Highway Administration's Federal-Aid Highway Program, Federal Lands Highway Program.
  • Eligible applicants: "[F]ederally recognized Indian Tribes identified on the list of 'Indian Entities Recognized and Eligible to Receive Services from the Bureau of Indian Affairs' (published at 86 FR 18552 or future updates)."98
  • Allowable expenses: funding of projects planning integrated interoperable emergency communications equipment, and installation of vehicle-to-infrastructure communications equipment99

Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)

Emergency Communications

CISA's "Emergency Communications" landing page provides access to resources supporting emergency communications coordination, planning, and priority services. See https://www.cisa.gov/topics/emergency-communications.

Funding and Sustainment

Resources listed on CISA's "Funding and Sustainment" page "are available to help state, local, tribal, and territorial governments to identify available funding sources, plan emergency communications projects for federal grants, and inform decision-makers on the importance of funding and sustaining public safety communications systems." See https://www.cisa.gov/safecom/funding.

List of Emergency Communications Programs
