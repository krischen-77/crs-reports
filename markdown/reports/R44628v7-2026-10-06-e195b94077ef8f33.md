## Introduction

This report is intended to serve as a starting point for congressional offices covering voting and elections. It focuses on resources related to laws governing federal elections and voting. It outlines and describes some of the key sources of laws, pending legislation, regulations, cases, the Congressional Research Service (CRS) products, and federal agencies working in this subject area. This report also contains sections dedicated to the voting process, voting rights, campaign finance, and presidential elections.

## Election and Voting Law Resources

Election law in the United States is composed of limits and powers defined in the U.S. Constitution, as well as federal and state statutes, regulations, and court cases. This section identifies some of the major resources in each category.

### United States Constitution[1](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-06_R44628_93cfbf3f259e8fd07af0368992647acc3d1052d5.html#fn1)

The U.S. Constitution serves as the framework under which other election and voting laws operate.

CRS provides information about the Constitution in *The* *Constitution of the United States of America: Analysis and Interpretation* (CONAN).[2](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-06_R44628_93cfbf3f259e8fd07af0368992647acc3d1052d5.html#fn2) The [CONAN website](https://constitution.congress.gov/) presents the text of the Constitution along with analysis by CRS attorneys, including commentary on Supreme Court decisions that have interpreted the text.

### United States Code

The [*U**.**S**.* *Code*](http://uscode.house.gov/) is a consolidation and codification of the general and permanent laws of the United States, organized by subject matter. The *Code* is prepared by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel of the U.S. House of Representatives (OLRC).

In September of 2014, OLRC reorganized the provisions related to voting and elections, creating the new [Title 52, Voting and Elections](http://uscode.house.gov/browse/prelim@title52&edition=prelim). For more information about this change, see the OLRC website, "[Editorial Reclassification: Title 52, United States Code](http://uscode.house.gov/editorialreclassification/t52/index.html)."

Because of this reclassification, researchers may encounter references to outdated citations. OLRC provides a [table](http://uscode.house.gov/editorialreclassification/t52/Reclassifications_Title_52.pdf) with [former and current classifications](https://uscode.house.gov/classification/tables.shtml) side by side.

### Legislation

Congress.gov is the official website for U.S. federal legislative information. The website is developed and maintained by the Library of Congress using data originated and owned by the U.S. Senate and the U.S. House of Representatives. It is usually updated the morning after a House or Senate session adjourns.

Congress.gov coverage of bills begins in 1973, with full bill text beginning in 1993. Congress.gov categorizes legislation into policy areas and legislative subjects. For example, a search of the legislative subject "Elections, voting, political campaign regulation" will return a list of bills that can then be filtered by additional criteria. To filter by facets, such as Congress and Status of Legislation, use the tool along the left-hand side of the results' list.

### Regulations

Regulations, along with notices, proposed rules, and presidential documents, are published daily in the [*Federal Register*](https://www.federalregister.gov/). General and permanent rules are published annually in the *Code of Federal Regulations*, which is organized into titles according to subject area. The Government Publishing Office maintains the "[Electronic Code of Federal Regulations](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/ECFR?page=browse)," or e-CFR, an electronic version of the [*Code of Federal Regulations*](https://www.gpo.gov/fdsys/browse/collectionCfr.action?collectionCode=CFR). The e-CFR is updated frequently. Researchers can easily view "Last Amended Information" and a "Recent Changes" link per each title. [Title 11](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=0b7a5aa0a46d6d25d8f6fef592ab4d89&mc=true&tpl=/ecfrbrowse/Title11/11tab_02.tpl) contains regulations about federal elections.

### State Resources

This CRS report does not provide resources on voting laws for individual states. Instead, it directs users to resources for compilations of state laws. For an overview of issues related to elections and voting in the states, the National Conference of State Legislatures (NCSL) maintains an "[NCSL Elections Resources](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/ncsl-election-resources)" website.

HeinOnline, a subscription resource available to congressional offices through the CRS La Follette Congressional Reading Room, provides numerous Subject Compilations of State Laws categorized with the subject heading "[Voters and Voting](http://www.heinonline.org/HOL/SubjectCompilations?termsa=Voters%20and%20Voting&typea=sub_find&collection=scsl&submit=Find)." CRS law and reference librarians can assist congressional offices with HeinOnline Subject Compilations of State Laws on request.

### Federal Agencies[3](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-06_R44628_93cfbf3f259e8fd07af0368992647acc3d1052d5.html#fn3)

The [Federal Election Commission](http://www.fec.gov/) (FEC) is an independent regulatory agency created by Congress in 1975 to administer and enforce the Federal Election Campaign Act (FECA). The FEC's duties include disclosing public finance information, enforcing provisions of the FECA, and overseeing public funding of presidential elections.[4](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-06_R44628_93cfbf3f259e8fd07af0368992647acc3d1052d5.html#fn4)

The [Voting Section](https://www.justice.gov/crt/voting-section) of the Civil Rights Division of the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) enforces the civil provisions of federal laws that protect the right to vote, including the Voting Rights Act (VRA), the Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act (UOCAVA), the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA), the Help America Vote Act of 2002 (HAVA), and the Civil Rights Act (CRA). The DOJ provides a list and summary of these laws on its website "[Statutes Enforced by the Voting Section](https://www.justice.gov/crt/statutes-enforced-voting-section)."

The [U.S. Election Assistance Commission](https://www.eac.gov/) (EAC) is an independent, bipartisan commission established by HAVA.[5](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-06_R44628_93cfbf3f259e8fd07af0368992647acc3d1052d5.html#fn5) The EAC administers grant programs, provides for testing and certification of voting systems, and studies election issues. The EAC also assists election officials by issuing guidelines for voting systems and advice on implementing the act's requirements.[6](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-06_R44628_93cfbf3f259e8fd07af0368992647acc3d1052d5.html#fn6)

#### *Regulations*

Regulations promulgated by the Election Assistance Commission are published throughout the year in the [*Federal Register*](https://www.federalregister.gov/) and once a year in the annual update of the [*Code of Federal Regulations*](https://www.gpo.gov/fdsys/browse/collectionCfr.action?collectionCode=CFR). Regulations promulgated by the EAC are located in [Title 11, Chapter 2](http://www.ecfr.gov/cgi-bin/text-idx?SID=3e26e3e04f76b3921fd7cd82ae4fc8c8&mc=true&tpl=/ecfrbrowse/Title11/11chapterII.tpl).

### Campaign Finance[9](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-06_R44628_93cfbf3f259e8fd07af0368992647acc3d1052d5.html#fn9)

Campaign finance law addresses issues including fundraising, spending, and reporting. This section contains resources for conducting legal research on those topics.

#### Laws

##### Federal Statutes

[Federal Election Campaign Act of 1971](http://uscode.house.gov/statutes/pl/92/225.pdf) (FECA) (P.L. 92-225)

[Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act of 2002](http://uscode.house.gov/statutes/pl/107/155.pdf) (BCRA) (P.L. 107-155)

[Presidential Election Campaign Fund Act of 1966](http://uscode.house.gov/statutes/pl/89/809.pdf) (PECA) (P.L. 89-809)

[Presidential Primary Matching Payment Account Act](http://uscode.house.gov/statviewer.htm?volume=88&page=1297) of 1974 (P.L. 93-443)

#### *Cases*

Federal courts have played a significant role in shaping campaign finance law, including invalidating portions of the Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act. The FEC maintains websites on "[Selected Campaign Finance Law Court Cases](http://www.fec.gov/law/litigationmajor.shtml)" and a "[Court Case Alphabetical Index](https://www.fec.gov/legal-resources/court-cases/court-case-alphabetical-index/)."

CRS Report R45320, *Campaign Finance Law: An Analysis of Key Issues, Recent Developments, and Constitutional Considerations for Legislation*, by L. Paige Whitaker, discusses provisions of the Federal Election Campaign Act (FECA) and Supreme Court decisions relevant to campaign finance law and legislation. CRS Legislative Attorney L. Paige Whitaker also authored CRS Infographic IG10054, *Campaign Finance Law and the U.S. Supreme Court**.*

#### Legislation

##### Federal Legislation

CRS Report R41542, *The State of Campaign Finance Policy: Recent Developments and Issues for Congress*, by R. Sam Garrett,discusses recent legislative activity on campaign finance from the 115th through the 119th Congresses. CRS Insight IN12198, *Federal Election Commission Administrative Fine Program*, by R. Sam Garrett, also discusses recent legislative activity extending that program's expiration date.

Updated information on the status of the bills mentioned in these products is available from Congress.gov.
