Summary

This report is intended to provide Members of Congress and their staff with information and resources regarding federal funding for programs that may support local emergency communications projects. The report provides a brief overview of selected grant and loan programs organized by agency. Agencies covered include the U.S. Department of Homeland Security's (DHS's) Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), the U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC), the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), and the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT). For each program identified, information is included on eligible applicants and allowable costs. [**Table A-1**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#_Ref245211512) includes links to each program's website and most recent Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO). Additionally, the report includes selected resources from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), in DHS, on identifying emergency communications programs and funding and sustaining emergency communications projects.

Introduction

This report is intended to provide Members of Congress and their staffs with information and resources regarding federal funding programs to support local emergency communications projects. According to the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), during an incident, "emergency communications may include alerts and warnings; directives about evacuation, curfews, and other self-protection actions; and information about response status, family members, available assistance, and other matters that impact response and recovery."[1](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn1 "U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS), Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), \"Lesson 3. Communicating in an Emergency,\" February 2014, https://training.fema.gov/emiweb/is/is242b/student%20manual/sm_03.pdf .")

This report provides a brief overview of selected grant and loan programs organized by agency. Agencies covered include the U.S. Department of Homeland Security's (DHS) Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), the U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC), the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), and the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT). For each program identified, this report includes the program's website, its Assistance Listing Number,[2](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn2 "Grants.gov defines Assistance Listings as follows: \"Formally known as CFDA [ Catalog of Federal Domestic Assistance], the authoritative source of information about federal government programs that provide grants, loans, scholarships, insurance, and other types of assistance or benefits to the American public.\" Grants.gov defines Assistance Listing Number as \"a unique number, formally known as the CFDA Number, assigned to a program or activity with Assistance Listings. The first two digits...") and information on program eligibility. [**Table A-1**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#_Ref245211512) includes links to each program's website and most recent Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO).

This report also highlights resources from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), within DHS, on identifying emergency communications programs and funding and sustaining emergency communications projects.

Information in this report is intended to assist with a variety of potential emergency communication circumstances. Thus, the report identifies and describes an array of programs and funding sources that interested parties could assess in the context of particular situations and needs. The listed programs and funding sources typically detail the purposes and availability of the grants to foster such assessments.

Agency Programs

U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS), Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)

FEMA's preparedness grants "support our citizens and first responders to ensure we work together as a nation to build, sustain and improve our capability to prepare for, protect against, respond to, recover from and mitigate terrorism and other high-consequence disasters and emergencies."[3](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn3 "FEMA, \"Preparedness Grants,\" July 20, 2026, https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness .") Many of FEMA's preparedness grant programs list interoperable communications equipment as an allowable expense.[4](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn4 "For additional information on authorized interoperable communications equipment , see FEMA, \"Authorized Equipment List,\" April 18, 2024, https://www.fema.gov/grants/tools/authorized-equipment-list .")

Grants vary in funding levels and mechanisms. For some grants, FEMA allocates funds to states and territories on a formula basis. In these instances, every state and territory receives funding, but funding varies across jurisdictions based on an assessment of risk of terrorism or disaster. For other grants, applicants compete for funding based on certain pre-set criteria.

Eligibility also varies across programs. In some cases, FEMA awards grants directly to states. States then award funding to subrecipients, typically local entities and agencies. For certain grants, FEMA pre-selects or pre-determines eligible subrecipients. In other cases, tribal or local entities (e.g., fire departments, transit agencies) apply directly. Typically, these grants are reviewed, rated, ranked, and selected on a competitive basis.[5](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn5 "For more information on DHS preparedness grants, funding levels and mechanisms, and eligibility, see CRS Report R48828, State and Local Homeland Security: DHS Preparedness Grants , by Shawn Reese .")

FEMA's [*Preparedness Grants Manual*](https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/manual) provides interested parties with information on "policies and procedures for managing preparedness grants; guidance specific to each grant; [and] rules and regulations that guide the proper management of FEMA grants."[6](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn6 "FEMA , Preparedness Grants Manual , July 2026, p. 6, https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema-gov_rsl_fy26_preparedness-grants-manual.pdf .")

Below are high-level descriptions of various FEMA preparedness grants that could potentially be used to fund emergency communications projects. The cited sources contain additional details on the purposes and availability of the grants.

Homeland Security Grant Program (HSGP)

The HSGP includes three individual grant programs aimed at helping states, local governments, tribes, and territories in "preventing, protecting against, mitigating, responding to and recovering from acts of terrorism and other threats."[7](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn7 "FEMA, \"Homeland Security Grant Program,\" September 23 , 2026, https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/homeland-security .") These programs fund "a range of preparedness activities, including planning, organization, equipment purchase, training, exercises, and management and administration across all core capabilities and mission areas."[8](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn8 "FEMA, \"Homeland Security Grant Program . \"")

* **Program website****:** <https://www.fema.gov/grants/preparedness/homeland-security>
* **Assistance** **L****isting** **N****umber****:** 97.067
* **Eligible applicants****:** state administrative agencies (SAAs)[9](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn9 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Homeland Security Grant Program , Notice of Funding Opportunity, p. 5 , https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_gpd_hsgp-nofo-fy2026_0.pdf .")

There are three grant programs under HSGP:

* 1. **State Homeland Security Program****.**These grants "support the implementation of risk-driven, capabilities-based State Homeland Security Strategies to address capability targets."[10](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn10 "FEMA, \"Homeland Security Grant Program.\"") Funding is provided to states, which are required to pass through at least 80% of the funds to tribal or local entities.[11](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn11 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Homeland Security Grant Program , p. 47 .") **Allowable expenses** include personnel training; exercises and simulations; and procuring technology or equipment to support surveillance, communications, and data analysis.[12](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn12 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Homeland Security Grant Program , p. 14 .")
* 2. **Urban Area Security Initiative****.** This program "provides funding to enhance regional preparedness and capabilities in designated high-threat, high-density areas."[13](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn13 "FEMA, \"Homeland Security Grant Program.\"") Funding is provided to states, which are required to pass through at least 80% of the funds to tribal or local entities.[14](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn14 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Homeland Security Grant Program , p. 47.") **Allowable expenses** include personnel training; exercises and simulations; and procuring technology or equipment to support surveillance, communications, and data analysis.[15](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn15 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Homeland Security Grant Program , p. 14.")
* 3. **Operation Stonegarden****.** This program "provides funding to enhance cooperation and coordination among state, local, tribal, territorial, and federal law enforcement agencies to jointly enhance security along the United States land and water borders."[16](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn16 "FEMA, \"Homeland Security Grant Program.\"") States submit applications on behalf of eligible subrecipients ("county-level or equivalent governments and federally recognized tribal governments in states bordering Canada or Mexico, or those with international water borders").[17](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn17 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Homeland Security Grant Program , p . 5.") **Allowable expenses** include interoperable data systems, such as computer-aided dispatch systems and records management systems.[18](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn18 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Homeland Security Grant Program , p. 91.")

One of the objectives is to "integrate effective, reliable, available, and timely alert and warning capabilities to enhance public emergency communications."[35](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn35 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Next Generation Warning System Grant Program , Notice of Funding Opportunity, p. 7, https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_gpd_ngws-nofo_fy2026.pdf .")

* **Program website****:** <https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/practitioners/integrated-public-alert-warning-system/ngwsp>
* **Assistance Listing Number****:** 97.138
* **Eligible applicants****:** states or Indian tribes (tribal nations) as defined in [2 C.F.R. §200.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR2a6a0087862fd2c/section-200.1)[36](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn36 "DHS, Fiscal Year 2026 Next Generation Warning System Grant Program , p. 4 .")
* **Allowable** **expenses****:** "installation of IPAWS-compatible sirens and/or 'giant-voice systems'"[37](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49514.html#fn37 "FEMA, \"The Next Generation Warning System (NGWS) Grant Program: Frequently Asked Questions , \" p. 3 , https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_gpd_ipaws-ngws-faqs_fy2026.pdf .")

For additional information on IPAWS—the national alerting system that state, local, and tribal entities can use to distribute alerts—see CRS Report R48363, [*The Integrated Public Alert and Warning System (IPAWS): Primer and Issues for Congress*](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/R48363), by Brian E. Humphreys.
