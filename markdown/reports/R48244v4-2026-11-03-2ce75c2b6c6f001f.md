The Department of Transportation’s (DOT’s) Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration           Analyst in Transportation
(FMCSA) develops and issues standards for states’ testing and licensing of commercial motor         Policy
vehicle (CMV) drivers. FMCSA has the authority to establish pilot programs to test changes to
commercial driver licensing requirements. FMCSA established the Safe Driver Apprenticeship
Pilot Program (SDAP) on January 14, 2022, to meet requirements in Section 23022 of the
Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA; P.L. 117-58). Section 23022 required the Secretary
of Transportation to submit a report to Congress in March 2026 to provide a recommendation, based on data collected, on
whether the level of safety achieved by the pilot program for CMV drivers under 21 years of age was similar to or greater
than the level of safety for CMV drivers aged 21 years or older, as determined by FMCSA. SDAP was the second
congressionally directed pilot program for truck drivers under 21 years of age. Since 2014, there has been federal support for
programs to train under-21-year-old CMV drivers due to concerns of an aging CMV workforce and employee turnover. As of
2025, workers aged 55 years or older comprised 30% of the U.S. truck transportation workforce.

FMCSA began receiving applications for SDAP first from motor carriers in July 2022, then from apprentices and
experienced drivers in November 2022, and concluded the program in November 2025. Although not statutorily required,
FMCSA made quarterly program enrollment data publicly available beginning in April 2023. Between November 2022 and
June 2025, the program received a total of 80 applications from apprentices who were commercial driver’s license (CDL)
holders between 18 and 20 years of age. FMCSA approved 95% of applicants to participate in the program. Of the applicants
approved, 55% completed the two required probationary periods. The program had 76 apprentice participants making up
2.5% of the 3,000 total allowed apprentice participants per Section 23022 of the IIJA. The program enrolled 62 motor carriers
(29% of 211 total applications received), making up 6.2% of the 1,000 total allowed motor carriers expected by DOT. Motor
carriers not enrolled either did not meet DOT’s qualifications (42% of total applicants) or voluntarily withdrew their
application (26% of total applicants). In 2023, DOT required interested motor carriers to meet DOT’s qualifications and
register their apprenticeship program with the Department of Labor (DOL). In 2024, Congress modified SDAP through
provisions in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2024 (P.L. 118-42). As a result of the changes, DOT could no longer
require motor carriers to register their apprenticeship programs with DOL or require them to use inward- or rear-facing
cameras to collect safety data.

Following SDAP’s conclusion, Congress may consider various policy options regarding the program. Congress could
establish a permanent apprenticeship program, initiate a new pilot program, or discontinue SDAP. If Congress is satisfied
with the results of SDAP, Congress could establish a permanent apprenticeship program, taking into consideration alignment
between DOT and DOL to improve the administrative efficiency of apprenticeships. Alternatively, Congress could direct
DOT to initiate a new pilot program based on the findings from related studies and reports. If Congress decides not to
authorize a new pilot program, under 21-aged CMV drivers would not be allowed to participate in interstate commerce
pursuant to existing regulatory standards. Section 5401 of the Building Unrivaled Infrastructure and Long-term Development
for America’s 250th Act (Build America 250 Act; H.R. 8870), a surface transportation reauthorization bill introduced and
ordered reported in the House of Representatives in May 2026, would extend the apprenticeship pilot program. Some of the
issues identified in this report may be addressed in congressional debates concerning the Build America 250 Act (H.R. 8870),
through other legislation, or as part of committee oversight.

Introduction

Congress has long been concerned about the overall adequacy of the U.S. trucking workforce and
the recruitment and retention of experienced drivers to maintain that workforce in the face of
aging and employee turnover. According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, in 2025, workers aged
55 or older represented 30% of the U.S. truck transportation workforce.1 To attract younger
drivers to the trucking workforce, Section 23022 of the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act
(IIJA; P.L. 117-58), enacted in 2021, required the Secretary of Transportation to establish a three-
year apprenticeship pilot program for commercial drivers. The program was intended to allow
apprentices—commercial driver’s license (CDL) holders between 18 and 20 years of age—to
operate a commercial motor vehicle (CMV) in interstate commerce under the supervision of an
experienced driver (see “Experienced Driver Applicants”). The Department of Transportation’s
(DOT’s) Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) established the Safe Driver
Apprenticeship Pilot Program (SDAP) on January 14, 2022, to meet the requirements of the
IIJA.2 The program concluded on November 7, 2025.3 SDAP was the second congressionally
directed pilot program for truck drivers under the age of 21.4

This report provides a brief overview of SDAP. The report first explains FMCSA’s regulatory and
administrative role, then presents SDAP enrollment data reported in 2023, 2024, and 2025.5 It
also identifies policy options for congressional consideration. Congress might consider these or
related issues during debates on surface transportation reauthorization, through other legislation,
or as part of committee oversight. Congress included provisions to extend SDAP in the Build
America 250 Act (H.R. 8870), a surface transportation reauthorization bill ordered reported in the
House of Representatives in May 2026.

the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) promulgated regulations for highway safety. In

FHWA oversight of the CDL licensing process came later. In the Commercial Motor Vehicle
Safety Act of 1986 (Title XII of P.L. 99-570), Congress required DOT to issue regulations
establishing minimum uniform standards for states’ issuance of CDLs and commercial learner’s
permits (CLPs). States are not allowed to issue an unrestricted CDL to anyone who does not meet
the driver qualifications stated in Title 49, Section 391.11, of the Code of Federal Regulations.
Additionally, the act authorized the Secretary of Transportation to grant waivers and exemptions
from the FMCSRs and to conduct pilot programs “to evaluate alternatives to regulations relating
to, or innovative approaches to, motor carrier, commercial motor vehicle, and driver safety.”6

The Under 21 Military CDL Pilot Program, the first congressionally directed pilot program for
younger CMV drivers, was enacted in 2015 in Section 5404 of the Fixing America’s Surface
Transportation Act (FAST Act; P.L. 114-94). The FAST Act directed DOT to develop a pilot
program to allow a limited number of individuals between 18 and 20 years of age to operate
CMVs in interstate commerce if they received heavy-vehicle driver training while serving in the
military. From 2019 to 2022, FMCSA carried out this FAST Act pilot program, which also
allowed individuals receiving military-based heavy vehicle driver training to be hired by a
participating motor carrier for the pilot program.7

Safe Driver Apprenticeship Pilot Program
SDAP is the second congressionally directed pilot program for CMV drivers under the age of 21.8
In contrast to the first pilot program, authorized by the FAST Act, drivers participating in SDAP
must hold only an intrastate CDL to be eligible for the program. Normally, an individual between
18 and 20 years of age is issued a CDL with a “K” restriction for intrastate-only use in
accordance with Title 49, Section 383.153(a)(10)(vii), of the Code of Federal Regulations.
Drivers under 21 years of age cannot operate a CMV in interstate commerce without an
exemption from the K license restriction. Under Section 23022 of the IIJA, Congress directs
FMCSA to provide exemptions from the K license restriction through a formal pilot program
process for apprentice participants aged 18-20 years. SDAP met this requirement.

Following the conclusion of SDAP on November 7, 2025, some interest groups have discussed
the potential continuation of SDAP.9 On November 14, 2026, the American Trucking
Associations (ATA) submitted a petition to FMCSA to extend SDAP for five years for previous
apprenticeship participants. FMCSA requested comments on ATA’s petition between January 28,
2026, and February 27, 2026. Several organizations, including the Commercial Vehicle Training
Association, the Truckload Carriers Association, and the Next Generation in Trucking
Association, expressed support for the petition as a trucking workforce development measure.

Program Requirements for Motor Carriers

For motor carriers, SDAP required the completion of four tasks as part of the application process:
(1) submit an application demonstrating achievement of eight safety qualifications; (2) commit to
submitting monthly data on apprentices as required by Section 23022 of the IIJA; (3) register
their apprenticeship program with the Department of Labor (DOL); and (4) provide their
program’s DOL-registered apprenticeship number to DOT.10 (See “Motor Carrier Applicants.”)

On March 14, 2024, Congress enacted provisions in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2024
(P.L. 118-42), changing the 2023 SDAP program requirements enforced by DOT. Section 422 of
the act states that “none of the funds made available by this or any other Act may be used to
require the use of inward facing cameras or require a motor carrier to register an apprenticeship
program with the Department of Labor as a condition for participation in the Safe Driver
Apprenticeship Pilot Program.” On May 14, 2024, FMCSA published a notice of revision to the
pilot program in the Federal Register that revised motor carrier participation requirements to
reflect these provisions.11 Although motor carriers were no longer required to register their
apprenticeship program with DOL, they were still required to submit an application addressing
the safety qualification criteria and commit to monthly data reporting.12

Program Enrollment Reported in 2023-2025
FMCSA made quarterly reports on SDAP publicly available beginning in April 2023.13 FMCSA
began collecting motor carrier applications on July 26, 2022, and experienced driver and
apprentice applications in November 2022. Applications collected by FMCSA in 2022 appear to
have been reported in first quarter of 2023 data. FMCSA presented quarterly data in four
categories: Motor Carriers, Experienced Drivers, Apprentice Drivers, and Apprentice Driver
Progress.

12 apprentice drivers). By the conclusion of the program, enrollment numbers totaled 2.5% (76
apprentices) of the maximum number of apprentice drivers allowed under the law (3,000) and

Experienced Driver Applicants

SDAP defined an experienced driver as an individual older than 26 years of age with a minimum
of five years of experience driving a CMV in interstate commerce.17 Experienced drivers were
required to apply through an approved motor carrier to participate.18 An apprentice driver was
required to be accompanied by an experienced driver sitting in the passenger seat of the CMV
during the apprentice’s probationary periods.19 Experienced drivers were able to serve for a
maximum of two years. Throughout the entirety of the service period, experienced drivers needed
to hold a valid CDL, have no preventable accidents reported to DOT, and have no pointed moving
violations.20

Table 2 provides data on experienced driver applications for SDAP. Of the 153 applications
reported between 2023 and 2025, 141 were approved (92%) and one was disapproved (<1%).
Eleven experienced drivers were disenrolled from the program before their service period ended:
eight voluntarily withdrew (5%), two were removed from the carrier (1%), and one was removed
for safety reasons (<1%).21

Apprentice Driver Applicants and Program Progress
Apprentice drivers under SDAP were individuals aged 18-20 years who held a CDL.22 Apprentice
drivers were required to complete two probationary periods: a 120-hour period, which included at
least 80 hours of driving time, followed by a 280-hour period, which included at least 160 hours
of driving time. Both probationary periods had performance benchmarks against which an
apprentice’s competency was measured.23 Interested eligible drivers applied directly to an
approved motor carrier for an SDAP apprenticeship position and awaited approval by FMCSA for
acceptance to SDAP.24

FMCSA received a total of 80 apprentice driver applications from the end of 2022 through the
middle of 2025. FMCSA approved 76 applications (or 95% of all applications received). Table 3
provides data reported by FMCSA on apprentice driver applications and determinations.

Completion of Probationary Periods

Report to Congress

At the conclusion of the pilot program, the Secretary of Transportation is statutorily required in
Section 23022(g) of the IIJA to submit a report within 120 days, addressing six items:

    1.  the findings and conclusions of the pilot program;
    2.  an analysis of the safety record of apprentice drivers who participated in the
       program;
    3.  the number of drivers who discontinued participation in the apprenticeship
       program before completion;
    4.  a comparison of the safety records of participating drivers before, during, and
         after the probationary periods;
    5.  a comparison, for each participating driver, of average on-duty time, driving
        time, and time spent away from their home terminal before, during, and after the
        probationary periods; and
    6.  a recommendation, based on the data collected, on whether the level of safety
       achieved by the pilot program was equivalent to or greater than the level of safety
        for equivalent CMV drivers aged 21 years or older, as determined by the
        agency.27

Policy Options for Congress

This report discusses three possible policy options if Congress were to consider the status of
SDAP; additional options may exist. Congress could establish a permanent apprenticeship
program by statute, initiate a new pilot program, or maintain the status quo with SDAP
discontinued.

Establish a Permanent Safe Driver Apprenticeship Program

If Congress is satisfied with the results of SDAP, it could create a similar permanent
apprenticeship program. This could involve legislation directing DOT to promulgate regulations
to provide exemptions from K license restrictions to 18-20-year-old CDL holders who participate
and complete an approved apprenticeship program. Program requirements could be based on
those in Section 23022 of the IIJA and the SDAP program requirements. In considering such a
program, Congress might be interested in the federal administration required to carry it out. Were
such a permanent program established, Congress could direct DOT to solely regulate the
apprenticeship program approval process. However, because DOL regulates registered
apprenticeship programs through Title 29, Section 29, of the Code of Federal Regulations, there
might be potential for duplication of federal efforts between DOT and DOL. Alternatively,
Congress could choose to require that motor carriers receive joint approval from DOT and DOL
to carry out a registered apprenticeship program. In the 119th Congress, the DRIVE-SAFE Act
(H.R. 5563) proposes to establish an apprenticeship program that would retain provisions from
Section 23022 of the IIJA.

Initiate a New Pilot Program

Congress could pursue a new or separate apprenticeship pilot program for CLP holders between
the ages of 18 and 20. Organizations have expressed interest in offering apprenticeship
opportunities for pursuers of CDLs in addition to early career CDL holders. On February 23,
2023, FMCSA announced that Pitt Ohio Express, LLC, filed an application for an exemption
from the CDL requirements for SDAP participants under 21 years of age stating that the
exemption would increase SDAP apprentice driver participation.30 On May 9, 2024, FMCSA

Discontinue the Pilot Program

Congress could choose not to authorize a new apprenticeship program under DOT following
SDAP’s conclusion. If Congress chooses not to authorize a new apprenticeship program, the
statutes and regulations that predate SDAP would remain, and states could continue issuing CDLs
with K restrictions to individuals who are under 21 years of age. CDL holders with a K restriction
because of age would follow their state’s process to remove the restriction upon reaching 21 years
of age if the driver pursues interstate commerce.

If Congress wishes to maintain the status quo, registered apprenticeship programs may exist as a
legacy of SDAP. In 2023, at least 34 motor carriers had registered programs with DOL in
accordance with Title 29, Section 29, of the Code of Federal Regulations.32 Congress may wish to
ensure that any registered apprenticeship programs established as part of SDAP also are
deactivated by directing DOL to terminate those programs. As an alternative to terminating the
registered apprenticeship programs, Congress may direct DOL, in consultation with DOT, to
verify that such programs can operate in compliance with federal CDL laws after SDAP’s
termination.
