Summary

On September 30, 2026, Senator Capito, on behalf of herself, Senator Heinrich, Senator Lee, and Senator Whitehouse, introduced S. 5653—the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (BAAJA). BAAJA contains seven titles within two divisions, each with some provisions that directly or indirectly relate to energy infrastructure or permitting. The bill would reform certain federal environmental review and permitting processes to potentially expedite infrastructure projects.

The bill is organized according to two divisions.

Division A—Committee on Environment and Public Works—has four titles:

  • National Environmental Policy Act of 1969
  • Clean Water Act
  • Endangered Species Act of 1973
  • Project Certainty

Division B—Committee on Energy and Natural Resources—has three titles:

  • Electric Transmission
  • Energy Permitting
  • Historic Preservation

This report identifies the provisions contained within BAAJA, with abbreviated information about their content and the citations to relevant existing law, where applicable.

Overview

On September 30, 2026, Senator Capito, on behalf of herself, Senator Heinrich, Senator Lee, and Senator Whitehouse, introduced S. 5653—the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (BAAJA). The bill would address numerous aspects of infrastructure permitting—especially for electricity and energy projects—including federal processes for environmental considerations and historic preservation.

The bill is organized according to two divisions.

Division A—Committee on Environment and Public Works—has four titles:

  • National Environmental Policy Act of 1969
  • Clean Water Act
  • Endangered Species Act of 1973
  • Project Certainty

Division B—Committee on Energy and Natural Resources—has three titles:

  • Electric Transmission
  • Energy Permitting
  • Historic Preservation

This report provides a short summary of each provision of BAAJA as introduced; citations to relevant existing law that would be amended by the provisions, where applicable; relevant CRS background reports, where applicable; and CRS experts and points of contact that congressional clients may contact for additional information. For provisions with affected U.S.C. citations, the citations are listed in order of appearance in the bill.

Division A—Committee on Environment and Public Works

Title I—National Environmental Policy Act of 19691

Subtitle A—Declaration of Environmental Policy

Section 1101. Purposes; procedure; savings provision.

Summary of provision. Section 1101 would amend the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (NEPA; 42 U.S.C. §§4321 et seq.) to specify that it prescribe a procedure to inform the public about environmental effects of major federal actions and require the result of the NEPA process to be an informational document used in federal decisionmaking. It would state that NEPA does not mandate a particular outcome in decisionmaking.

Section 1102. Definitions.

Summary of provision. Section 1102 would reorganize NEPA's definitions from Section 111 to a new Section 3 and make them applicable to both of NEPA's titles.2 It would remove two existing definitions, modify several existing definitions, add 15 new definitions, and make conforming amendments. For example, it would expand the list of exclusions in the definition of "major federal action," remove the definition of "participating Federal agencies," and add definitions for terms such as "authorization" and "make public."

Section 1105. Procedure for determination of level of review.

Summary of provision. Section 1105 would amend the process for determining whether an action requires an environmental document (i.e., a threshold determination) and which level of review applies. It would authorize the use of two or more categorical exclusions (CEs) on a proposed agency action—excluding overlapping CEs based on size, acreage, or dollar amount thresholds—and recognize statutory CEs; it would add state, tribal, and non-NEPA federal reviews that are functionally equivalent to NEPA reviews as threshold grounds for not preparing an environmental document; it would allow a sponsor to request an environmental impact statement (EIS) in place of an environmental assessment (EA); and it would bind other agencies to a lead agency's threshold determination.

Section 1106. Timely and unified Federal reviews.

Summary of provision. Section 1106 would revise and expand NEPA Section 107, which currently governs many of the procedural elements of agency environmental reviews. Section 1106 would expand requirements governing roles and responsibilities of lead, joint lead, and cooperating agencies for federal decisions "relating to an authorization." Among other changes, the section would expand lead agency duties in managing a schedule for environmental review and needed authorizations; specify aspects of CEQ's role in resolving disputes associated with participating agencies and deadlines; and add interagency scheduling and coordination requirements. The section would also eliminate consideration of certain alternatives to a proposed action; place limitations on when an agency is required to consider new scientific or technical research; and facilitate the use of state and tribal environmental review documents.

Additionally, Section 1106 would limit the scope and timing of cooperating agency comments and prescribe certain public comment periods for environmental impact statements. The section would establish deadlines for authorization decisions following completion of the NEPA process, and it would provide administrative consequences if an agency misses a deadline for environmental reviews and authorizations. The section would expand judicial review for missed deadlines for NEPA and related authorization decisions.

It would specify annual reporting requirements for agencies and CEQ that include information related to missed deadlines for environmental documents, progress toward adhering to deadlines year over year, and recommendations on how to improve agency adherence to deadlines.

Affected U.S.C. citation. The provision would amend the following section of the U.S. Code:

Section 1110. Judicial review.

Summary of provision. Section 1110 would affect the timing, venue, available remedies, and other considerations associated with judicial review of NEPA and potentially related claims. This would include a statute of limitations to file a claim of NEPA noncompliance 150 days after an EIS is published in the Federal Register or an authorization is "made public"; limits on who can file a claim; requirements that claims be filed in a federal court of appeals; and a general prohibition on any judicial remedy awarding vacatur or injunctive relief unless certain criteria are met beyond finding of a NEPA violation.

Affected U.S.C. citation. The provision would make the following change to the U.S. Code:

Subtitle B—Council on Environmental Quality

Section 1121. Council on Environmental Quality.

Summary of provision. Section 1121 would establish a statutory framework for and direct CEQ to lead development of electronic tools for federal environmental reviews (i.e., "eNEPA technologies") and authorizations. It would direct the CEQ chair to, among other things, establish data standards and minimum technological functions, require "key permitting agencies" to begin implementing those functions, establish an interconnected federal data system (i.e., "ePermit unified interagency data system") with a common authorization portal, provide public access to certain information, and require "key permitting agencies" to submit biannual reports on the status of meeting the implementation requirements. It would also direct the CEQ chair to develop performance measures for agencies and personnel, authorize contracts supporting implementation, and require annual reports to certain congressional committees.3 It would also amend NEPA to increase the authorization of appropriations from $1 million to $5 million annually.

Affected U.S.C. citations. The provision would amend the following sections of the U.S. Code:

Section 1132. Environmental Review and Permitting Tracker.

Summary of provision. Section 1132 would require CEQ, in coordination with the General Services Administration (GSA), to maintain an online database tracking environmental reviews and authorizations for transparency projects. A "transparency project" would be defined in Section 1131 as any proposed agency action requiring an EIS or any proposed agency action requiring an EA that has not been completed by 13 months after the deadline established in Section 107(g) of NEPA, as amended by BAAJA. Entries on the tracker would identify information such as project location, major features, purpose and need, agency contact information, schedule and compliance status, and description of any litigation directly related to the project.

Section 1204. Permits for dredged or fill material.

Summary of provision. Section 1204 would limit the period of time during which EPA could issue a Section 404(c) "veto" of a Section 404 permit for the discharge of dredged or fill material into waters of the United States. It would extend the permit term limit for Section 404 general permits from 5 years to 10 years. It would specify that the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) consideration of environmental effects for general permits be limited to "water quality requirements," which would be newly defined in the bill, and that any adverse effects of discharges of dredge or fill material into less than two acres of waters of the United States would be considered minimal. It would require USACE to maintain certain nationwide permits and would raise the threshold of allowable impacts for coverage under these permits.

Title III—Endangered Species Act of 19735

Section 1301. Definitions.

Summary of provision. Section 1301 would define the terms "best scientific and commercial data available" and "best scientific data available" under the Endangered Species Act (ESA; 16 U.S.C. §§1531 et seq.).

These terms would

(A) mean all relevant and objective scientific and commercial information available at the time of the agency action; and

(B) include credible and reliable data, quantitative analyses, conceptual and numerical models, and model results that—

(i) account for known or potential sources of error;

(ii) are applied using prevailing principles, methods, tools, and professional standards of practice; and

(iii) are impartially gathered and objectively applied without presumptions or policy prescriptions that bias the application.

Section 1301 would replace "the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands" with "the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands" in the definition of "state."

Affected U.S.C. citation. The provision would amend the following section of the U.S. Code:

Section 1302. Timeline for Section 7 consultations.

Summary of provision. Section 1302 would change the deadline for formal consultation under Section 7—which requires federal agencies to consult with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) and National Marine Fisheries Service as to the effects of certain federal actions on listed species and critical habitat—from 90 days to 60 days. It would shorten the time frame for extensions of the consultation period that require only a written explanation to a relevant applicant from 150 days to 100 days. It would also shorten the time frame after which the Secretary of the Interior or Commerce would be required to obtain a relevant applicant's consent for extensions of the consultation period from 150 days or more to 100 days or more.

Section 1302 would authorize a permit or license applicant to sue if the Secretary of the Interior or Commerce and the consulting federal agency propose to extend the consultation period beyond 145 days and do not provide a written statement to or obtain consent from the applicant, as applicable.

Section 1302 would require that Section 7 consultation be finished no later than the date of completion of any NEPA documentation required for the federal action, unless the Secretary of the Interior or Commerce approves an extension due to "extraordinary circumstances beyond the control, and not due to any action, of the consulting parties."

Section 1304. State assumption of consultation responsibilities.

Summary of provision. Section 1304 would direct the Secretary of the Interior to establish a program that would allow the Secretary to assign the consultation responsibilities of the Secretary to states, at the state's election, under certain conditions. (This program would apply only to consultations conducted by FWS.)

Section 1304 would not allow for the assignment of consultation responsibilities for projects or other agency actions that exceed the boundaries of a state. If the project or agency action would occur within one state but affect an area outside that state's boundaries, the bill would require the state to consult with the other affected states, if they have assumed consultation responsibilities, or with the Secretary, if the states have not assumed such responsibilities.

Section 1304 would require the Secretary to issue proposed regulations to establish the program and ensure states adequately carry out assumed consultation responsibilities within 270 days of the enactment of BAAJA and a public comment period of not less than 60 days.

Section 1304 would require a participating state to verify, before assuming Section 7 consulting responsibilities, that it (1) had the financial, personnel, and legal resources to carry out Section 7 consultations; (2) waived sovereign immunity for the responsibilities assumed by the state; and (3) provided for public comment on the state application for consultation consistent with the bill's public notice and comment requirements. Section 1304 would require the Secretary to (1) respond to the state in writing if an application to assume consultation responsibilities is denied, identifying the reasons for denial; and (2) provide technical assistance to the state in addressing those reasons, if requested. It would direct the Secretary to approve a state application for consultation responsibilities within 180 days if the Secretary determines that the state demonstrated legal, financial, and personnel capability to conduct consultations; otherwise met the requirements in statute and regulation for the program; and entered into a written agreement with the Secretary that met certain requirements and was executed by the governor or head of the responsible state agency.

Section 1304 would give the district courts of the United States exclusive jurisdiction over any civil action against a state for failure to carry out any responsibility of the state under the program and would direct that such actions be governed by "the legal standards and requirements" that would apply to an action taken by the Secretary.

Section 1304 would authorize a permit or license applicant to elect to consult with the Secretary regardless of state assumption of consultation responsibility.

Section 1304 would direct the Secretary to conduct audits and monitoring of state implementation of the consultation responsibility. It would also require the Secretary to submit an annual report to certain congressional committees.6

Section 1304 would provide for the termination of state assumption of consultation responsibilities by either the Secretary or the state, subject to notice requirements and other criteria.

Affected U.S.C. citation. The provision would make the following change to the U.S. Code:

  • Would add new Section 7A to ESA (16 U.S.C. §§1531 et seq.)

Title IV—Project Certainty7

Section 1401. Maintaining Federal authorizations or permits for projects with non-Federal sponsors.

Summary of provision. Absent certain exceptions, Section 1401 would prohibit a federal agency from revoking, rescinding, withdrawing, terminating, suspending, amending, or altering a federal authorization or permit in effect on or after September 16, 2026, that is necessary for project construction or "operation at full capacity," or from taking "other action to interfere with or prevent construction or operation at full capacity" of a project that has secured all necessary federal authorizations or permits. Exceptions would include actions directed by court order; violation by the authorization or permit holder of federal law or material breach of permit conditions; fraud in obtaining the authorization or permit; request by the permittee, holder of the federal authorization, or nonfederal project sponsor; and certain measures to protect life, property, national security, or defense. Section 1401 would provide for judicial review of alleged rescission, suspension, or modification of a federal authorization or interference with a fully permitted facility, with remedies to the permit holder, including injunctive relief and a portion of project costs (to be paid from the Judgment Fund).

Affected U.S.C. citations. N/A.

Section 1402. Ensuring fairness for specific types of covered projects.

Summary of provision. Section 1402 would provide a cause of action and remedies for developers of "covered projects" for which federal authorizations or permits were "improperly denied" or "unreasonably delayed" due to "a pattern of disparate treatment," meaning a "substantial increase" in rates of unreasonable delay or improper denial, compared to the preceding five years, that result from "an intentional course of action by the Federal Government to create such a pattern." It would entitle prevailing plaintiffs to injunctive relief, attorneys' fees, economic damages, and additional compensation for costs of construction up to full project costs (to be paid from the Judgment Fund). It would specify what types of projects are "covered" based on listed sectors, resources, and technologies.

Section 1402(c) would establish a one-year deadline for agencies to issue decisions on authorizations for covered projects that do not require an EA or EIS.

Summary of provision. Title III—Historic Preservation14

Section 2301. Historic preservation.

Summary of provision. Section 2301 would define "property of traditional religious or cultural importance" and specify when such properties are to be considered for the purposes of inclusion on the National Register of Historic Places and reviews under Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA; 54 U.S.C. §§300101 et seq.)

Section 2301 would require the Secretary of the Interior to conduct more frequent reviews of state historic preservation programs and tribal historic preservation programs for compliance with NHPA requirements and to establish certain penalties for noncompliance.

Section 2301 would establish additional statutory directives to federal agencies for how consideration of historic properties should occur under Section 106 of the NHPA. These would include narrowing the types of actions or projects subject to review; defining the types of effects to historic properties that may be considered "adverse"; creating deadlines for completion of Section 106 obligations; limiting the instances of judicial review; and limiting when and how consultation with state historic preservation officers and tribal historic preservation officers is required during the review process.

Affected U.S.C. citations. The provision would amend the following sections of the U.S. Code:

  • 54 U.S.C. §302706 (for eligibility of properties of religious or cultural importance to Tribes and Native Hawaiian Organizations)
  • 54 U.S.C. §302302 (for reviews of state historic preservation officers)
  • Would add new Section 302707 to the end of 54 U.S.C. Ch. 3027 (for reviews of tribal historic preservation officers)
  • 54 U.S.C. §306108 (for Section 106 review amendments)
  • 54 U.S.C. §306114 (conforming amendment)

Section 2302. Funding for the Historic Preservation Fund; use and availability; grants for Tribal Historic Preservation officers; additional funding.

Summary of provision. Section 2302 would authorize to be appropriated for deposit to the Historic Preservation Fund (HPF) $200 million annually for FY2027 through FY2036 and would make any deposits available "without further appropriation or fiscal year limitation." The provision would replace existing authorizing language for HPF, which has historically been authorized to receive mandatory deposits of $150 million annually from OCS energy revenues, which are available for expenditure only when appropriated by Congress.

Section 2302 would direct the Secretary of the Interior to establish a competitive grant program for tribal historic preservation officers to develop online databases of their agreements, determinations, findings, and supporting documentation, and to develop a comprehensive list of properties of religious or cultural importance to the tribe. It would authorize appropriations of $20 million annually for the program for FY2027 through FY2030.
