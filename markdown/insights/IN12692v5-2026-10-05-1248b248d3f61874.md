U.S. Aircraft Combat Losses in Operation Epic Fury: Considerations for Congress

Updated October 5, 2026
(IN12692)

## Overview

On February 28, 2026, the United States, in coordination with Israel, initiated military operations against Iran initially under the designation Operation Epic Fury (OEF). The conflict has involved air, maritime, and missile combat engagements across the Middle East. The pace of combat activity declined amid a [ceasefire](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/04/1167264) in April. [Within weeks, some strikes](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/05/08/us-iran-ceasefire-hormuz-attacks/) resumed, and conditions remain fluid.

The Department of Defense (DOD, which is "using a secondary Department of War designation," under [Executive Order](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/10/2025-17508/restoring-the-united-states-department-of-war) 14347 dated September 5, 2025) on September 14, 2026, [published](https://www.dodig.mil/reports.html/Article/4600623/operation-epic-fury-oef/) a *Lead Inspector General* *(Lead IG)* *Report to* *the United States* *Congress on Operation Epic Fury (OEF)*, which assessed [certain equipment losses](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Sep/09/2003993626/-1/-1/1/OEF_Q3_JUN2026_FINAL_508%20SECURE.PDF#page=20) relating to operations against Iran between April 1 and June 30. Some analysts and DOD officials have published varying estimates of the cost to replace lost equipment, including aircraft. In September 2026, the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) [estimated](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2026-09/62756-Iran.pdf#page=18) the cost of equipment losses as of August 1 at between $1.9 billion and $3.3 billion, depending on DOD plans for replacements.

Listed below are 81 fixed-wing or rotary-wing aircraft, including uncrewed aircraft systems (UAS, or drones), reportedly lost or damaged in U.S. operations against Iran since February 28, according to news reports, statements by DOD and U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM), and the DOD Inspector General (DODIG, which was [designated](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Sep/09/2003993626/-1/-1/1/OEF_Q3_JUN2026_FINAL_508%20SECURE.PDF#page=3) the Lead IG for OEF). The number of aircraft may remain subject to revision due to multiple factors, which may include classification, ongoing combat activity, and attribution. Some damaged aircraft may have been [returned](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/wilsbach-kc-135s-damaged-epic-fury-return-to-service/) to active service.

## Potential Issues for Congress

These reported incidents may raise several considerations for congressional oversight:

* **Information** **a****vailable to Congress****.**Congress may assess whether or not it has sufficient information and time to evaluate the potential effects of aircraft damage and losses in U.S. military operations and potential DOD plans or programs to acquire replacements.
* **Budgetary** **i****mpacts****.** Members of Congress may consider whether or not to approve, reject, or modify requests for funding relating to the replacement or repair of aircraft lost or damaged in OEF.
* **Force** **s****ufficiency****.** Congress may assess whether losses in certain high-demand platforms that are aging and limited in number, such as the E-3 Sentry, create capability gaps or increase risk in other theaters.
* **Industrial** **b****ase** **c****apacity****.** Congress may assess whether current production lines and supply chains can replace lost aircraft within time frames needed to meet DOD operational requirements. Congress may seek information about the extent to which competing demands—including foreign military sales or production constraints—may affect DOD's ability to regenerate capacity.
* **Operational** **r****isk****.** Congress may assess whether reported losses reflect changes in the threat environment or in adversary capabilities. Congress may also assess whether any changes to the threat might signal the need to [adjust](https://www.afa.org/agenda/wilsbach-keynote/#:~:text=For%20decades%2C%20American,point%20defense%20Airmen.) U.S. equipment; operational concepts; tactics, techniques, and procedures; or basing posture.
