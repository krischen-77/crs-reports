Confirmation of U.S. Circuit and District
Court Nominations During Lame Duck
Sessions of Congress (1946-2024)


October 26, 2026

This Insight provides information and analysis related to U.S. circuit and district court nominations
confirmed during lame duck sessions held by the Senate during the postwar period (i.e., since 1946). For
the purpose of this Insight, a lame duck session is defined as any portion of a Congress’s regular session
that occurs after the general election in November but before the end of its constitutional term on January
3 of the next calendar year.

The primary purpose of lame duck sessions during this period was to complete congressional action on
legislation, including program authorizations and appropriations legislation (see CRS Report R45154 for
additional information about lame duck sessions of Congress).

Lame duck sessions held by the Senate have also been used to consider a President’s judicial nominations.
Of the 21 lame duck sessions held by the Senate during the postwar period, at least one U.S. circuit or
district court nomination was confirmed in 16 (76%) of the sessions. These 16 lame duck sessions are
reported in Figure 1.

During this same period, the Senate did not confirm any circuit or district court nominations during lame
duck sessions held in 1948 (80th Congress), 1994 (103rd Congress), 2000 (106th Congress), 2008 (110th
Congress), or 2016 (114th Congress). These sessions are not included in Figure 1.

Number Confirmed

As shown by Figure 1, the Senate confirmed a total of 22 circuit court nominations and 163 district court
nominations during 16 lame duck sessions held from 1950 to 2024. The greatest number of circuit court
nominations confirmed during a lame duck session was five, which occurred in 2010 at the end of the
111th Congress. The greatest number of district court nominations confirmed during a lame duck session
was 27, which occurred in 2014 at the end of the 113th Congress.

Type of Preceding Election

During this period, it was more common for circuit and district court nominations to be confirmed during
lame duck sessions that followed midterm elections than presidential elections. Of the 22 circuit court
nominations confirmed, 19 (86%) were confirmed during a lame duck session that followed a midterm
election, while 3 (14%) were confirmed during a session that followed a presidential election. These three

(confirmed to the Seventh Circuit in 2020), and Embry J. Kidd (confirmed to the Eleventh Circuit in

Of the 163 district court nominations confirmed, 114 (70%) were confirmed during a lame duck session
that followed a midterm election, while 49 (30%) were confirmed during a session that followed a
presidential election. Of the latter group of 49 nominees, 46 (94%) were confirmed during lame duck
sessions that followed the 2012, 2020, and 2024 presidential elections.

When Nominations Were Submitted

During this period, it was more common for circuit and district court nominations confirmed during lame
duck sessions to have been formally submitted by a President before an election than after it.

Of the 22 circuit court nominations confirmed, 15 (68%) were formally nominated before an election and
7 (32%) were nominated after the election. For the 15 nominations submitted prior to an election, the
average number of days a nomination was first submitted prior to an election date was 245 days (8.1
months).

Of the 163 district court nominations confirmed, 127 (78%) were formally nominated before an election
and 36 (22%) were nominated after the election. For the 127 nominations submitted prior to an election,
the average number of days a nomination was first submitted prior to an election was 153 days (5.0
months).

When Nominations Were Confirmed

Circuit and district court nominations confirmed during lame duck sessions were confirmed, on average,
36 and 31 days, respectively, after election dates during this period. There have been instances, however,
of shorter lengths of time from an election date to a nomination being confirmed. For example, during the
lame duck session for the 107th Congress, there were 17 district court nominations confirmed by voice
vote on November 14, 2002—nine days following the 2002 election.

Changes in Majority Party Control

The Senate has confirmed circuit and district court nominations during lame duck sessions which
followed elections that led to a change in majority party control of the Senate in the next Congress. In the
period covered by Figure 1, this occurred for the first time in 1954. As of this writing, it most recently
occurred in 2024 during the lame duck session for the 118th Congress, when the Senate confirmed one
circuit court nomination and 21 district court nominations.

Other Types of Judgeships

While not included within the scope of this Insight, the Senate has also confirmed judges to other types of
federal courts during lame duck sessions held since 1946—including the U.S. Court of International
Trade, the U.S. Court of Appeals for Veterans Claims, and the U.S. Court of Federal Claims.

Since the Twentieth Amendment was ratified in 1933, which shortened the period of time from when a
general election occurred to when Members-elect began their constitutional terms in office, there has not
been, as of this writing, a Supreme Court Justice confirmed during a lame duck session of Congress. The
most recent nominee confirmed during a lame duck session prior to the ratification of the Twentieth
Amendment was Harlan Fiske Stone—who was confirmed on February 5, 1925, during the second
session of the 68th Congress, which followed the election on November 4, 1924.
