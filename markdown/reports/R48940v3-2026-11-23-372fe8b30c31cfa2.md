Summary

The United States in 2025 was home to an estimated 50.4 million foreign-born individuals, who lived in every state and congressional district. This report provides a snapshot of recent U.S. Census Bureau estimates for the foreign-born population (i.e., people now living in the United States who were not U.S. citizens at birth) in each state, as well as estimates for the foreign-born population in each congressional district. It also discusses alternative and additional sources of data on the foreign-born population.

---

Introduction

Many people now living in the United States were born in foreign countries. As of 2025, an estimated 50.4 million foreign-born residents[1](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48940.html#fn1 "In this report, the term foreign-born mean s any person now living in the United States who was not a U.S. citizen at birth. This is broader than the definition of immigrant used by the Department of Homeland Security 's (DHS 's ) Office of Homeland Security Statistics (OHSS) : \" Any person lawfully in the United States who is not a U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or person admitted under a nonimmigrant category as defined by the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) section 101(a)(15)...") accounted for roughly 14.7% of the national population, according to the U.S. Census Bureau's [American Community Survey (ACS)](https://www.crs.gov/Reports/IN12303), a large-scale survey of households in the 50 states, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico that produces detailed demographic, economic, housing, and social statistics.[2](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48940.html#fn2 "These statistics come from American Community Survey ( ACS ) , Table DP02 , \" Selected Social Characteristics in the United States , \" https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDP1Y2025.DP02?q=DP02&g=010XX00US&y=2025&d=ACS+1-Year+Estimates+Data+Profiles . For more information about the ACS, see CRS Insight IN12303, The American Community Survey , by Taylor R. Knoedl .")

This report provides a snapshot of recent ACS estimates for the foreign-born population in each state, as well as estimates for the foreign-born population in the 435 congressional districts as configured in the 119th Congress (2025-2026). The foreign-born population in the United States includes naturalized U.S. citizens, lawful permanent residents (i.e., green card holders), temporary-visa holders such as foreign students, and people illegally present in the United States. The Census Bureau does not collect or publish information about whether an immigrant is authorized to be in the United States.[3](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48940.html#fn3 "According to the U.S. Census Bureau , \"The ACS questionnaires do not ask about immigration status. The population surveyed includes all people who indicated that the United States was their usual place of residence on the survey date.\" See Census Bureau, American Community Survey and Puerto Rico Community Survey: 2025 Subject Definitions , p. 66 , https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/tech_docs/subject_definitions/2025_ACSSubjectDefinitions.pdf .")

The Census Bureau provides access to additional ACS data tables related to the foreign-born population via its [data.census.gov](https://data.census.gov) website, including

* [Table B05001](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT1Y2025.B05001?q=B05001), "Nativity and Citizenship Status in the United States," which tabulates the number of U.S. citizens by naturalization and the number of U.S. residents who are not citizens; and
* [Table B05006](https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT1Y2025.B05006?q=B05006), "Place of Birth for the Foreign-Born Population in the United States," which provides a country-level breakdown for place of birth.[4](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48940.html#fn4 "ACS Table B05006 excludes people born at sea and does not break down estimates for all countries .")

The "[Statistics for Unauthorized Immigrants](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48940.html#_Ref225171079)" section below discusses available estimates of the number of people living in the United States without authorization. The "[Additional Sources](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48940.html#_Ref225171209)" section discusses other sources of statistics about the foreign-born population.

Census Bureau

In addition to the ACS estimates discussed above, the [Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/) publishes [data for the foreign-born population](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/foreign-born/data/tables/cps-tables.html) based on the CPS.[17](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48940.html#fn17 "Census Bureau, \" Foreign-Born CPS Data Tables , \" https://www.census.gov/topics/population/foreign-born/data/tables/cps-tables.html .") These tables include national-level demographic and socioeconomic breakdowns as well as headcounts by generation: a *first generation* of people who were born abroad, a *second generation* of U.S.-born individuals with at least one foreign-born parent, and a *third-and-higher generation* of U.S.-born individuals with two U.S.-born parents. As of 2025, CPS-based estimates put the first-generation population at 53.12 million (15.7% of national population), the second-generation population at 44.25 million (13.1%), and the third-and-higher generation population at 240.3 million (71.2%).

The Census Bureau also produces estimates for *net* *international migration*—the total combined movement of people in and out of the United States—as part of its [Population Estimates Program](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest.html). Net international migration is [one component of change in the national population](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/popest/2020s-national-total.html), along with natural change via births and deaths.[18](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48940.html#fn18 "Census Bureau, \" Population and Housing Unit Estimates Tables , \" https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/data/tables.html .")
