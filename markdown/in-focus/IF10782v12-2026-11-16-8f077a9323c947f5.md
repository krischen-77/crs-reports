Arctic National Wildlife Refuge (ANWR) Oil and Gas Program: Establishment and Provisions for Leasing and Development

Updated November 16, 2026
(IF10782)

[Section 20001](https://www.congress.gov/115/statute/STATUTE-131/STATUTE-131-Pg2054.pdf#page=182) of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act ([P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97)), enacted in December 2017, established an oil and gas program in the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge (ANWR or the Refuge). This CRS product discusses the requirements in [P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) and a subsequent law, [P.L. 119-21](http://www.congress.gov/law/119/pub/21), for the program's implementation. Other CRS products contain more detailed analyses of ANWR's energy prospects and biological resources, the legislative history of the Refuge, and recent developments in the oil and gas program:

* CRS In Focus IF12006, [*Arctic National Wildlife Refuge: Status of Oil and Gas Program*](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF12006)
* CRS Report RL33872, [*Arctic National Wildlife Refuge (ANWR): An Overview*](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/RL33872)

ANWR consists of 19 million acres in northeast Alaska, administered primarily by the Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) in the Department of the Interior ([**Figure 1**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/HTML/IF10782.html#_Ref294010756)). The Refuge's 1.57-million-acre [Coastal Plain](https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/oil-and-gas/about/alaska/coastal-plain-arctic-national-wildlife-refuge) is viewed as an onshore oil prospect, with a [mean estimate by the U.S. Geological Survey](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/fs-0028-01/fs-0028-01.htm) of 7.7 billion barrels of technically recoverable oil on federal lands (10.4 billion barrels including Alaska Native lands and adjacent waters). It also is a center of activity for [caribou and other wildlife](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20181003), with Native subsistence uses and critical habitat for polar bears under the [Endangered Species Act](https://www.crs.gov/Reports/R46677) (16 U.S.C. §§1531-1544). The enactment of [P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) culminated a decades-long congressional debate over whether to authorize development of the Coastal Plain's mineral resources or to continue prohibiting development to protect biological and subsistence values.

[P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) required two oil and gas lease sales in the Refuge's Coastal Plain, and a subsequent law, [P.L. 119-21](http://www.congress.gov/law/119/pub/21), mandated four additional sales. The [most recent lease sale](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF12006) occurred in June 2026. Pursuant to these sales, the Department of the Interior (DOI) has [issued 11 active oil and gas leases](https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/docs/2026-06/2026_CoastalPlain_LeaseSale_Tract_Results_Map.pdf) in the Coastal Plain. No oil and gas production has occurred in the Coastal Plain to date. Members of Congress continue to provide oversight of DOI's leasing activities and to consider legislative measures to shape the program.

Establishment of Oil and Gas Program

The ANWR provisions in [P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) responded to a reconciliation instruction to the Senate Committee on Energy and Natural Resources in the FY2018 concurrent resolution on the budget ([H.Con.Res. 71](https://www.congress.gov/bill/115/hconres/71)). The committee was [instructed](https://www.congress.gov/115/bills/hconres71/BILLS-115hconres71enr.pdf#page=18) to report changes in laws within its jurisdiction to reduce the deficit by at least $1 billion for the FY2018-FY2027 period. The Congressional Budget Office [estimated](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/115th-congress-2017-2018/costestimate/53415-hr1conferenceagreement.pdf) the ANWR provisions, along with other oil and gas provisions in Title II, would increase net offsetting receipts by about $1.1 billion during this period (although actual lease sales did not yield the anticipated amounts).

[P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) directed the Secretary of the Interior, acting through DOI's Bureau of Land Management (BLM), to establish and administer a competitive program for leasing, development, production, and transportation of oil and gas in and from ANWR's Coastal Plain. Although the Refuge as a whole is administered by FWS, under the Mineral Leasing Act of 1920 (MLA; 30 U.S.C. §§181 et seq.), BLM manages onshore federal energy and mineral resources, not only on its own lands but also on those of other federal agencies. The law further provided that Section 1003 of the Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act of 1980 (ANILCA; [P.L. 96-487](http://www.congress.gov/law/96/pub/487)), which prohibited oil and gas development in ANWR unless authorized by Congress, shall not apply to the Refuge's Coastal Plain.

Refuge Purposes

ANILCA stated [four purposes](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-94/pdf/STATUTE-94-Pg2371.pdf#page=20) for which ANWR was to be managed, including (1) conserving fish and wildlife populations and habitats, (2) fulfilling U.S. international fish and wildlife treaty obligations, (3) providing for continued subsistence use by local residents, and (4) ensuring water quality and quantity within the Refuge. [P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) amended ANILCA to add, as a [fifth purpose](https://www.congress.gov/115/statute/STATUTE-131/STATUTE-131-Pg2054.pdf#page=183) of the Refuge, "to provide for an oil and gas program on the Coastal Plain." Under the National Wildlife Refuge System Administration Act of 1966, as amended (16 U.S.C. §668dd), and under ANILCA [Section 304](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-94/pdf/STATUTE-94-Pg2371.pdf#page=23) and regulations at [43 C.F.R. Section 3100.3(j)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-43/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-3100/subpart-3100/section-3100.3), FWS undertakes [compatibility determinations](https://www.fws.gov/policy-library/603fw2) to assess whether proposed refuge activities are compatible with the purposes for which a refuge was established as well as the mission of the National Wildlife Refuge System as a whole. The addition of oil and gas development as a refuge purpose thus affects the context for a compatibility determination.

Oil and Gas Program Management

[P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) directed BLM to manage the oil and gas program on the ANWR Coastal Plain in a manner "similar to" the administration of lease sales under the Naval Petroleum Reserves Production Act of 1976 (NPRPA, 42 U.S.C. §§6501 et seq.) and associated regulations, except as otherwise provided. The NPRPA provided for competitive oil and gas leasing in the [National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska](https://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF13119) (NPR-A), subject to certain conditions. The regulatory framework for the NPR-A under the NPRPA ([43 C.F.R. Part 3130](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-43/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-3130)) includes requirements for leasing terms, bonding, environmental obligations, and other activities associated with oil and gas development. BLM's October 2025 [record of decision](https://eplanning.blm.gov/documents/?id=782546eb-a7f2-f011-8406-001dd80ef717&spid=7e1c2496-a8f2-f011-8406-001dd80ef717) (p. 6) for the oil and gas program discussed the agency's interpretation of this provision.

Revenues and Royalties

[P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) set the royalty rate for oil and gas leases in ANWR's Coastal Plain at 16.67%. The law directed that 50% of revenues derived from oil and gas leases on the Coastal Plain (including [royalties, rents, and bonus bids](https://www.crs.gov/Reports/R46537?source=search#_Toc51860227)) be distributed to the State of Alaska and that the remaining 50% be deposited into the U.S. Treasury as miscellaneous receipts. This split differs from the general framework established by the MLA (30 U.S.C. §191), under which the State of Alaska typically receives 90% of the revenue from federal onshore oil and gas leases within the state, minus a 2% cost-sharing deduction, with 10% deposited in the Treasury as miscellaneous receipts. (All other states receive 50% of the revenues from leases within their states, minus a 2% cost-sharing deduction, while 40% are deposited in the [Reclamation Fund](https://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF10042) and 10% are deposited in the Treasury as miscellaneous receipts.) For the more recent Coastal Plain lease sales required under [P.L. 119-21](http://www.congress.gov/law/119/pub/21), that law provided different revenue terms: the State of Alaska receives 50% of revenues from Coastal Plain leasing for FY2025-FY2033 and 70% of revenues thereafter, with the remainder deposited in the Treasury as miscellaneous receipts.

Schedule of Lease Sales

[P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) required at least two area-wide lease sales in the Refuge's Coastal Plain. An initial lease sale was required within four years of the law's enactment (i.e., by December 2021), and a second lease sale within seven years of enactment (December 2024). Each ANWR lease sale had to offer at least 400,000 acres and had to include those areas with the highest potential for discovery of hydrocarbons. [P.L. 119-21](http://www.congress.gov/law/119/pub/21) mandated four additional lease sales within one year, three years, five years, and seven years, respectively, of that law's July 2025 enactment. These sales also must offer at least 400,000 acres and must include those areas with the highest potential for discovery of hydrocarbons. For information on ANWR lease sales conducted to date, see CRS In Focus IF12006, [*Arctic National Wildlife Refuge: Status of Oil and Gas Program*](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF12006).

Surface Development and Rights-of-Way

[P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) required the Secretary of the Interior to grant any easements or rights-of-way necessary to carry out the provisions for exploration, development, production, and transportation in the Coastal Plain. It directed the Secretary to authorize "up to 2,000 surface acres" of federal land on the Coastal Plain to be covered by production and support facilities. (This 2,000-acre limit does not appear to apply to [Alaska Native surface lands](https://www.crs.gov/Reports/RL33872?source=search#_Toc196751306) on the Coastal Plain, owned by the Kaktovik Inupiat Corporation or through individual Native allotments.) The law specified that "airstrips and any area covered by gravel berms or piers in support of pipelines" shall be included in the 2,000 acres. Whether BLM could limit facilities to cover fewer than 2,000 acres under this provision has been [debated](https://alaskabeacon.com/2026/07/09/under-new-agreement-federal-government-limits-obstacles-to-oil-leasing-in-alaskas-arctic-refuge/). For the sales required in [P.L. 119-21](http://www.congress.gov/law/119/pub/21), that law stated that the provisions of [P.L. 115-97](https://www.congress.gov/law/115/pub/97) regarding rights-of-way and surface development also apply.
