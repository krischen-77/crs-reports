## Summary

The Arctic is a key strategic region for Russia with significant military and economic implications. Russia is the largest of the Arctic states in terms of territory, stretching over 24,000 kilometers of coastline. The Arctic is central to Russian strategic and military concerns and is home to Russia's Northern Fleet, which operates about two-thirds of Russia's submarine-based nuclear deterrent, according to the U.S. intelligence community's 2026 Annual Threat Assessment. The Arctic also plays an important role in Russia's energy and economic development plans. After the Cold War and prior to Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the Arctic was a region of multilateral cooperation, but tensions have since grown in the region between Russia and the other Arctic states. In a March 2025 speech at the Arctic Forum in Murmansk, Russian President Vladimir Putin stated, "the role and importance of the Arctic for Russia and for the entire world are obviously growing. Regrettably, the geopolitical competition and fighting for positions in this region are also escalating."

For over a decade, Russia has increased its military capabilities in the Arctic, including reestablishing old Soviet-era military bases, building new bases and infrastructure, announcing the creation of new units and increasing the size of existing forces, reorganizing command and control structures, and undertaking new efforts to modernize the Northern Fleet—including its submarine forces. It remains unclear whether Russia has the financial and personnel resources to carry out its military modernization efforts and plans in the Arctic, or in what time frame it could do so. Additionally, the Russian military posture in the Arctic has become increasingly precarious with the accession of Finland and Sweden into NATO (in 2023 and 2024, respectively) and heavy ground-force losses suffered in Ukraine. According to Lieutenant General James Adams, Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency, in April 2026 testimony before Congress, "As the Arctic becomes a focal point of international competition, the Kremlin is increasingly concerned by the growth of NATO forces in the region and the perceived threat they pose to Russia's economic interests. In response, Moscow is modernizing its military forces in the region to counter NATO, defend these economic interests, and ensure its dominance over the Northern Sea Route, though requirements in Ukraine constrain resources devoted to these efforts."

Depending on Russia's military modernization efforts in the Arctic, Congress may assess U.S. national security priorities in the region. Congress also may consider how increased Russian military capabilities could threaten U.S. and allied interests; evaluate allied and NATO plans and military posture; and deploy various legislative and oversight tools to support, reject, or otherwise modify the Administration's approach to the region. Potential considerations for Congress include

* the scale and speed of Russian military posture adjustments in the Arctic and the feasibility of Russia achieving those goals, including how those adjustments could affect U.S. and allied security and strategic objectives in the region;
* how to exercise oversight of plans to increase and/or modify the U.S. military presence in the Arctic;
* the state of current and future appropriations for Arctic-capable military assets, such as icebreakers; and
* how U.S. strategic and military plans for the region incorporate and synchronize with other allied partners and NATO.

## Introduction

The Arctic region is one of Russia's top strategic priorities.[1](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn1) According to the Department of Defense's (DOD's) *2024 Arctic Strategy*, "The Arctic plays a significant role in Russia's security and economic calculations."[2](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn2) Russia is the largest of the Arctic states in terms of territory, covering over 24,000 kilometers of Arctic Ocean coastline—some 53% of the Arctic Ocean's total coastline.[3](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn3) The Arctic holds an important role in Russian strategic thinking, and the region's economic development is a central component of Russia's energy and economic growth plans.[4](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn4) In March 2025, Russian President Vladimir Putin stated, "Today, the North has become central to [Russia's] development efforts. This is a sovereign and historical choice.... [T]he objectives we set for and address in the Arctic, and the projects we undertake here must match this historical scale and have a horizon spanning decades, if not centuries."[5](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn5)

Russia's strategic position in the Arctic has declined since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The Arctic region holds considerable economic potential, but Russia's ability to develop natural resources in the Arctic has been constrained by U.S. and European sanctions. Russia's military position also has become more vulnerable with conventional ground forces losses in Ukraine.[6](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn6) In addition, the accession of Finland and Sweden to NATO (in 2023 and 2024, respectively) brought NATO military capabilities, including long-range precision strike systems, closer to—and in range to potentially threaten—Russia's Northern Fleet, with its ballistic missile submarines.[7](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn7) On August 31, 2026, Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov stated, "NATO and other Western countries make no secret of their true motives, openly declaring their intention to turn the Arctic into a new front for opposing the development of Russia and its like-minded partners."[8](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn8) Some observers assert that these factors have contributed to a heightened Russian government perception that its Arctic economic and security interests are interconnected.[9](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn9)

## Russian Arctic Policy

Russia's Arctic policy focuses on ensuring Russia's sovereignty and status as an Arctic power, exploiting the region's natural resources (e.g., oil, natural gas), and developing the Northern Sea Route (NSR)—a shipping corridor running along Russia's northern Arctic coastline—as a viable transportation artery. Developing the NSR would shorten transport times between Europe and the Asia-Pacific region.[10](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn10) To achieve these objectives, the Russian government has increasingly securitized the Arctic and expanded Russia's military capabilities in the region to protect and project its Arctic ambitions.

Since 2020, the Russian government has adopted several new strategy documents outlining plans to bolster Russia's Arctic military capabilities, assert its sovereignty in the region, and develop the region's resources and infrastructure.[11](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn11) These documents include the following:

* *Foundations of the Russian Federation State Policy in the Arctic to 2035*, March 2020 (amended in February 2023)
* *Strategy for the Development of the Arctic Zone of the Russian Federation and Provision of National Security for the Period up to 2035*, October 2020 (amended in February 2023)

In addition to specific Arctic strategies, the Russian government's 2022 *Maritime Doctrine of the Russian Federation* identifies the "the Arctic basin adjacent to the coast of the Russian Federation, including the water area of the Northern Sea Route" as a "vital area" for Russia.[12](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn12) The Russian government's 2023 *Concept of the Foreign Policy of the Russian Federation* also highlights the Arctic as a priority region.[13](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn13)

The Russian government has taken steps to improve its control and coordination of the various policy actors to guide and implement its strategies in the Arctic. In August 2024, President Putin announced the creation of the Maritime Collegium under the control of Russia's former Secretary of the Security Council Nikolai Patrushev to oversee and coordinate Russia's various policy actors.[14](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn14) The Maritime Collegium has been tasked with developing and protecting Russia's status as a global maritime power, overseeing the development of the Arctic, and advancing Russia's interests in the region.[15](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn15)

## Russian Military Posture in the Arctic

The Arctic plays a key role in Russia's security posture and sea-based nuclear retaliatory capability (see [**Figure 1**](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#_Ref242955856)). Russia has invested in increasing its air defense, naval, and ground forces in the Arctic region (discussed below).[16](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn16) Russia's Northern Fleet—which covers much of the Arctic and is based at Severomorsk, in the Kola Peninsula bordering Norway and Finland—traditionally has received priority in the Russian Navy.[17](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn17) According to the 2026 *Annual Threat Assessment of the U.S. Intelligence Community*,

The bulk of Russia's Arctic forces are concentrated in the Kola Peninsula, which hosts about two-thirds of Russia's second-strike nuclear capabilities. The area is home to Russia's Northern Fleet, including seven of the country's nuclear-armed ballistic missile strategic submarines. Russia has made major investments in the fleet, including adding long-range missiles, UAVs [unmanned aerial vehicles], and underwater drones. Russia has at least three air bases on the Kola Peninsula that host fighter jets, surveillance, and transport aircraft.[18](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn18)

Additionally, Russia has announced a large-scale increase in conventional ground forces in the Arctic. According to DOD's 2024 *Arctic Strategy*,

Despite some attrition of Russia's conventional land forces due to losses in Ukraine, its strategic, air, and maritime forces remain intact. Further, Russia has demonstrated the ability to reconstitute and reorganize its conventional ground forces, which illuminates the potential for future improved readiness and combat expertise in the Arctic.[19](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn19)

### Northern Fleet

Largely unaffected by the war in Ukraine, the Northern Fleet continues to modernize and receive new and upgraded surface vessels and submarines.[20](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn20) According to the Norwegian Intelligence Service, "The modernization of the Northern Fleet is progressing slowly. More naval platforms are being armed with modern long-range precision-guided weapons. Over the course of 2026, the Northern Fleet will come to have three Severodvinsk-class multirole submarines and three frigates available, all carrying hypersonic Tsirkon missiles."[21](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn21)

Additionally, the Northern Fleet (as well as Russia's other fleets) was returned to the direct operational command of the Russian navy in 2023, and Russian officials and other observers have noted an increased role for the navy in Russian strategic thinking.[22](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn22) In 2025, Russian President Putin approved a new naval strategy to 2050 and allocated over 8.4 trillion rubles (over $106 billion at current exchange rates) for new shipbuilding over the next decade, much of it to bolster the Northern Fleet.[23](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn23) Some observers assess there is a potential disconnect between ambition and reality for further development of the Northern Fleet, especially as Russia's shipyards struggle to meet current output and maintenance requirements.[24](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn24)

### Aerospace Forces

Russia's Aerospace Forces in the Arctic are also undergoing reorganization and are essential to detect and interdict any air threats.[25](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn25) Air defense units previously under the Northern Fleet's command have been transferred to the 6th Air and Air Defense Army assigned to the Leningrad Military District (see below), which coordinates air assets in the region in concert with Aerospace Forces Main Command.[26](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn26) The Northern Fleet's air units have been reorganized into the 7th Composite Aviation Corps and include reconnaissance, multi-role strike fighter, transport, and rotary wing aircraft.[27](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn27)

### Ground Forces

Russian ground forces are an essential part of Russia's military strategy to protect its northwest border and the Northern Fleet in the Kola Peninsula.[28](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn28) Amid Finland's and Sweden's accession to NATO, the Russian military is adapting its force posture and has launched a large-scale reorganization effort to bolster its military capabilities in the region, including creating new units and increasing the size of existing forces (see text box, below).[29](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn29) In June 2026, Lieutenant General Pasi Välimäki, Commander of the Finnish Army, reportedly estimated that Russia could increase its ground forces based near Finland's borders from 20,000 to upwards of 80,000.[30](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn30) In 2024, the Russian military reactivated the Leningrad Military District to oversee and coordinate Russia's military posture in the region.[31](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn31) According to Finnish military intelligence, "the Leningrad Military District, which is located close to Finland, is planned to be significantly reinforced in the future. In the Kola region, an army corps will be expanded into an army, and a completely new army corps will be established in Karelia and several brigades will be expanded into divisions."[32](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn32)

To support its revised posture in the Arctic, the Russian military reportedly is supporting extensive infrastructure projects, which include renovating defunct Soviet-era military facilities and constructing new ones on Russia's borders with Finland and Norway.[33](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn33)

Russian military's Arctic ground forces redeployed to fight in the war in Ukraine have suffered significant casualties, posing challenges to Russia's force posture in the Arctic.[34](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn34) Russia faces reconstituting units that suffered attrition in Ukraine, and it would take time for Russia's Arctic units to regain the lost experience and for personnel to be adequately trained to operate effectively in an Arctic environment.[35](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn35) Some observers and allied intelligence assessments indicate that it is unclear whether the Russian military will be able to fully achieve its stated objectives in the Arctic given the demands for units and personnel in other regions.[36](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn36)

## U.S. Policy and Considerations for Congress

The Trump Administration has highlighted the importance of the Arctic for U.S. national security interests and has argued for an increased U.S. and NATO presence to counter activities by Russia and the People's Republic of China (PRC, or China) in the Arctic. According to the 2026 National Defense Strategy, "we [the United States] have seen adversaries' influence grow from Greenland in the Arctic to the Gulf of America, the Panama Canal, and locations farther south."[37](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn37) The Department of Defense reportedly is working on improving coordination among the combatant commands (European, Northern, and Pacific) with areas of responsibilities that overlap in the Arctic.[38](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn38) The Trump Administration also has sought to bolster some U.S. Arctic capabilities, such as the acquisition of icebreakers.[39](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn39) In October 2025, the Trump Administration signed a memorandum with Finland to procure up to 11 Arctic Security Cutters (ASCs), including 4 to be produced at shipyards in Finland, to address what the Administration describes as a "critical capability gap" with potential adversaries.[40](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn40)

NATO has increased its operations and presence in the Arctic, including by launching a new mission in February 2026 called Arctic Sentry in response to "Russia's increasing military activity and China's growing interest in the Arctic."[41](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn41) Among other actions, NATO has established a Forward Land Force in Finland's Lapland region, and the United Kingdom (UK) has pledged to double the number of UK troops deployed to Norway to 2,000.[42](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn42) In May 2026, the United States, Canada, the Kingdom of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden released a joint statement on Arctic security that recognized the strategic importance of the Arctic and acknowledged increased Russian military activity.[43](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn43)

In the 119th Congress, some Members have expressed concern regarding military activity and economic development in the Arctic by Russia and other foreign adversaries (see, for example, S.Res. 763 and S. 4708, the Arctic Security and Diplomacy Act) and their implications for U.S. security—such as early warning and monitoring for potential nuclear launches or bombers circumnavigating—in the region.[44](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn44) The FY2026 National Defense Authorization Act (P.L. 119-60, §5143) authorizes an Ambassador-at-Large for the Arctic; among other duties, the Ambassador is to establish and carry out an "Arctic Watcher Program," including to "monitor and combat the People's Republic of China, Russian Federation, and other malign influence campaigns across the Arctic region."[45](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn45) In addition, Section 5143(h) requires the Secretary of State, in coordination with the Director of National Intelligence and the Secretary of Defense, to annually submit to the "appropriate congressional committees a report on Russian and Chinese malign influence in the Arctic region." Additionally, Section 7019(e) of the National Security, Department of State, and Related Programs Appropriations Act, 2026 (Div. F, P.L. 119-75), and H.Rept. 119-217 required the State Department to provide a report to Congress on U.S. diplomatic priorities for the Arctic.[46](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn46)

In the 119th Congress, some Members may have an interest in assessing and conducting oversight of U.S. policy and considering legislation regarding the following:

* The effect of U.S. and European sanctions regimes on Russian military and economic infrastructure in the Arctic region, including the NSR.[47](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R49487_54406bced389049f91cb7da7b3085bbb8cb8a916.html#fn47)
* The status of Russia's military reconstitution of Arctic forces and the implications for NATO, partner, and U.S. regional priorities and strategic objectives.
* The progress of U.S. strategic and military Arctic plans and priorities, including the training, modernization, and status of U.S. and allied Arctic military capabilities and coordination among combatant commands.
* Current or proposed appropriations to fund Arctic-specific military systems, such as Arctic Security Cutters.
