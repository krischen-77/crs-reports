Senate Committee Reports a Farm Bill That
Would Delay Onset of States Sharing SNAP
Benefit Costs


Updated October 5, 2026

The Senate Agriculture Committee reported legislation (S. 5526) to the full Senate that would delay onset
of states sharing Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefit costs with the federal
government. The changes to SNAP cost-sharing are part of a proposed 2026 farm bill. Under current law,
states are scheduled to begin sharing SNAP benefit costs on October 1, 2027. The committee bill would
delay the cost-sharing onset by one year (to begin October 1, 2028). The proposal would also increase the
maximum share of SNAP benefits that states with the highest payment error rates would have to pay,
raising it from 15% to 20%, beginning FY2031.

The House-passed 2026 farm bill (H.R. 7567) has no provision affecting federal-state cost sharing,
retaining current law. The Senate committee bill has other significant changes to the nutrition title of the
farm bill compared to the House bill, though these other changes are not discussed in this Insight.

SNAP Federal and State Financing

Historically, the cost of SNAP benefits has been 100% federally financed. States are responsible for
certifying household eligibility and issuing benefits to households. States’ associated administrative costs
have been split about 50%-50% between the federal government and the states.

The FY2025 budget reconciliation law (P.L. 119-21) newly requires a state that has a payment error rate
of 6% or more to pay a share of the state’s SNAP benefit costs. The payment error rate is an estimate of
the dollar value of both overpayments and underpayments to households divided by total benefit
payments in a state. The estimate comes from the SNAP quality control system, which takes a sample of
households from each state and determines the accuracy of their SNAP eligibility and benefit
determinations. The FY2025 reconciliation law provision’s basing SNAP cost-sharing on payment error
rates is in addition to financial liabilities from high error rates that were in preexisting law.

Under current law, the onset of cost-sharing can be delayed if a state’s error rate is sufficiently high.
States with estimated FY2025 payment error rates of 13.33% or higher have cost-sharing delayed until
October 1, 2028 (FY2029). States with estimated FY2026 payment error rates of 13.33% or higher will
have state cost-sharing delayed until October 1, 2029 (FY2030).

The FY2025 reconciliation law also increases the state share of administrative costs to 75%, effective
October 1, 2026.

Most States Would Share Costs Under FY2025 Payment Error Rates

In June 2026, the U.S. Department of Agriculture released FY2025 payment error rates. Figure 1 shows
jurisdictions ranked by their FY2025 payment error rates and where those rates place them in the FY2028
SNAP cost-sharing parameters. Those with the highest payment error rates (of 13.33% or higher) qualify
for a delay in cost-sharing to at least FY2029. Those with payment error rates of less than 6% would
retain 100% federal financing of benefits. Other jurisdictions are subject to cost-sharing beginning in
FY2028 unless their FY2026 payment error rates fall to less than 6%.

Proposed Changes to Scheduled State SNAP Benefit Cost-Sharing

The 2026 farm bill reported by the Senate Agriculture Committee would delay the onset of state SNAP
benefit cost-sharing by one year. It would begin on October 1, 2028 (FY2029). States would be permitted
to base their FY2029 cost-sharing on the lower of FY2026 or FY2027 payment error rates.

States with payment error rates of 13.33% or higher in both FY2025 and FY2026 would qualify for a
delay in cost-sharing until FY2030. In addition, beginning with FY2031, states with error payment rates
of 10% or higher would be required to pay 20% (instead of 15%) of benefit costs.

The Senate Agriculture Committee farm bill would not amend the FY2025 reconciliation law’s increase in
the state share of administrative costs.
