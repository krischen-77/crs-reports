Farm Bill Primer: Rural Broadband Provisions
Updated November 20, 2026 (IF13248)

Since 2002, Congress has included provisions addressing rural broadband (i.e., high-speed internet access) in the rural development title of farm bills. The Agriculture Improvement Act of 2018 (2018 farm bill; P.L. 115-334) amended, reauthorized, and codified many of the rural broadband programs administered by the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA). The 2018 farm bill also provided authorizations of appropriations for many of the USDA broadband programs.

The 119th Congress is considering farm bills with broadband provisions. The House-passed Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026 (H.R. 7567) and the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry-reported Agricultural Act of 2026 (S. 5526) would both amend and reauthorize some of the broadband programs in the 2018 farm bill and create new broadband programs.

Background

According to June 2026 data from the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), about 12% of people living in rural areas in the United States lack fixed terrestrial (excluding satellite and mobile technology) broadband access at the benchmarked speeds of at least 100 megabits per second download and 20 megabits per second upload (i.e., 100/20 Mbps). The USDA Rural Utilities Service administers four programs to support broadband deployment in rural areas.

  • 1. The Community Connect Grant Program provides grants to deploy broadband to economically distressed communities and their essential community facilities (7 U.S.C. §950bb-3).
  • 2. The Distance Learning and Telemedicine Program provides grants to help rural communities acquire the technology and training to connect educational and medical professionals with students, teachers, and patients in rural areas (7 U.S.C. §950aaa-2).
  • 3. The ReConnect Program provides grants, loans, and loan-grant combinations to build and update facilities or equipment needed to provide broadband access to rural areas. The program requires broadband service to be provided through fixed terrestrial technologies. Fiber-optic cable ("fiber") is the most common fixed terrestrial technology able to provide broadband service at the required program speeds. As of November 2026, the ReConnect Program is a pilot program without specific statutory authority. Congress provided USDA general statutory authority to create the pilot program through the Rural Electrification Act of 1936, as amended (7 U.S.C. §§901 et seq.).
  • 4. The Rural Broadband Program provides loans, loan-grant combinations, and loan guarantees to construct or improve facilities and equipment used to provide broadband access to rural areas (7 U.S.C. §950bb).

2018 Farm Bill Provisions

The rural development title of the 2018 farm bill and its extensions authorized appropriations for rural broadband programs and included program-specific provisions, which are discussed below.

Community Connect Grant Program

Congress codified the Community Connect Grant Program to provide grants to eligible entities for broadband transmission projects in rural areas.

Distance Learning and Telemedicine Program

Congress required USDA to set aside at least 20% of the funding for the Distance Learning and Telemedicine Program for telemedicine projects providing substance abuse treatment services through FY2026.

Rural Broadband Program

Congress added a grant component to the Rural Broadband Program in addition to loans and loan guarantees. Prior to the 2018 farm bill, Congress had provided funding for loans and loan guarantees but not for grants. Congress directed USDA to prioritize funding to project applications that would serve rural communities without broadband service. Rural communities without broadband service for this program are communities without residential broadband service of at least 10/1 Mbps. Congress also required USDA to prioritize funding to project applications that would provide broadband service to rural communities that have populations of less than 10,000 permanent residents, are experiencing out-migration, or are isolated from other population centers.

Other Rural Broadband Provisions in the 2018 Farm Bill

Additional provisions in the 2018 farm bill that relate to rural broadband include the following:

  • Verify applicant eligibility. Congress directed USDA to consult with the National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) to verify applicant eligibility for USDA broadband programs, using the broadband assessment and mapping data provided by NTIA (7 U.S.C. §950bb-6(a)). Congress also directed USDA to ensure that no duplication exists across broadband programs administered by USDA, FCC, and NTIA (7 U.S.C. §950bb-6(b)).
  • Public database. Congress directed the Secretary of Agriculture to make available to the public a searchable database on the Rural Utilities Service website. This database would include information on applications submitted to USDA broadband programs that would provide broadband to end users, such as homes and businesses (7 U.S.C. §950cc). The database is also to identify the applicants that received funding. USDA Rural Development established a public searchable map database, which includes approved projects and those under review for all USDA broadband programs.
  • Innovative Broadband Advancement Program. Congress reauthorized the Rural Gigabit Network Pilot and renamed it the Innovative Broadband Advancement Program. The program is authorized to provide loans and grants for projects that demonstrate innovative broadband technologies that decrease costs and increase broadband service speeds in rural areas (7 U.S.C. §950bb-2). Congress provided authorization of appropriations but, to date, has not funded this program.
  • Middle mile infrastructure. Congress established a new program to provide grants, loans, and loan guarantees for middle mile broadband infrastructure projects in rural areas (7 U.S.C. §950bb-1). Congress defined middle mile infrastructure as "any broadband infrastructure that does not connect directly to end-user locations (including anchor institutions) and may include interoffice transport, backhaul, Internet connectivity, data centers, or special access transport to rural areas" (7 U.S.C. §950bb-1(b)). Congress provided authorization of appropriations but, to date, has not funded this program.

Selected Broadband Program Provisions in H.R. 7567 and S. 5526

Rural Broadband Program

H.R. 7567 and S. 5526 differ in how they would restructure certain aspects of the existing Rural Broadband Program, including the name of the program. H.R. 7567 would amend the authority for the Rural Broadband Program, terminate the ReConnect Program, and transfer the unobligated funds into the proposed ReConnect Rural Broadband Program. S. 5526 would amend the authority for the Rural Broadband Program, rename it the ReConnect Program, and require USDA to simplify the application process. Under both proposals, USDA would be authorized to issue grants, loans, and loan guarantees (H.R. 7567 would also allow for loan-grant combinations). Under S. 5526, funding would be prioritized for applicants that demonstrate experience with constructing and operating broadband networks. The bill would also allow for the constructed broadband network to be owned and operated by an entity other than the awardee.

Both proposed programs would allow for a range of technologies to deliver broadband service, although S. 5526 directs the USDA to prioritize applications for projects that "can easily scale speeds over time" to meet future bandwidth needs (i.e., fiber). In some remote or rural areas, low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite technologies can provide broadband service where other technologies, such as fiber or cable, may not be feasible due to geographical limitations. In addition, some of these technologies may have lower deployment costs than fiber in certain cases. Not all technologies provide equal reliability. For example, weather conditions or heavily forested terrain may cause interruptions in LEO service due to the requirement that the satellite be in view of both the customer's and the provider's ground stations. Another potential consideration is the limited competition in broadband service provided by LEO satellites: Customers may be dependent on a small number of businesses and their respective business decisions (e.g., to adjust prices). Congress may consider if and how to prioritize funding for broadband technologies to effectively address rural U.S. connectivity.

Community Connect Grant Program

H.R. 7567 and S. 5526 would amend the eligible service areas for the Community Connect Grant Program to areas where households do not have broadband service at speeds of at least 25/3 Mbps (an increase from the current speed of 10/1 Mbps). The build-out speed differs between H.R. 7567 (at least 250/125 Mbps) and S. 5526 (at least 100/20 Mbps). These proposed changes may result in broader eligibility for rural communities to be provided with service at higher speeds. Should more areas be eligible, the competition for this funding may increase.

Proposed Broadband Technical Assistance Program and Last Acre Program

H.R. 7567 would establish the Broadband Technical Assistance Program, which would make grants to eligible entities for the purpose of delivering technical assistance and training to rural communities to improve access to USDA broadband programs. The bill would also authorize USDA to make grants to eligible entities for the purpose of collecting broadband service data to use in a variety of ways (e.g., establishing the availability of broadband in rural areas, determining eligible service areas for USDA broadband programs).

S. 5526 does not contain a Broadband Technical Assistance Program provision. The bill would establish the Last Acre Program, which would authorize USDA to issue grants and loans to eligible entities to provide 100/20 Mbps broadband service to agricultural lands that lack broadband at those speeds. USDA would be required to collect data from farmers and ranchers on broadband access on their farms and to create an online portal to allow farmers and ranchers to register to be considered as eligible service areas.

Congress may consider whether the implementation of new grant programs would need additional appropriations. Congress may also consider redirecting funding from existing USDA broadband programs that build out broadband infrastructure and how this may impact expansion of broadband service in rural areas.
