Senate Committee Reports a Farm Bill That Would Delay Onset of States Sharing SNAP Benefit Costs

Updated October 5, 2026
(IN12723)

The Senate Agriculture Committee reported legislation (S. 5526) to the full Senate that would delay onset of states sharing Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefit costs with the federal government. The changes to SNAP cost-sharing are part of a proposed 2026 farm bill. Under current law, states are scheduled to begin sharing SNAP benefit costs on October 1, 2027. The committee bill would delay the cost-sharing onset by one year (to begin October 1, 2028). The proposal would also increase the maximum share of SNAP benefits that states with the highest payment error rates would have to pay, raising it from 15% to 20%, beginning FY2031.

The House-passed 2026 farm bill (H.R. 7567) has no provision affecting federal-state cost sharing, retaining current law. The Senate committee bill has other significant changes to the nutrition title of the farm bill compared to the House bill, though these other changes are not discussed in this Insight.

### SNAP Federal and State Financing

Historically, the cost of SNAP *benefits* has been 100% federally financed. States are responsible for certifying household eligibility and issuing benefits to households. States' associated *a**dministrative* costs have been split about 50%-50% between the federal government and the states.

The FY2025 budget reconciliation law (P.L. 119-21) newly requires a state that has a *payment error rate* of 6% or more to pay a share of the state's SNAP benefit costs. The payment error rate is an estimate of the dollar value of both overpayments and underpayments to households divided by total benefit payments in a state. The estimate comes from the SNAP *quality control system*, which takes a sample of households from each state and determines the accuracy of their SNAP eligibility and benefit determinations. The FY2025 reconciliation law provision's basing SNAP cost-sharing on payment error rates is in addition to financial liabilities from high error rates that were in preexisting law.

Under the FY2025 reconciliation law provision, the percentage of benefit costs a state is required to pay depends on the state's payment error rate. The benefit cost-sharing is scheduled to start October 1, 2027 (beginning of FY2028), though it is delayed for certain states with high payment error rates (see below).

The FY2028 benefit cost-sharing percentage will be based on the lowest payment error rates from FY2025 or FY2026. For FY2029 and subsequent years, the benefit cost-sharing percentage will be based on the payment error rate for the third preceding fiscal year.

Under current law, the onset of cost-sharing can be delayed if a state's error rate is sufficiently high. States with estimated FY2025 payment error rates of 13.33% or higher have cost-sharing delayed until October 1, 2028 (FY2029). States with estimated FY2026 payment error rates of 13.33% or higher will have state cost-sharing delayed until October 1, 2029 (FY2030).

The FY2025 reconciliation law also increases the state share of administrative costs to 75%, effective October 1, 2026.

### Proposed Changes to Scheduled State SNAP Benefit Cost-Sharing

The 2026 farm bill reported by the Senate Agriculture Committee would delay the onset of state SNAP benefit cost-sharing by one year. It would begin on October 1, 2028 (FY2029). States would be permitted to base their FY2029 cost-sharing on the lower of FY2026 or FY2027 payment error rates.

States with payment error rates of 13.33% or higher in both FY2025 and FY2026 would qualify for a delay in cost-sharing until FY2030. In addition, beginning with FY2031, states with error payment rates of 10% or higher would be required to pay 20% (instead of 15%) of benefit costs.

The Senate Agriculture Committee farm bill would not amend the FY2025 reconciliation law's increase in the state share of administrative costs.
