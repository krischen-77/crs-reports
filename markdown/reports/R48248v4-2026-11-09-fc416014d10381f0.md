The Paris Agreement (PA) and Nationally
Determined Contributions (NDCs)

The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) has been the
foundational multilateral treaty for cooperation among nations on greenhouse gas (GHG)-induced
climate change since its adoption in 1992.1 The Paris Agreement (PA) is a multilateral treaty in
pursuit of the UNFCCC’s objective and guided by its principles.2 Out of 198 Parties to the
UNFCCC, 194 are Parties to the PA as of November 9, 2026.3 The United States is no longer a
Party to the PA, as of January 3, 2026.4 For more information about the United States withdrawal
from the PA, see CRS Report R48504, U.S. Withdrawal from the Paris Agreement: Process and
Potential Effects, by Jonathan D. Haskett.

The PA defines a collective, long-term objective of “holding the increase in the global average
temperature to well below 2℃ [Celsius] above pre-industrial levels and pursuing efforts to limit
the temperature increase to 1.5℃ above pre-industrial levels.”5 In pursuit of that objective, the PA
requires all parties, regardless of “developed” or “developing” country status, to maintain
nationally determined contributions (NDCs) that specify by how much a party plans to reduce
their GHG emissions, among other things.6

NDCs are the primary vehicle through which Parties to the PA communicate their intended
contributions to achieving countries’ collective temperature and climate goals. NDCs reflect each
country’s own national climate objectives and plans, including emission reduction goals,
adaptation plans, and other elements. Under the terms of the PA, all NDCs must address GHG
emissions reductions and all NDCs should list economy-wide absolute GHG emission reduction
targets, with flexibility for developing countries to move over time toward such targets.7 NDCs
are updated every five years. Each NDC update is a progression that reflects the “highest possible
ambition” in light of each country’s particular circumstances.8

In advance of the adoption of the PA at COP21 in Paris, Parties were invited to communicate their
intended NDC (INDC).9 Upon a Party’s ratification of the PA, the Party’s INDC became their
First NDC. Thus, each Party has submitted at least one NDC.10 Thereafter, Parties are required to
communicate their NDC every five years. The most recent NDCs were due in 2025 and cover the

Conditional NDCs

When preparing NDCs, some countries specify conditions which reflect the country’s domestic
context and capabilities that would allow the implementation of certain measures.17 Parties to the
PA recognized that countries have different capacities to mitigate GHG emissions, by stating that
“enhanced support for developing country Parties will allow for higher ambition in their
actions.”18 Many countries requesting international support include two sets of targets: targets
unconditional on international support, and more ambitious targets conditional on international
support (financial, technical, or other support). Some countries combine these targets while others
only include conditional GHG mitigation targets, indicating that without international support,
goals to reduce GHG emissions will not be met.

Table 1 lists every Party with an NDC target conditional on international support. As of
November 9, 2026, 129 of 194 Parties had submitted NDCs conditional on international support.
NDC targets are extracted from countries’ NDC submissions found in the UNFCCC’s NDC
Registry.19 Table 1 reflects the most recent submissions found in the NDC Registry. Thus, some
of the NDC targets are from a Party’s first or second NDC and have not been updated by the
Party. To provide consistency throughout Table 1, the language used to describe the NDC targets
may reflect slight modifications to the language used in the NDCs themselves.

Some countries’ submissions may include targets for several years. However, in Table 1, the
Congressional Research Service (CRS) reflects only the target for the period required by the
pertinent NDC. For example, a country may include 2030, 2035, and 2050 targets in their 2025
