Summary

Domestic Situation

Ecuador is an ethnically and geographically diverse country of 18.1 million people, encompassing parts of South America's Pacific coast, Amazon Basin, and Andean highlands. President Daniel Noboa (2023-present), of the right-leaning National Democratic Action (ADN) movement, has largely centered his policymaking on addressing Ecuador's public security crisis. Since roughly 2021, illicit activity linked to transnational criminal organizations (TCOs) has contributed to a security crisis in Ecuador, with gangs fighting for control of drug trafficking routes and prisons and attempting to influence politics. Other aspects of Noboa's agenda include pursuing International Monetary Fund-backed economic reforms to increase investment and improve government finances. Noboa's ADN has forged an informal legislative majority with the support of smaller parties to advance his legislative agenda; he also has sought to advance his agenda through two referendums, with mixed results.

Under the Noboa administration, some observers have voiced concerns about an erosion of democratic norms, including judicial independence and press freedoms. Critics also have argued that several law enforcement investigations targeting members of Ecuador's largest political opposition party, left-of-center Citizen Revolution, are politically motivated. The political opposition also has accused the Noboa administration of co-opting independent electoral institutions following several decisions favorable for ADN ahead of subnational elections scheduled for November 2026. Noboa has rejected claims of rising authoritarianism under his government.

U.S. Relations and Congressional Interests

Following a period of bilateral tensions under former President Rafael Correa (2007-2017), U.S.-Ecuador ties have strengthened, including under President Noboa. During the second Trump Administration, the United States and Ecuador have expanded bilateral security cooperation and conducted combined military operations to curtail drug trafficking and other illicit activity linked to TCOs. Since August 2025, the U.S. government has designated four Ecuadorian gangs as foreign terrorist organizations and specially designated global terrorists. The two countries also have signed nonbinding bilateral agreements to allow the transfer of some third-country migrants from the United States to Ecuador (July 2025) and to advance cooperation on critical minerals (February 2026) and trade (March 2026).

Congress, at times, has sought to shape the executive branch's approach to U.S.-Ecuador relations. In 2022, for example, Congress enacted the United States-Ecuador Partnership Act of 2022 as Title LV, Subtitle B, of the James M. Inhofe National Defense Authorization Act for FY2023 ([P.L. 117-263](http://www.congress.gov/cgi-lis/bdquery/R?d117:FLD002:@1(117+263))). Authorized until December 2027, the act includes measures aimed at strengthening bilateral security cooperation and commercial ties to promote democracy and regional stability and to counter malign foreign influence.

In the 119th Congress, Members have expressed a range of views on Ecuador and U.S. policy. Some Members have commended President Noboa as a strong U.S. ally and called for an expansion of bilateral security cooperation to dismantle TCOs. Some Members have voiced concern about human rights conditions amid Ecuador's security crisis and possible democratic erosion under President Noboa; those of this view have called on the Trump Administration to suspend combined U.S.-Ecuador military operations and to ensure U.S. security assistance does not contribute to potential human rights abuses. Looking ahead, Congress may continue to assess security, economic, and human rights conditions in Ecuador—including any implications for regional security efforts—and consider legislative and other measures to shape bilateral relations.

Political and Economic Conditions

President Daniel Noboa of the right-leaning National Democratic Action (ADN) political movement first took office in November 2023. Ecuadorians elected Noboa to complete the term of former President Guillermo Lasso (2021-2023), who had called snap elections held in August 2023. In April 2025, Noboa won a full four-year presidential term after obtaining 55.6% of the vote in a presidential runoff election against Luisa González of the left-of-center Citizen Revolution (RC) movement.[1](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn1 "CRS Insight IN12530, Ecuador's 2025 Elections: Implications for U.S. Policy .") Although González alleged voter fraud in the election, international observers expressed confidence in the results, while noting concerns about "conditions of inequity during the campaign" that favored Noboa's candidacy.[2](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn2 "Organization of American States (OAS), 2025 Presidential Runoff in Ecuador: Preliminary Report OAS Electoral Observation Mission , April 15, 2025 , p. 2. The OAS electoral observation mission has not released a final electoral observation report.")

In February 2025, Ecuador held legislative elections to elect all 151 members of the unicameral National Assembly. Support was largely divided between ADN (66 seats) and RC (67 seats) blocs. Noboa's ADN has forged an informal legislative majority with the support of smaller parties. Intraparty disagreements since the elections have reduced RC's legislative bloc and eroded the block's voting cohesion.[3](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn3 "Patricio Díaz, \"El Oficialismo Consol idó Mayoría en la Asamblea tras Fracturas en el Correísmo y Pachakutik,\" Ecuadvisa , May 15, 2026.")

President Noboa has sought to implement portions of his agenda through national referendums, with mixed results. In November 2025, voters rejected four Noboa-backed proposals, including measures that would have reversed a constitutional ban on foreign military bases in Ecuadorian territory and established a constituent assembly to draft a new constitution.[7](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn7 "CNE, \"Resultados Definitivos de la Consulta Popular y Referéndum 2025,\" Resolución PLE-CNE-3-1-12-2025 , December 1, 2025.")

Governance and Human Rights Concerns

Some observers have voiced concerns about an erosion of democratic norms under the Noboa administration. In August 2025, the National Assembly passed a law that increased government oversight and regulation of nongovernmental organizations (NGOs), which President Noboa argued was necessary to detect and prevent possible illicit financial flows tied to money laundering and tax evasion.[8](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn8 "Amnesty International, Impact of R estrictive L aws on C ivil S ociety O rganizations in the Americas , March 23, 2026; and Robel Revelo, \"Noboa E xpide R eglamento de la Ley de Transparencia Social, que R egula el F inanciamiento de las F undaciones ,\" Primicias , October 27, 2025.") In late 2025, U.S.-based NGO Human Rights Watch asserted that Noboa administration officials used the law to freeze the bank accounts of Indigenous and environmental groups that had protested the government's removal of diesel subsidies.[9](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn9 "Human Rights Watch, \" Ecuador: Government Freezes Groups' Bank Accounts ,\" December 9, 2025.") Some observers have asserted that President Noboa has used combative rhetoric to increase political pressure on Ecuador's Constitutional Court—potentially undermining judicial independence—since the court suspended some Noboa-backed security legislation in mid-2025.[10](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn10 "See, for example, UN Office of the High Commissioner on Human Rights, \" Ecuador: Interference with Constitutional Court T hreatens Ru le of L aw and S afeguards A gainst A buse of P ower ,\" August 15, 2025.") Some groups have raised alarms about deteriorating press freedoms, pointing to increased violence against journalists by criminal groups and pressure from government officials.[11](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn11 "See, for example, Reporters Without Borders, \"The Americas: Rather than Helping Hard-pressed Journalists, Authorities are Threatening Them,\" 2026 World Press Freedom Index , April 30, 2026.")

November 2026 Subnational Elections

On November 29, 2026, Ecuador is scheduled to hold subnational elections for more than 5,000 provincial, municipal, and local offices, including 23 prefectures (provincial government heads) and 222 mayorships.[12](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn12 "CNE , \"Convocatoria Elecciones Seccionales y CPCCS 2027,\" Resolución P LE-CNE-1-31-7-2026 , July 31, 2026. In 23 of Ecuador's 24 provinces, executive power is divided between a directly elected prefect and a presidentially appointed governor. The province of Galápagos , which is governed by a governing council is the only exception .") Voters are also set to elect all seven members of the Council for Citizen Participation and Social Control (*Consejo de Participación Ciudadana y Control Social*)—an autonomous oversight entity charged with conducting the selection process for several independent national offices, including the attorney general, human rights ombudsman, comptroller, and electoral authorities, among other functions.[13](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn13 "Consejo de Participación Ciudadana y Control Social , \"Nuestra Institución,\" https://www.cpccs.gob.ec/nuestra-institucion/introduccion/ .") All elected officials are scheduled to take office on May 14, 2027, for four-year terms.

The political opposition has accused the Noboa administration of co-opting independent electoral institutions following several decisions seemingly favorable for ADN ahead of the elections.[14](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn14 "\"Ecuador: Suspended Opposition Party Warns of Encroachin g 'Dictatorship ' ,\" LatinNews Daily , March 10, 2026.") In March 2026, a judge on Ecuador's Electoral Dispute Tribunal (TCE) imposed a nine-month suspension on RC, the largest opposition party, amid an ongoing illicit campaign finance investigation.[15](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn15 "The investigation is one of several initiated by Ecuador's acting attorney general , which RC representatives argue is politically motivated. Tribunal Contencioso Electoral, Causa Nro. 029-2926-TVE, Boleta de Notificación, March 9, 2026.") The suspension prevents RC from fielding candidates in the elections. Later that month, electoral authorities rescheduled the elections to be held in November 2026 instead of February 2027, as originally scheduled, to avoid potential weather disruptions related to an anticipated El Niño climate pattern.[16](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn16 "CNE, \" Resolución de Actualización del Calendario Electoral, \" PLE-CNE-1-27-3-2026 , March 30, 2026.") Some critics argued that the move was a politically motivated attempt to reduce the time available for RC-affiliated candidates to change party affiliation ahead of the elections.[17](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn17 "See, for example, Pedro Labayen Herrera, \"Ecuador News Round-Up No. 26: Noboa's Authoritarianism Deepens Amid Growing US Support,\" Center for Economic and Policy Research, June 23, 2026.") In April 2026, electoral officials dissolved two other political organizations for alleged insufficient membership; the TCE reversed one party's dissolution following a legal challenge in June.[18](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn18 "Roger Vélez, \"Jueza del TCE Deja Sin Efecto Cancelación de Unidad Popular tras Aceptar Recursos de Partido,\" Primicias , June 3, 2026.") In July 2026, the TCE suspended another left-of-center party (*AMIGO*)—which had partnered with RC to field some of its candidates—under similar circumstances to that of RC.[19](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn19 "Jackeline Beltrán, \" Movimiento Amigo, que C obija a los C andidatos del C orreísmo, es S uspendido por N ueve M eses por el TCE ,\" Primicias , July 17, 2026.") In August 2026, the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights, an independent regional commission, called on the Noboa administration to ensure "fair political participation" in the upcoming elections.[20](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn20 "Inter-American Commission on Human Rights , \" IACHR C alls on Ecuador to E nsure P luralist P olitical P articipation in U pcoming L ocal E lections ,\" August 31, 2026.")

Security Crisis[21](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn21 "Unless otherwise noted, this section draws from International Crisis Group , Paradise Lost? Ecuador's Battle with Organised Crime , Report No. 109, November 12, 2025; and U.S. Department of State (DOS), International Narcotics Control Strategy Report (INCSR) , Vol. I: Drug and Chemical Control , March 2025 , p p. 193-197 .")

Homicide rates in Ecuador were among the lowest in Latin America for decades, but violence has risen since roughly 2021; from 2023 to 2025, Ecuador reportedly had the highest homicide rate in the region.[22](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn22 "See data from InSight Crime's Homicide Round-Up for 2023, 2024, and 2025, https://insightcrime.org/news/special-series/yearly-homicide-round-up/  .") The increase in violence has been driven primarily by increased competition among Ecuadorian gangs affiliated with Mexican cartels and other TCOs, shifts in regional drug-trafficking patterns, and entrenched corruption in Ecuador's law enforcement and judicial institutions. These gangs fight for control of drug trafficking routes, primarily in Ecuador's coastal provinces, that are used to ship Colombian and Peruvian cocaine through Ecuador's ports to international markets. In March 2026, a U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) official stated that about 70% of the world's cocaine is shipped from Ecuadorian ports.[23](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn23 "U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), \"FBI Opens Permanent Office in Ecuador,\" March 19, 2026.") High-fatality riots in prisons, from which criminal groups reportedly run their operations, have plagued Ecuador since 2020. Ecuador also has faced increased violence tied to TCO-linked illicit mining operations. Some TCOs appear to have infiltrated Ecuadorian government institutions. In late 2024, for example, a judge convicted 20 people—including former judges, government officials, and police—for their roles in a corruption network linked to organized crime.[24](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn24 "Fiscalía General del Estado, \"Metastasis Case: Prosecutor's Office Obtains Sentences Against 20 Defendants for Organized Crime,\" November 26, 2024.")

Since January 2024, President Noboa has repeatedly asserted that his government is engaged in an "internal armed conflict," designated 22 local criminal groups as terrorist organizations, enabled the armed forces to conduct public security functions, and declared numerous states of emergency to address the violence.[25](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn25 "Presidente Daniel Noboa, Decreto Ejecutivo 111 , January 9, 202 4 and Decretjo Ejecutivo 424 , June 18, 2026 .") Noboa's approach has led to the arrest of some high-profile gang leaders, although violence remains pervasive. Several human rights groups have alleged that some security forces have committed human rights violations, including enforced disappearances.[26](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn26 "UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights, \" UN Committee on Enforced Disappearances P ublishes F indings on Ecuador, Iraq, Malawi , and Samoa ,\" March 25, 2026.")

Economic Conditions

After three years of declining economic growth amid security challenges and seasonal energy shortages driven by drought and aging energy infrastructure, Ecuador's economy rebounded with 3.7% gross domestic product (GDP) growth in 2025; the International Monetary Fund (IMF) projects 2.7% GDP growth in 2026.[27](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn27 "Gross domestic product data in this section are drawn from International Monetary Fund (IMF), World Economic Outlook database, October 2026.") Ecuador's growth in 2025 was aided in part by low inflation, an improved labor market, and strong non-oil exports.[28](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn28 "IMF, Ecuador: Fifth Review Under the Extended Arrangement Under the Extended Fund Facility , April 22, 2026.") Although Ecuador is a net energy exporter, which may allow it to benefit somewhat from increased international oil prices tied to the 2026 U.S.-Israel conflict with Iran, elevated energy prices also have increased government expenditures on remaining fuel subsidies that the Noboa administration has not yet phased out.[29](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn29 "\"Subsidios a Combustibles Aumentan Pese a Baja de Precios en Ecuador,\" El Oriente , July 15, 2026.") Fluctuations in rainfall tied to the El Niño climate pattern could also disrupt agricultural production and increase food prices, according to the Economist Intelligence Unit.[30](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn30 "Economist Intelligence Unit, One-Click Report: Ecuador , October 6 , 2026.") Reduced rainfall could also hinder hydroelectric power output, which accounts for more than 70% of Ecuador's electricity generation; the Noboa administration initiated electricity rationing measures for some commercial consumers in late September 2026.[31](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn31 "Energy Institute, Statistical Review of World Energy , 2026, pp. 63, 66 ; and see, for example, Ministerio de Ambiente y Energía, X post, October 9, 2026 , https://x.com/EcuadorMAE/status/2108701017953239510 .")

A priority for the Noboa administration is to improve Ecuador's fiscal situation in line with a $5 billion IMF financing agreement originally approved in May 2025 (IMF officials expanded the agreement by $1 billion in July 2025). In April 2026, the IMF Executive Board approved a disbursement of about $400 million, bringing total disbursements to date under the agreement to about $3.7 billion.[32](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn32 "IMF, Ecuador: Fifth Review Under the Extended Arrangement Under the Extended Fund Facility , April 22, 2026.") President Noboa also has sought to boost foreign investment in Ecuador, including from the People's Republic of China (PRC, or China), which reportedly has served as a key source of financing and investment since roughly 2009.[33](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn33 "Sebastián Hurtado, \"Noboa's Balancing Act Between China and the U.S.,\" Americas Quarterly , July 2, 2025.") A free trade agreement between the two countries entered into force in May 2024. President Noboa has traveled to China twice to meet with PRC leader Xi Jinping, most recently in August 2026. During the visit, the two leaders signed seven agreements to strengthen cooperation in various areas, including trade and investment, energy, and security. China also committed nearly $43 million for projects intended to improve security, health, and education.[34](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn34 "Gobierno del Ecuador, \"Ecuador y China Abren una N ueva Etapa de su Relación con el Inicio de la Cooperación Fotovoltáica y Nuevos Acuerdos,\" August 18, 2026.")

U.S.-Ecuador Relations and Issues for Congress

During the second Trump Administration, U.S.-Ecuador cooperation on several issues of U.S. concern has increased. The two countries have expanded bilateral security cooperation and initiated combined military operations to combat TCO-linked illicit activity under the Shield of the Americas and Americas Counter Cartel Coalition (A3C)—two interrelated U.S.-led regional initiatives composed largely of ideologically aligned Western Hemisphere governments.[35](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn35 "The Department of Defense (DOD) is \"using a secondary Department of War designation,\" under Executive Order 14347 of September 5, 2025. DOD, \"Americas Counter Cartel Conference Joint Security Declaration,\" March 5, 2026; and DOS, \"Joint Statement on Defending Hemispheric Sovereignty,\" September 22, 2026.") During a September 2026 visit to Ecuador, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio stated that Ecuador was "the most involved and aggressive partner" in the Shield of the Americas and A3C.[36](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn36 "DOS, \" Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Ecuadorian Foreign Minister Roberto Kury Deliver Statements ,\" September 10, 2026 (hereinafter: DOS, September 10, 2026.)") During his visit, Secretary Rubio also announced the launch of a Millennium Challenge Corporation compact with Ecuador, which he stated would include a $12 million grant to the Ecuadorian government to benefit the country's electricity sector and economy.[37](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn37 "DOS, September 10, 2026.") In 2025 and 2026, the two countries signed nonbinding bilateral agreements on migration, critical minerals, and trade (discussed below).

Congress, at times, has sought to shape the executive branch's approach to U.S.-Ecuador relations. In 2022, Congress enacted the United States-Ecuador Partnership Act of 2022 as Title LV, Subtitle B, of the James M. Inhofe National Defense Authorization Act for FY2023 ([P.L. 117-263](http://www.congress.gov/cgi-lis/bdquery/R?d117:FLD002:@1(117+263))). Authorized until December 2027, the act includes measures aimed at strengthening the bilateral partnership between the United States and Ecuador in support of democratic institutions and rule of law, sustainable and inclusive economic growth, and conservation.

Security Cooperation

Over the past decade, successive U.S. Administrations have expanded security cooperation with Ecuador to support the country's efforts to counter TCO-driven violence and related illicit activity. In February 2024, Ecuador ratified a status of forces agreement (SOFA), which creates a framework for U.S. military personnel to operate in Ecuador, and an agreement enabling combined maritime naval operations to combat drug trafficking and other illicit activities.[38](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn38 "DOS, Ecuador (24-223.1) – Status of Forces Agreement , s igned at Quito October 6, 2023 , entered into force February 23, 2024 ; and Ecuador (24-223) – Agreement Concerning Counter Illicit Transnational Maritime Activity Operations , s igned at Washington , September 27, 2023 , entered into force February 23, 2024 .") In June 2026, President Noboa issued an executive decree stating that foreign personnel participating in security operations would "enjoy immunity" in accordance with international agreements Ecuador has signed.[39](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn39 "Presidente Daniel Noboa, Dec r eto Ejecutivo 424 , June 18, 2026.") Legislative Action and Issues for Congress

Although the National Security, Department of State, and Related Programs (NRSP) Appropriations Act, 2026 ([P.L. 119-75](http://www.congress.gov/law/119/pub/75), Division F), did not provide an aggregate amount for bilateral aid in Ecuador, the act and its explanatory statement specified $30 million in INCLE aid and $10 million in FMF for Ecuador. It also allocated an additional $20 million in INCLE aid to combat illegal mining in Ecuador, Colombia, and Peru. During the FY2027 appropriations process, Congress could consider whether to appropriate law enforcement and/or military assistance for Ecuador or to leave such allocation decisions to the executive branch. The House-passed FY2027 NSRP appropriations bill ([H.R. 8595](http://www.congress.gov/bill/119/hr/8595)) and its accompanying report ([H.Rept. 119-631](http://www.congress.gov/congressional-report/119/hrpt/631)) would designate $40 million in INCLE aid and $12.5 million in FMF aid for Ecuador. [H.Rept. 119-631](http://www.congress.gov/congressional-report/119/hrpt/631) would also direct that an unspecified amount of assistance be made available for Ecuador to combat illicit activity, to strengthen Ecuador's democratic institutions and rule of law, and to promote economic development. The Senate Appropriations Committee has yet to mark up an FY2027 NSRP appropriations bill.

During the 119th Congress, Members have expressed a range of views on U.S.-Ecuador security cooperation. Some Members of Congress have called for an expansion of bilateral security cooperation to dismantle TCOs.[61](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn61 "See, for example, Letter from Senator Bernie Moreno to U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth ( who is using \"Secretary of War\" as a \"secondary title\" under Executive Order 14347 dated September 5, 2025 ), May 27, 2026.") Members supportive of expanded U.S.-Ecuador security cooperation could seek to appropriate increased security assistance for Ecuador, designate the country as a major non-NATO ally legislatively, or take up other legislation to shape the U.S. policy approach toward shared security issues of concern, including illegal mining (e.g., [S. 3496](http://www.congress.gov/bill/119/s/3496)).

Trade Ties

The United States is Ecuador's largest goods trading partner. U.S.-Ecuador goods trade reached $17.6 billion in 2025, with the United States running a $543 million trade deficit. Petroleum oils and gases (e.g., liquefied propane and butane) accounted for more than 60% of total U.S. goods exports, while top U.S. imports from Ecuador included crude oil, shrimp, and cocoa beans.[68](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn68 "U.S. Census Bureau data, as reported by Trade Data Monitor , accessed October 29 , 2026.")

In March 2026, the U.S. and Ecuadorian governments signed an agreement on reciprocal trade (ART).[69](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn69 "Unless otherwise noted, this paragraph draws from U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) , \"Agreement Between the United States of America and the Republic of Ecuador on Reciprocal Trade,\" signed March 13, 2026 .") The nonbinding ART aims to reduce tariff and nontariff barriers to trade and to increase national security alignment by cooperating on investment security and export control measures. The agreement, inter alia, would set a new tariff schedule for some U.S. imports from Ecuador. In October 2026, Ecuador's Constitutional Court ruled that the ART requires legislative approval before its ratification in Ecuador.[70](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn70 "Corte Constitucional (@CorteConstEcu), X post, October 16, 2026, https://x.com/CorteConstEcu/status/2111191006448148918.") U.S. imports from Ecuador may continue to face additional tariffs. In July 2026, the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) imposed tariffs, with some exceptions, on goods imported from 60 economies, including Ecuador's, after an investigation under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, as amended (19 U.S.C. §§2411-2420), into the alleged "failure to impose and effectively enforce a prohibition on the importation of goods produced with forced labor."[71](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn71 "Office of the U.S. Trade Representative, \" Notice of Actions in Section 301 Investigations of Acts, Policies, and Practices of Various Economies Related to the Failure of Each Economy t o Impose and Effectively Enforce a Prohibition on the Importation of Goods Produced w ith Forced Labor ,\" 91 Federal Register 47318 , Ju ly 28 , 2026.") As a result of the ART, Ecuador was one of 17 economies that received a 10% tariff rate rather than the 12.5% rate imposed for most of the other investigated economies.

Migration Cooperation

The Noboa administration has cooperated with the United States on migration issues. In July 2025, the two governments entered into an agreement allowing the transfer of some third-country nationals from the United States to Ecuador.[77](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn77 "U.S. Department of Homeland Security, \" Agreement Between the Government of the United States of America and the Government of the Republic of Ecuador Relating to the Transfer of Third-Country Nationals to Ecuador ,\" 90 Federal Register 51376 , exchange of diplomatic notes dated July 16, 2025 , and July 23, 2025, November 17, 2025.") Although the agreement text does not specify requirements for such transfers, Ecuadorian officials reportedly have stated that Ecuador has agreed to receive a maximum of 300 adult Spanish-speaking asylum seekers with no criminal records from the United States per year to pursue their protection claims in Ecuador.[78](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn78 "See, for example, Carlos Cuevas, \"Este es el Acuerdo con el que Ecuador Recibirá Desde EE.UU. Migrantes que Solicitan Asilo,\" Bloomberg Línea , November 14, 2025.") Between January and July 2026, Ecuador reportedly received 121 migrants of various nationalities (with Venezuelan, Cuban, and Honduran nationals accounting for the majority) from the United States pursuant to the agreement.[79](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn79 "\"Decenas de Migrantes Llegan a Ecuador en Vuelos desde Estados Unidos,\" Ecuavisa , July 17, 2026.")

Some NGOs have argued that Ecuador is not equipped to receive asylum seekers, in part due to the country's security challenges, which have contributed to emigration from Ecuador.[80](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn80 "See, for example, Center for Gender & Refugee Studies, \"The U.S.-Ecuador Asylum Cooperative Agreement: Why It Fails the Law and Endangers Asylum Seekers,\" March 25, 2026.") According to UN data, an increasing number of Ecuadorians have sought asylum abroad since 2020, including over 122,000 in 2025 (with 93% of claims made in the United States).[81](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn81 "UN High Commissioner on Refugees, Refugee Data Finder , accessed July 20, 2026, https://www.unhcr.org/refugee-statistics .") Violence also has driven increased internal displacement. One NGO recorded 132,000 internal displacements due to conflict and violence in Ecuador in 2025, a 31% increase from 2024 and the third-highest level recorded among countries in the Americas, after Haiti and Colombia.[82](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn82 "Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre, Global Report on Internal Displacement , May 2026, p. 49.")

Legislative Action and Issues for Congress

Congress may conduct oversight over third-country migrant transfers from the United States to Ecuador as it assesses whether to support, modify, or reject such transfers through its legislative prerogatives. During the 119th Congress, some Members have sought more information about human rights conditions in Ecuador and measures to deter potential human rights abuses, including within the context of the country's third-country migrant transfer agreement with the United States. [S.Res. 879](http://www.congress.gov/bill/119/sres/879), introduced in September 2026, would require the State Department to provide a report within 30 days of adoption on human rights practices in Ecuador, including the treatment of third-country migrants transferred from the United States, pursuant to Section 502B of the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961, as amended (22 U.S.C. §2304). The resolution would prohibit the delivery of security assistance to Ecuador unless subsequently authorized by law until the report is provided.[83](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49188.html#fn83 "22 U.S.C. §2304(c)(3). For more information, see CRS In Focus IF11197, U.S. Arms Sales and Human Rights: Legislative Basis and Frequently Asked Questions , by Paul K. Kerr and Michael A. Weber .") Congress also could consider whether to authorize, restrict, or require reporting on the use of U.S. foreign assistance to implement the third-country migrant transfer agreements with Ecuador and other countries in FY2027 NSRP appropriations legislation.
