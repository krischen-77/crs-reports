Summary

There are many federal civil service leadership and support positions in the legislative and executive branches of the federal government that may be subject to noncareer (political) appointments. The nature of these positions varies widely and includes positions such as agency heads and their immediate subordinates, policy executives and advisors, and aides who report to these officials. Since 1952, successive volumes of United States Government Policy and Supporting Positions have identified the positions in the federal government to which political appointments can be made. The book is often referred to as the "Plum Book" due to both the purple cover of the physical book and the list of positions found within its pages. In 2022, Congress passed legislation requiring the information in the Plum Book to be published in an online database.

Congress may be interested in identifying certain information about these positions. Such information could include, for each position, its title, type of appointment, name of individual(s) currently holding the role, location, salary, tenure (if applicable), and expiration date of the term (if applicable).

This report provides a brief history and overview of the Plum Book and Plum Reporting website, an explanation of the various political appointment types listed on the website, and examples of regional positions within federal agencies.

United States Government Policy and Supporting Positions (The "Plum Book")

After winning the presidential election in 1952, President Eisenhower requested a list of federal government positions to which he could make noncareer appointments. As a result, the United States Government Policy and Supporting Positions book1 was created. The book is often referred to as the "Plum Book" due to both the purple cover of the physical book and the list of positions found within its pages. Since 1960, the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs and the House Committee on Government Reform (and the committees' predecessors) alternately published new editions of the Plum Book every four years.

The major categories of positions listed in the Plum Book include the following:

  • Executive Schedule and salary-equivalent positions paid at the rates established for levels I through V of the Executive Schedule
  • Senior Executive Service "General" positions
  • Senior Foreign Service positions
  • Schedule C positions excepted from the competitive service by the President or by the director of the Office of Personnel Management (OPM) because of the confidential or policy-determining nature of the position duties
  • Other positions at the GS-14 level and above excepted from the competitive civil service by law because of the confidential or policy-determining nature of the position duties2

The Plum Reporting Website

In 2022, Congress passed the Periodically Listing Updates to Management Act (or PLUM Act) as a title of the James M. Inhofe National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023.3 The PLUM Act requires the OPM director to establish and maintain an online database4—entitled the "Plum Reporting website" 5—of content previously published in the Plum Book.6

As seen in the Figure 1 screenshot below, this database lists positions in the federal government to which political appointments can be made. The database is organized by appointing agency and generally includes, for each position, position title, name of incumbent, geographic location of the position, type of appointment, salary,7 and (if an appointment to the office has a fixed term) its length and expiration date.8 In some cases, it also lists individuals serving in acting roles.9

Not every listed position on the Plum Reporting website has an entry in every column. For example, the expiration column will have an entry only if the incumbent is serving in a position that has a fixed term.10

The information on the website is self-reported by each agency, and OPM publishes updates. It is typically not updated in real time. Under Title 5, Section 3330f(4)(A), of the U.S. Code, agency heads are required to provide updates not less than once a year on the policy and supporting positions in the agency, appointees occupying such positions, and appointees formerly occupying the positions.

While agencies are required to certify that the information provided is complete and accurate,11 the data provided on the website is only as current as the most recent update conducted by OPM. This issue of currency could result in discrepancies between an agency's real-time appointment numbers and those provided in the database.

Plum Reporting Website Searches

The Plum Reporting website provides drop-down categories that allow searching and filtering by agency12 (e.g., department, independent regulatory, boards and commissions) and component13 (e.g., bureau, office). By clicking "Advanced Filters" at the top right-hand corner of the search box, a user can additionally search by position, type of appointment, pay plan, and position status.14 The screenshot in Figure 2 displays what the website looks like when "Advanced Filters" is checked.

The database also has the following search features available:

  • Filtering by Chief Financial Officer (CFO) Act agencies15 only feature. This feature restricts results to positions within CFO agencies.16 The toggle for the feature is located above the search filters and to the right of the "Agency" drop-down box.
  • "Display Historical Data" feature. This feature shows historical data for a position along with the current result. When this feature is activated, the name of the prior incumbent will appear below the current incumbent, and the dates of service will display below the position title (which can be clicked to display the position lineage). OPM notes that when a user clicks on a historical result, "historical records only include incumbencies within the current administration." This button is located under the search filters and above the results on the right-hand side of the page.
  • "Search within results" feature. This feature allows a user to type keywords to filter results. This text box is located under the search filters and above the results on the right-hand side of the page.
  • "Download" feature. This feature allows a user to download all data in the Plum Reporting dataset in a CSV file (a type of file in the format of a spreadsheet). Once the data is downloaded, a user can filter or sort the data into a spreadsheet format. This download button is located under the search filters and above the results on the right-hand side of the page. It is labeled "All Data (CSV)" in blue text.
  • "Sort by" feature. This feature allows a user to sort results by position title; expiration date; ID; level, grade, or pay; location; name of incumbent; pay plan; tenure; or type of appointment. This drop-down menu is located under the search filters and above the results on the left-hand side of the page.

Positions may not always appear under their expected respective bureaus or offices. This may depend on how a department decides to submit a position for the website.17

Vacant Positions

In addition to the position titles and names of the incumbents, the Plum Reporting website also lists the vacancy status of a position.18 As mentioned previously, because the Plum Reporting website's data is typically not updated in real time, some of the information in the database may not be current.

Those seeking vacancy information may wish to supplement their Plum Reporting website searches by consulting the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO) Vacancy Tracker,19 federal agency websites, and Congress.gov's nominations database20 as additional resources. It can be difficult to establish with certainty whether an agency, without a formal announcement, either (1) filled a vacancy by designating an individual to serve in an acting capacity or (2) assigned the duties of a vacant position to a current employee who is still officially occupying his or her current position.

Source: Office of Personnel Management, "PLUM Reporting: Plum Data," September 27, 2026, https://www.opm.gov/about-us/open-government/plum-reporting/plum-data/.

On the Plum Reporting website itself, positions listed as vacant do not display the appointment type associated with the position, as seen above in Figure 3. OPM notes that this is because "several categories of positions can be filled by more than one type of appointment, e.g., Senior Executive Service (SES) positions listed in this publication may be filled by using career, noncareer, limited emergency, or limited term appointment authorities."21

If a user, keeping this OPM treatment of the appointment type in mind, clicks the drop-down and searches for a specific "Type of Appointment," the database will display both the relevant filled and vacant positions matching those criteria, even if the listed vacant positions themselves do not actively display an appointment type on the screen.

Additionally, if the Plum Reporting website data is downloaded into a CSV file, vacant positions will display their associated types of appointment in Column E of the downloaded spreadsheet. An example can be seen in Figure 4. This may be useful when attempting to identify vacant positions with presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed (PAS) and presidentially appointed (PA) appointment types. For such positions, the category of position cannot be filled by more than one appointment type, so the appointment type should appear in every case.

Historical Federal Political Appointment Information

The website also hosts a Plum Archive,22 which includes an Excel spreadsheet of positions listed on the website during the Biden Administration (January 21, 2021, through January 20, 2025) and PDFs of the published Plum Book editions from 2016, 2020, and 2024.23 Because of the snapshot nature of Plum Book data, it may not be comprehensive or include all previous positions or prior incumbents.

A Brief Overview of Select Political Appointment Types

The Plum Reporting website includes positions that can be filled through political (or noncareer) appointment. It does not include positions in the civil service that are filled through a competitive hiring process.24

A brief description of the position types listed in the database is provided below.

  • Presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed (PAS). These positions are often referred to as "advice and consent positions" or "PAS positions." PAS positions are filled through nomination of an individual by the President. The President submits the nomination to the Senate for its consideration.25 When the Senate confirms a nomination, the President may formally appoint the individual to the position. Under certain circumstances, the President can exercise constitutional recess appointment authority to appoint an individual to an office while the Senate is in recess.26 Most PAS appointees serve at the pleasure of the President.27
  • The Plum Reporting website does not list positions in the judicial branch, such as U.S. circuit court and district judge positions, though these positions are presidentially appointed and Senate-confirmed.28
  • Presidentially appointed. These positions are often referred to as "PA" positions. A PA appointment requires the President to select and appoint an individual for the position. No Senate confirmation is required. Most PA appointees serve at the pleasure of the President.29
  • Schedule C. Schedule C appointees serve in confidential or policy-determining positions.30 Such appointments do not go through a merit-based competitive hiring process. Rather, in most cases, an agency submits a request to OPM to place a position in a Schedule C appointment category, and the authorization and granting of each position is considered on a case-by-case basis.31 Schedule C appointees "serve at the pleasure of the appointing authority (usually the agency head) and may be removed at any time."32 OPM states that "Schedule C positions authorized by OPM are automatically revoked when the incumbent leaves the position (i.e., there is no such thing as a 'vacant' Schedule C position)." If an individual departs a role and a "vacancy" occurs, the agency must submit to OPM a new request to fill the position as a Schedule C appointment type.
  • Career Senior Executive Service (SES) appointment. Career SES appointees typically serve in top-level policy and supervisory roles.33 These positions are filled through a merit-based competitive hiring process. Generally, incumbents may be removed from the SES for less than fully successful executive performance.34 Career appointees who are removed from the SES for performance reasons may be placed in non-SES civil service positions.35 Career appointees may also be removed generally from federal service for misconduct, neglect of duty, malfeasance, or failure to accept a directed reassignment or to accompany a position in a transfer of function.36
  • Most SES positions are filled through career appointments.37 And, not all career SES positions are listed on the Plum Reporting website. For example, the website does not include SES positions that are "career reserved."38 Approximately half of federal SES positions fall into this category. Career-reserved positions may be filled only through career appointments and therefore do not meet the criteria for inclusion in the Plum Book or Plum Reporting website. OPM publishes an annual list of "career reserved" SES positions in the Federal Register.39
  • Noncareer SES appointment. Noncareer SES appointees are not hired through a competitive process. However, they must meet the position qualifications,40 and OPM must generally approve an agency's use of noncareer hiring authority. A noncareer appointee serves at the pleasure of the appointing authority (usually the agency head) and may be removed at any time.41 Up to 10% of SES positions across the executive branch may be filled by noncareer appointees at any given time.42 The proportion of noncareer appointees may vary by agency, however, so noncareer appointments at some agencies may exceed 10%, while such appointments at other agencies may be fewer than 10%. Generally, no agency may exceed a 25% limit.43
  • Limited term SES appointment (TA). Limited term appointees typically serve in short-term project-related or presidential transition roles. Their service may not exceed a period of three years.44 Such an appointee serves at the pleasure of the appointing authority (usually the agency head) and may be removed at any time. By law, limited appointments may not exceed 5% of all SES positions government-wide.45
  • Limited emergency SES appointment (EA). Limited emergency appointees typically serve in short-term roles to meet urgent and unanticipated needs. Their service may not exceed a period of 18 months.46 Such an appointee serves at the pleasure of the appointing authority (usually the agency head) and may be removed at any time.
  • Appointment excepted by statute (XS). These appointments are authorized by the parent agencies' governing legislation and are subject to the terms of said legislation. Sometimes these positions are referred to as excepted service positions.47

Identifying Political Appointee Positions Located at the State, City, or Regional Level

Congressional offices may be interested in identifying political appointee positions located at the state, city, or regional level.

While the Plum Reporting website lists some positions that are appointed to specific states or districts,48 such as U.S. Marshals and U.S. Attorneys, it does not list any U.S. district or circuit judge positions.49

Below, CRS has included several screenshots from the Plum Reporting website of searches for political appointee positions at the state, city, and regional levels.

Source: Office of Personnel Management, "PLUM Reporting: Plum Data," September 27, 2026, https://www.opm.gov/about-us/open-government/plum-reporting/plum-data/

To identify positions located in a specific state or city, a user can either search for a city or state within the "Search Within Results" box on the Plum Reporting website (see screenshots above and below) or download the dataset into a CSV file and filter it by location, found in column G of the downloaded spreadsheet.

When searching using the "Search Within Results" box on the Plum Reporting website, incomplete results may occur. For example, typing the state name may pull up positions that have the state name in the position titles but may not capture all positions located in the state.50 Similarly, typing a state abbreviation into the "Search Within Results" may pull up extraneous results in addition to positions within the state.51

As such, trying a variety of search options may yield more comprehensive results.

Career reserved positions are those that involve day-to-day functions of government and require impartiality. By law (5 U.S.C. §3132(b)(1)) and regulation (5 C.F.R. §214.402), certain types of positions must be removed from political influence to ensure public confidence in the impartiality of certain government functions. Examples include audits, inspections, civil or criminal law enforcement, grants administration, and security matters, the principal duties of which involve day-to-day government operations.
