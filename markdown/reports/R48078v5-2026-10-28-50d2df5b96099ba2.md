## Summary

Non-U.S. nationals (*aliens* under federal statute and regulation) subject to removal from the United States may seek asylum and other forms of humanitarian protection during standard and expedited removal processes. Asylum may be granted to persons in the United States, regardless of their immigration status, who are unable or unwilling to return to their countries because of persecution or a well-founded fear of persecution based on race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group, or political opinion.

The Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) authorizes the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to charge aliens with immigration violations and process them for standard removalproceedings in immigration court. During removal proceedings, an alien may apply for asylum, withholding of removal, and protection from removal under the Convention Against Torture (CAT) to avoid removal. Immigration courts are within the Executive Office for Immigration Review, an agency within the U.S. Department of Justice.

Section 235(b) of the INA (8 U.S.C. §1225(b)) establishes a separate, expedited removal process for certain aliens. Under this process, if an immigration officer determines that an alien lacks valid entry documents or has tried to procure admission through fraud or misrepresentation, the alien may be ordered removed without further hearing or review. The expedited removal process may be used for persons apprehended by DHS at a U.S. port of entry, at or near a U.S. border between ports of entry, or in the interior of the United States if they have been present in the country for less than two years without being admitted or paroled. During expedited removal, individuals who express fear of persecution or torture if returned to their countries of origin or express an intent to apply for asylum may undergo credible fear screenings to determine whether they may qualify for asylum or other forms of protection from removal. An alien who meets the threshold for a credible fear of persecution or torture may be referred to an immigration judge and placed in standard removal proceedings.

Although the law specifies which aliens may be subject to expedited removal, DHS generally has discretion over whether to place those individuals in either expedited or standard removal and may make such decisions based on operational circumstances on a case-by-case basis. Those persons whom DHS processes directly for standard removal—even if they are subject to expedited removal under the law—do not undergo credible fear screenings.

This report addresses frequently asked questions about credible fear and asylum, including those related to expedited and standard removal processes; credible fear screening processes, criteria, and legislative history; procedural protections; and data regarding credible fear and asylum outcomes.

## Introduction

The Immigration and Nationality Act (INA)[1](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn1) provides multiple processes for the removal of individuals who are not citizens or nationals of the United States (defined as *aliens* under the INA)[2](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn2) charged with immigration violations, including standard and expedited removal pathways. Both pathways allow individuals subject to removal to seek humanitarian protection, including through asylum, withholding of removal, and protection under the Convention Against Torture (CAT).[3](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn3)

During standard removal proceedings, immigration judges[4](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn4) in the U.S. Department of Justice's (DOJ's) Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR) determine whether an alien who has been charged with an immigration violation by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) is removable and whether that individual qualifies for any relief or protection from removal for which he or she has applied, including asylum. A person placed directly in standard removal does not undergo a credible fear screening.

Section 235(b) of the INA (8 U.S.C.§1225(b)) establishes a separate, expedited removal process for certain aliens. Under this process, if an immigration officer determines that the alien lacks valid entry documents or has tried to procure admission through fraud or misrepresentation, the alien may be ordered removed without further hearing or review.[5](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn5) The expedited removal process may potentially be used for persons apprehended by DHS at U.S. ports of entry, at or near the U.S. border between ports of entry, or in the interior of the United States if they have been present in the country for less than two years without being admitted or paroled.[6](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn6) Courts and DHS have generally treated the exercise of expedited removal authority as discretionary, meaning that DHS may opt to place an alien otherwise subject to expedited removal into standard removal proceedings instead.[7](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn7)

If an individual subject to expedited removal expresses a fear of persecution or torture if returned to his or her country of origin or an intent to apply for asylum, that person is referred for a *credible fear interview* with a DHS U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) asylum officer.[8](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn8) Individuals who during credible fear interviews show a *significant possibility* of establishing eligibility for asylum or related protections are referred to standard removal proceedings, where they may file asylum applications with the immigration court.[9](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn9)

This report addresses frequently asked questions related to defensive asylum and credible fear,[10](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn10) including those related to standard and expedited removal processes; credible fear screening processes, criteria, and legislative history; procedural protections; and data regarding credible fear and asylum outcomes.

## Asylum and Related Forms of Relief

### What is asylum?

Asylum can be granted to aliens who are physically present in the United States or arrive in the United States (whether or not at a port of entry) who are unable or unwilling to return to their home countries because of past persecution or a well-founded fear of future persecution based on one of five statutorily defined protected grounds (race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group, or political opinion) and who satisfy other requirements.[11](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn11) Asylum is a discretionary form of relief, meaning an alien might be denied such relief despite meeting eligibility requirements.[12](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn12) Persons granted asylum (known as asylees) can become U.S. lawful permanent residents (LPRs) after one year of physical presence in the United States as asylees.[13](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn13) When an individual in removal proceedings applies for asylum, it is considered a *defensive* asylum application.[14](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn14)

### What related forms of relief or protection may be available to asylum seekers?

In some cases, an alien may be statutorily barred from either applying for or receiving asylum (e.g., because the application is untimely or the applicant has been convicted of a particularly serious crime).[15](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn15) However, even if an alien is ineligible for asylum, he or she may still pursue related forms of relief, including withholding of removal and protection under the CAT.[16](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn16)

An applicant for withholding of removal must meet a higher burden than the standard for asylum and must prove that it is more likely than not (i.e., a clear probability) that he or she will be persecuted because of one of the five protected grounds.[17](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn17) An applicant for CAT protection must show that it is more likely than not that he or she will be tortured by a public official or other person acting with the consent or acquiescence of an official.[18](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn18)

Unlike asylum, withholding of removal and CAT protection are mandatory forms of protection from removal and may not be denied as a matter of discretion (i.e., the adjudicator must grant the applicant protection if the qualifications are met).[19](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn19) Nonetheless, while asylum affords the recipient an opportunity to pursue LPR status, a grant of withholding of removal or CAT protection provides no path to LPR status and only prevents removal to the country where the alien would likely face persecution or torture (but not necessarily to a third country).[20](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn20)

### Do all asylum seekers have credible fear interviews?

The only asylum seekers who undergo credible fear interviews (also known as *credible fear screenings*) are those who are placed in expedited removal proceedings (see "[What is expedited removal?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164244977)") and express a fear of persecution or torture, a fear of returning to their home countries, or an intent to apply for asylum.[21](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn21)

Persons placed directly in standard removal proceedings do not have credible fear interviews. These persons do not have to establish a credible fear to be able to request asylum from an immigration judge (see "[What is standard removal?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164088427)").

## Standard and Expedited Removal Processes

DHS has two primary law enforcement components responsible for immigration enforcement. U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) is primarily responsible for immigration enforcement at ports of entry and between ports of entry along the border. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is mainly responsible for interior enforcement. All aliens who arrive in the United States and those who are already in the country but have not been admitted (e.g., those who entered unlawfully) are considered *applicants for admission*.[22](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn22)These individuals are subject to inspection by immigration officers, who may determine whether they are subject to removal.[23](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn23)

### What is standard removal?

The INA provides for the removal of aliens whom DHS—typically ICE or CBP—charges with immigration violations.[24](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn24) DHS may process such an individual for standard removalproceedings by issuing a Notice to Appear (NTA) charging document and filing it with an immigration court.[25](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn25) DHS is required to detain certain individuals during their removal proceedings (e.g., those who have been convicted of committing certain crimes). In most other cases, DHS has discretion to release individuals on bond or their own recognizance.[26](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn26) Since 2025, immigration detention capacity has expanded substantially through congressional appropriations, allowing ICE to detain a larger number of aliens.[27](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn27)

During removal proceedings, an immigration judge determines whether the alien (referred to as the *respondent*) is removable as charged in the NTA and if that individual is eligible for any forms of relief or protection from removal, including asylum.[28](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn28) To be considered for asylum, the respondent must file an application for asylum with the immigration court. Individuals placed in standard removal proceedings do not undergo credible fear screenings as part of the asylum application process, although they may be referred to standard removal proceedings as the result of positive credible fear findings (see "[What is expedited removal?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164244977)").

Parties in standard removal proceedings include the respondent, who may or may not have counsel (see "[Do asylum applicants have legal representation during standard removal proceedings?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref163465353)"), and DHS, which is represented by counsel from ICE's Office of the Principal Legal Advisor.

#### What is the process for standard removal proceedings?

Standard removal proceedings typically consist of multiple hearings before an immigration judge. A respondent who fails to appear for any hearing may be ordered removed *in absentia* (see "[How many asylum applicants fail to appear for their immigration court hearings?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164086065)").[29](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn29) During the initial *master calendar hearing*, the immigration judge explains the respondent's rights, the charges against the respondent, and the nature of the proceedings; verifies the respondent's contact information; provides information about legal representation; and sets filing dates for applications.[30](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn30) To apply for asylum, the respondent must file an application with the court, generally within one year of arrival in the United States.[31](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn31) A respondent may use the same application to apply for withholding of removal and CAT protection, which may be available to those who are ineligible for asylum.[32](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn32)

During an evidentiary *merits hearing* (also called an *individual hearing*), the immigration judge addresses any challenges to DHS's charges of removability and considers the respondent's application(s) for relief and protection from removal.[33](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn33) Parties may present witnesses, testimony, and evidence. At the conclusion of the proceedings, the immigration judge issues a decision.

#### What are the possible outcomes of standard removal proceedings?

At the conclusion of the standard removal hearings, the immigration judge may do the following:

* Issue a removal order[34](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn34)
* Find the respondent removable and allow him or her to request *voluntary departure* and leave the United States at his or her own expense[35](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn35)
* Grant relief or protection from removal, such as asylum
* Terminate or dismiss a case[36](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn36)

The immigration judge may terminate a case in some circumstances, including, for example, if the respondent establishes eligibility for U.S. citizenship or demonstrates that the charges on the NTA are substantively or procedurally defective.[37](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn37) The immigration judge may dismiss a case when DHS moves to dismiss proceedings under certain grounds.[38](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn38) Parties may appeal an immigration judge's decision within 30 days to the Board of Immigration Appeals, EOIR's appellate component.[39](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn39)

#### Do asylum applicants have legal representation during standard removal proceedings?

The INA provides respondents with the privilege of being represented by counsel "at no expense to the government."[40](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn40) Respondents may obtain counsel at their own expense, obtain pro bono services, or represent themselves (pro se). The federal government generally may not fund counsel, but there are exceptions. Under EOIR's National Qualified Representative Program, counsel is provided to unrepresented detained respondents with mental disorders and disabilities that "may render them incompetent to represent themselves in immigration proceedings."[41](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn41) The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Office of Refugee Resettlement, which is responsible for the care and custody of unaccompanied alien children also funds legal services for some children under requirements specified in the William Wilberforce Trafficking Victims Protection Reauthorization Act of 2008 (P.L. 110-457).[42](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn42)

EOIR is required under federal regulations to provide respondents with a list of free or low-cost legal service providers.[43](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn43) Persons eligible to serve as respondents' representatives in immigration court are specified in federal regulation.[44](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn44) As of May 2026, among all pending immigration court cases, 45% of respondents were represented.[45](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn45)

### What is expedited removal?

Expedited removal is a streamlined removal process that may be used in cases involving certain arriving aliens and aliens who already entered the United States without inspection. An immigration officer may order the removal of a person arriving in the United States without a hearing or further review of the order to remove the individual if that person is inadmissible because he or she lacks valid entry documents or procured or sought to procure admission by fraud or misrepresentation.[46](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn46) In addition, Section 1225(b)(1) authorizes the DHS Secretary[47](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn47) to apply the expedited removal process to persons present in the United States who have not been admitted or paroled[48](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn48) if they have been in the country less than two years and are inadmissible because they lack valid entry documents or procured or sought to procure their admission by fraud or misrepresentation.[49](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn49)

Based on this statutory authority, DHS has historically employed expedited removal primarily for three categories of inadmissible aliens: (1) individuals arriving at U.S. ports of entry (e.g., a land border crossing or an international airport), (2) individuals who arrived in the United States by sea within the previous two years and have not been admitted or paroled by immigration authorities, and (3) individuals found in the United States within 100 air miles of the border within 14 days of entering the country who have not been admitted or paroled by immigration authorities.[50](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn50)

Under the second Trump Administration, DHS has implemented expedited removal to the fullest extent authorized by Section 1225(b)(1) to include all aliens who are present in the United States without admission or parole, have been in the country less than two years, and lack valid entry documents, or procured or sought to procure admission through fraud or misrepresentation.[51](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn51)

Until 2025, during the expedited removal process, DHS officers generally asked the aliens questions to assess whether they feared returning to their home countries, including asking why they left their homes or countries, whether they have any fear of being returned, and whether they would be harmed in their countries.[52](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn52) In February 2025, DHS introduced a new protocol in which an alien is instead asked the reason for leaving the home country or country of last residence and whether that person has any questions or anything to add.[53](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn53)

Although a person in expedited removal proceedings typically has no right to a hearing or review of the expedited removal order, there is an exception for individuals who express an intent to apply for asylum, a fear of persecution or torture, or a fear of returning to their home countries.[54](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn54) In these cases, the immigration officer must refer the individual for an interview with an asylum officer (see "[Who conducts credible fear interviews?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164085958)") to make a credible fear determination.[55](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn55)

#### What is a credible fear determination?

A credible fear determination results from a screening process that evaluates whether a person placed in expedited removal proceedings might qualify for asylum, withholding of removal, or protection from removal under the CAT.[56](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn56) Federal statute defines *credible fear of persecution* as "a significant possibility, taking into account the credibility of the statements made by the alien in support of the alien's claim and such other facts as are known to the officer, that the alien could establish eligibility for asylum."[57](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn57) Federal regulations define *credible fear of torture* as "a significant possibility that the alien is eligible for" protection under the CAT.[58](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn58) As discussed in this report, depending on the outcome of the credible fear interview, the applicant may potentially apply for asylum and related protections in standard removal proceedings.

### How does DHS determine whether to put someone in standard removal proceedings or expedited removal?

As described previously (see "[What is expedited removal?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164244977)"), expedited removal has previously been applied mainly to aliens who have recently arrived at U.S. ports of entry or have been apprehended at U.S. land borders and are found to be inadmissible because they lack valid entry documents or procured or sought to procure admission through fraud or misrepresentation.[59](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn59) Section 1225(b)(1) also authorizes (but does not require) the expedited removal process for other aliens physically present in the country for less than two years if they have not been admitted or paroled and are inadmissible on the same grounds.[60](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn60) Citing this authority, DHS in 2025 expanded the scope of expedited removal to cover individuals present in the United States less than two years who meet the criteria for expedited removal (see the "[What is expedited removal?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164244977)" section).

If an immigration officer determines that an individual is inadmissible on certain other grounds, such as having engaged in specified criminal activity, he or she must be detained and processed for standard removal.[61](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn61) In other situations, DHS has discretion to place certain aliens otherwise subject to expedited removal directly into the standard removal process by issuing NTAs (see "[What is standard removal?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164088948)") and release them from custody.[62](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn62) For example, in general, DHS may process for standard removal and release individuals who pose no threat to safety or national security and are low flight risks. In the recent past, this has happened amid operational constraints at the border—for instance, lack of capacity in CBP holding facilities for migrants to await credible fear screenings.[63](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn63)

Under the second Trump Administration, expedited removal has been prioritized when applicable. In February 2025, then-ICE Acting Director Caleb Vitello, whose officers and agents are responsible for interior enforcement, issued the following guidance concerning the agency's expansion of expedited removal in the interior of the United States:

ICE immigration officers will apply [expedited removal] to aliens who cannot establish to the satisfaction of the immigration officer that they were physically present in the United States continuously for the two-year period immediately prior to the date of the determination of inadmissibility, and who otherwise fall within the legal parameters of the expedited removal designation.[64](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn64)

### Can DHS move someone from standard removal proceedings to expedited removal?

An immigration judge may order dismissal when DHS files a motion to dismiss under certain grounds, including, among others, when "[c]ircumstances of the case have changed after the notice to appear was issued to such an extent that continuation is no longer in the best interest of the government."[65](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn65) In February 2025, Vitello issued guidance to ICE employees stating that, under DHS's expansion of expedited removal,[66](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn66) "ICE personnel will take all necessary steps to review the alien's case and consider, in exercising your enforcement discretion, whether to apply expedited removal. This may include steps to terminate any ongoing removal proceedings," citing the changed circumstances ground.[67](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn67)

Subsequently, reporters, advocates, and other observers have documented instances of individuals' (including asylum applicants') removal proceeding being dismissed, after which they were detained by ICE and placed into expedited removal.[68](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn68)

## Credible Fear Screening Process

### Has credible fear screening always been part of the asylum process for migrants arriving at U.S. borders?

Asylum regulations and statutory provisions did not originally include a credible fear screening process. In the 1970s, the former Immigration and Naturalization Service (INS)—the DOJ agency primarily responsible for administering and enforcing immigration laws prior to the creation of DHS—issued regulations that established procedures for applying for asylum in the United States and for adjudicating asylum applications. In 1980, with the enactment of the Refugee Act of 1980, a three-paragraph asylum section was added to the INA.[69](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn69) INS asylum regulations, which were revised between 1980 and 1994, continued to set forth procedures.[70](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn70) It was not until enactment of the Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act of 1996 (P.L. 104-208, Division C, §302(a)) that expedited removal and credible fear screening became features of the asylum system.[71](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn71)

### What is the process for a credible fear interview?

#### Who conducts credible fear interviews?

USCIS asylum officers conduct credible fear interviews. An asylum officer is an immigration officer who "has had professional training in country conditions, asylum law, and interview techniques comparable to that provided to full-time adjudicators of [asylum] applications."[72](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn72) The asylum officer is "supervised by an officer" who has the same training and qualifications and "has had substantial experience adjudicating asylum applications."[73](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn73)

#### What rights are available to applicants who are referred for credible fear interviews?

The asylum officer is required to explain to the applicant the credible fear interview process, the right to consultation before the interview, the right to request a review of the asylum officer's determination, and the consequences of failing to show a credible fear of persecution or torture.[74](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn74) If the applicant cannot proceed with the interview in English, the asylum officer is required to obtain the assistance of an interpreter.[75](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn75) The asylum officer must also create a summary of the material facts as stated by the applicant and, at the end of the interview, review the summary with the applicant and provide him or her with an opportunity to correct any errors in it.[76](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn76)

An applicant may consult any person before a credible fear interview.[77](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn77) "Such consultation shall be at no expense to the Government and shall not unreasonably delay the process."[78](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn78) The consulted person may be present at the interview and permitted, at the discretion of the asylum officer, to offer a statement at the end of the interview.[79](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn79) The applicant may also present evidence during the interview.[80](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn80)

#### Are applicants detained pending the outcome of their credible fear screenings?

An alien "shall be detained" pending a determination of whether that person has a credible fear of persecution or torture.[81](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn81) ICE is responsible for immigration detention during this process. Although detention is generally mandatory, immigration officials, at their discretion, have statutory authority to parole applicants for admission into the United States temporarily "on a case-by-case basis for urgent humanitarian reasons or significant public benefit."[82](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn82) Under DHS regulations, the agency may parole aliens who have been placed in expedited removal proceedings, including those who are being detained pending a credible fear interview, if they do not present security or flight risks and fall within certain categories (e.g., those who have serious medical conditions, those who will be witnesses in judicial proceedings, or those "whose continued detention is not in the public interest").[83](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn83)

In July 2025, ICE issued new interim guidance[84](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn84) directing ICE employees to consider all unlawfully present aliens in the United States who have not been admitted—regardless of how long they have resided in the country—in addition to those arriving in the United States without authorization, as "applicants for admission" and therefore requiring that these individuals be detained for the duration of their removal proceedings.[85](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn85) Under this guidance, these individuals may not be released from custody unless they have been granted immigration parole and are ineligible for bond hearings before immigration judges. This policy is associated with a large increase in habeas corpus litigation filed in federal courts.[86](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn86)

### What is the difference between the requirement to establish credible fear and the requirement to establish reasonable fear?

Aliens placed in expedited removal proceedings who indicate an intention to apply for asylum or fear of returning to their country of origin are interviewed to determine whether they have a credible fear of persecution or torture.[87](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn87)

A separate streamlined procedure applies to the removal of aliens who have unlawfully reentered the United States after having been previously removed under orders of removal and, upon being apprehended by immigration officers, express a fear of persecution or torture if returned to their native countries.[88](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn88) Under this reinstatement of removal process, the alien's prior removal order is reinstated and may not be reopened or reviewed, and the alien is typically removed without a hearing or opportunity to pursue relief from removal.[89](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn89) An alien with a reinstated removal order who expresses a fear of returning to the country of removal is entitled to administrative review of that claim.[90](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn90) An asylum officer will interview the alien to determine whether he or she has a *reasonable fear* of persecution or torture.[91](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn91)

The reasonable fear standard is a higher standard of proof than the credible fear standard. In expedited removal proceedings, to establish a credible fear, a person must show a "significant possibility" of establishing eligibility for asylum, withholding of removal, or CAT protection.[92](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn92) To establish reasonable fear in reinstated removal proceedings, an alien must show a "reasonable possibility" that he or she would be persecuted or tortured in the country of removal.[93](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn93) The reasonable possibility standard is the same standard used to determine whether an applicant has shown a "well-founded fear" of persecution for the purpose of asylum.[94](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn94) By contrast, a credible fear determination is a "low screening standard"[95](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn95) that considers only whether the alien has shown a "substantial and realistic possibility of success on the merits" of an application for asylum and related protections.[96](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn96)

An alien with a reinstated removal order who shows a reasonable fear of persecution or torture is placed in proceedings before an immigration judge for consideration of the alien's eligibility for withholding of removal and CAT protection only.[97](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn97) The alien may not apply for asylum in those proceedings.[98](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn98) On the other hand, an alien in expedited removal proceedings who shows a credible fear of persecution or torture may apply for asylum as well as withholding of removal and CAT protection.[99](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn99) This distinction is significant because, unlike asylum, which provides an alien with the opportunity to pursue LPR status,[100](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn100) a grant of withholding of removal or CAT protection bars removal to the country only if the alien fears persecution or torture (but not necessarily to a third country) and affords no pathway to LPR status.[101](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn101)

### What are the recent executive branch actions related to asylum, and how might they impact the credible fear screening process?

The Biden Administration and second Trump Administration, in response to large numbers of enforcement encounters at the southwest border—many with individuals seeking asylum—implemented various restrictions on asylum eligibility.

In May 2023, DHS and DOJ issued a final rule, "Circumvention of Lawful Pathways," that applied to aliens without valid entry documents who entered the United States at "the southwest land border or adjacent coastal borders" from May 11, 2023, to May 11, 2025, after traveling through third countries (countries other than the United States or their countries of citizenship or nationality).[102](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn102) The rule made these aliens subject to a rebuttable presumption of asylum ineligibility unless certain exceptions applied. For individuals placed in expedited removal and referred for credible fear interviews, the rule required the asylum officer conducting the interview to determine whether the alien is covered by the rule and, if so, whether the alien had rebutted the presumption.[103](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn103) If the alien was determined to be ineligible for asylum, the asylum officer made a negative credible fear determination and would consider whether the alien could show eligibility for withholding of removal and protection from removal under the CAT.[104](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn104) The rule was subject to legal challenge and is currently not in effect.

In October 2024, DHS and DOJ promulgated a final rule, "Securing the Border," in conjunction with an associated presidential proclamation that makes aliens entering the United States across the southern border during "emergency border circumstances" ineligible for asylum unless certain exceptions apply.[105](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn105) Like the "Circumvention of Lawful Pathways" rule, this rule requires consideration of whether an alien is subject to this asylum limitation during a credible fear interview.[106](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn106) Under the "Securing the Border" rule, CBP officers do not affirmatively ask individuals whether they fear returning to their countries to determine whether they should be placed in credible fear proceedings. Instead, to be considered for a credible fear interview, the individual must affirmatively express either a fear of return or an intention to apply for asylum or protection from removal.[107](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn107) If the alien makes an affirmative statement and the asylum officer finds that the alien is subject to the rule's limitation, the asylum officer will automatically make a negative credible fear determination and will, instead, consider whether the alien can show eligibility for withholding of removal and CAT protection.[108](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn108) The rule was subject to a legal challenge and is not currently in effect.[109](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn109)

Access to asylum was further restricted under a January 2025 presidential proclamation, "Guaranteeing the States Protection Against Invasion."[110](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn110) The proclamation states that "aliens engaged in the invasion across the southern border of the United States" on or after the date of the proclamation may not, among other things, apply for asylum until the President issues "a finding that the invasion at the southern border has ceased."[111](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn111) It also makes ineligible for asylum "any alien who fails, before entering the United States, to provide Federal officials with sufficient medical information and reliable criminal history and background information."[112](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn112) Those who are subject to the proclamation may be screened for CAT protection if they manifest a fear of torture in the countries of removal, and such screening is conducted under a heightened standard with no administrative review of the immigration officers' determinations.[113](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn113) The proclamation and its implementing guidance were subject to legal challenge.[114](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn114) As of the date of this report, DHS may enforce the restrictions on asylum under the proclamation while litigation continues but may not immediately repatriate aliens subject to the proclamation who are already present in the United States without complying with statutorily mandated removal procedures. It also may not restrict the ability of aliens subject to the proclamation from pursuing withholding of removal or CAT protection.[115](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn115)

Additionally, in May 2024, USCIS proposed a rule that would allow asylum officers to consider whether certain mandatory statutory bars to eligibility for asylum or withholding of removal (e.g., those applicable to persons who have been convicted of particularly serious crimes or have engaged in terrorist activity) apply in determining whether an individual can show a credible fear of persecution and in reasonable fear screenings (e.g., for reinstatement of removal).[116](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn116) Under this rule, if there is evidence that a mandatory bar could apply, the asylum officer would be permitted to issue a negative credible fear finding if the alien fails to show that there is a significant possibility that he or she would be unable to prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the mandatory bar does not apply and if the alien is otherwise unable to show a credible fear of torture.[117](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn117) DHS finalized the rule, without substantive changes, in December 2024, with an effective date of January 17, 2025.[118](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn118)

## Credible Fear Outcomes

### What are the possible outcomes of a credible fear screening?

If an asylum officer finds that an alien has a credible fear of persecution or torture, that individual is typically placed in standard removal proceedings (see "[What is standard removal?](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164088496)").[119](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn119)

When an asylum officer finds that an alien does not have a credible fear of persecution or torture, the alien may request that an immigration judge review that determination.[120](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn120) The immigration judge's review "shall be concluded as expeditiously as possible, to the maximum extent practicable within 24 hours, but in no case later than 7 days" after the asylum officer's decision.[121](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn121) If the alien declines further review, the asylum officer must order the alien removed from the United States without further hearing or review.[122](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn122)

If the immigration judge concurs with the asylum officer's negative credible fear finding, the applicant is to be removed from the United States, and the immigration judge's decision may not be appealed.[123](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn123)

Conversely, if the immigration judge determines that the alien has a credible fear of persecution or torture, the immigration judge must vacate the asylum officer's negative credible fear determination, and the alien may be placed in standard removal proceedings.[124](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn124) During those proceedings, the alien may pursue asylum, withholding of removal, and CAT protection.[125](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn125)

### Can asylum seekers withdraw their applications for admission during the credible fear screening process?

DHS may permit an individual, including someone placed in expedited removal, to voluntarily withdraw an application for admission if he or she intends (and is able) to depart the United States immediately.[126](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn126) An alien does not have a right to withdraw his or her application for admission. Instead, it is up to the discretion of the agency whether to permit the alien to withdraw the application and immediately leave the United States in lieu of undergoing removal proceedings.[127](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn127)

### Do asylum seekers automatically receive work authorization once they pass their credible fear screenings?

By law, an asylum applicant who is not otherwise eligible for employment authorization cannot be granted such authorization until at least 180 days have passed since the filing of a completed asylum application.[129](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn129) Regulations further provide that the applicant must wait 150 days after the receipt of a completed asylum application to apply for employment authorization, leaving a 30-day period for application processing.[130](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn130) These restrictions do not apply to asylum applicants who are authorized to work under other immigration categories, such as immigration parole or Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA).[131](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn131)

An asylum seeker granted work authorization through this application process receives an Employment Authorization Document (EAD). As of December 2025, the maximum validity period for an initial or renewal EAD issued to an asylum applicant is 18 months.[132](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn132)

### How many asylum applicants fail to appear for their immigration court hearings?

Under the law, individuals who fail to appear for their immigration court hearings must be ordered removed in absentia*.*[133](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#fn133)Therefore, the number of in absentia removal orders issued by immigration judges may be used as a measure of the number of individuals who fail to appear for their hearings.

EOIR publishes the annual number of in absentia removal orders for proceedings with filed asylum applications (see [**Table 3**](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-28_R48078_b24eddab1a97f029521f32e241692ef382c749e6.html#_Ref164086625)). The annual number of in absentia removal orders has increased substantially since FY2021, likely reflecting the large number of migrants who entered the country in recent years, many seeking asylum. In the first three quarters of FY2026, immigration judges issued 90,288 in absentia removal orders for asylum applicants, the largest number since at least FY2008 (earliest available data).
