# Memorial Day Speech Resources: Fact Sheet

Updated May 12, 2026
(R43973)

## Introduction

Memorial Day is a day of reflection and remembrance, [established](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title36/pdf/USCODE-2024-title36-subtitleI-partA-chap1-sec116.pdf) "to pay tribute to individuals who have made the ultimate sacrifice in service to the United States and their families."

Memorial Day also symbolically marks the beginning of summer, because it coincides with the end of the school year in many states.

This guide is designed to assist congressional offices with work related to celebrating Memorial Day.

## History

Memorial Day, originally called Decoration Day, began as a day for honoring troops who died during the Civil War. The date and location of the first local observance is disputed.[1](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-05-12_R43973_8f56d414e1365d61e24648850bbfcac323e2e055.html#fn1) On May 30, 1868, the Grand Army of the Republic, an organization of Union Civil War veterans, called for the [first national observance](https://www.cem.va.gov/cem/history/memdayorder.asp) by its members. In 1888, Congress passed [an act](http://uscode.house.gov/statviewer.htm?volume=25&page=165) making May 30 a holiday in the District of Columbia. Memorial Day is now officially observed on the last Monday in May, as prescribed in the 1968 Uniform Monday Holiday Act.

Many Americans observe Memorial Day by visiting cemeteries or memorials and placing American flags or wreaths in memory of those who have died in war. They may also participate in or attend Memorial Day parades.

Part of the Memorial Day observance is the [National Moment of Remembrance](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-106publ579/pdf/PLAW-106publ579.pdf). This practice encourages all Americans to pause wherever they are at 3:00 p.m. local time for a moment of silence to remember and honor those who died in service to the nation.

In many communities, citizens assist veterans with the placement of flags on gravestones in cemeteries and distribute flags to people participating in parades. The American flag may also be flown or hung outdoors at an individual's home.

On Memorial Day, as on Veterans Day, artificial poppies are purchased, and the donations are used to help veterans and their widows, widowers, and children.

## Government Resources

### Library of Congress

* Library of Congress, "[Today in History—May 30](https://www.loc.gov/item/today-in-history/may-30/)." Find more historical resources on the [Library of Congress website](https://www.loc.gov/search/?fa=original-format:web+page&q=%22memorial+day%22).
* Library of Congress American Folklife Center, "[Veterans History Project](https://www.loc.gov/programs/veterans-history-project/about-this-program/)." In 2016, the Gold Star Families Voice Act (P.L. 114-246) expanded the original scope of the Veterans History Project to include oral histories by immediate family members (parent, spouse, sibling, or child) of "members of the Armed Forces who died as a result of their service during a period of war."

### Other Resources

Many other federal agencies provide resources on the history and observation of Memorial Day, including the following:

* Arlington National Cemetery, "[Wreath Layings](https://www.arlingtoncemetery.mil/Visit/Ceremonies/Wreath-Layings)."
* Arlington National Cemetery, "[Request a Public Wreath Ceremony](http://www.arlingtoncemetery.mil/Visit/Events-and-Ceremonies/Request-a-Ceremony)."
* Defense Manpower Data Center, [Defense Casualty Analysis System](https://dcas.dmdc.osd.mil/dcas/app/home). Provides statistics on active-duty military deaths as well as detailed historical tables.
* National Archives and Records Administration, "[Memorial Day: A Commemoration](https://www.archives.gov/news/topics/memorial-day)."
* National Park Service, "[NPS Commemorations and Celebrations: Memorial Day](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/npscelebrates/memorial-day.htm)."
* U.S. Army Center of Military History, "[Memorial Day](https://history.army.mil/Research/Reference-Topics/Memorial-Day/)."
* U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, "[Memorial Day](https://www.va.gov/opa/speceven/memday/)." Information on veterans' cemeteries and flag protocol.
* U.S. Census Bureau, [Census Bureau Data](https://data.census.gov/). Tracks statistics about veterans. The Census Bureau also provides [information](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/veterans.html) and [visualizations](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/veterans/library/visualizations.All.html) that may be useful to include in speeches.
