The Paris Agreement (PA) and Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)

The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) has been the foundational multilateral treaty for cooperation among nations on greenhouse gas (GHG)-induced climate change since its adoption in 1992.[1](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn1 "United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) , New York, May 9, 1992 , U nited Nations (U .N. ) Treaty Collection, Chapter XXVII Environment, 7 ( S. Treaty Doc. No. 102-38 ).") The Paris Agreement (PA) is a multilateral treaty in pursuit of the UNFCCC's objective and guided by its principles.[2](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn2 "Paris Agreement to the U NFCCC , Paris, December 12, 2015 , U.N. Treaty Collection, Chapter XXVII Environment, 7.d (U.S. Department of State, T.I.A.S. No. 16-1104 ). Hereinafter referred to as UNFCCC, Paris Agreement.") Out of 198 Parties to the UNFCCC, 194 are Parties to the PA as of November 9, 2026.[3](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn3 "Iran, Libya, the United States, and Yemen are Signatories but not Parties to the PA.") The United States is no longer a Party to the PA, as of January 3, 2026.[4](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn4 "\"Paris Agreement — Status of Ratification,\" UNFCCC, https://unfccc.int/process/the-paris-agreement/status-of-ratification . On January 27, 2025, the United States notified the Secretary-General of the decision to withdraw from the PA, which took effect on January 27, 2026. (See the status of the PA at the United Nations Treaty Collection, https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XXVII-7-d&chapter=27&clang=_en#7 . )") For more information about the United States withdrawal from the PA, see CRS Report R48504, [*U.S. Withdrawal from the Paris Agreement: Process and Potential Effects*](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/R48504), by Jonathan D. Haskett.

The PA defines a collective, long-term objective of "holding the increase in the global average temperature to well below 2℃ [Celsius] above pre-industrial levels and pursuing efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5℃ above pre-industrial levels."[5](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn5 "UNFCCC, Paris Agreement, Article 2, para 1(a).") In pursuit of that objective, the PA requires all parties, regardless of "developed" or "developing" country status, to maintain *nationally determined contributions* (NDCs) that specify by how much a party plans to reduce their GHG emissions, among other things.[6](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn6 "UNFCCC, Paris Agreement, Article 3.")

NDCs are the primary vehicle through which Parties to the PA communicate their intended contributions to achieving countries' collective temperature and climate goals. NDCs reflect each country's own national climate objectives and plans, including emission reduction goals, adaptation plans, and other elements. Under the terms of the PA, all NDCs must address GHG emissions reductions and all NDCs should list economy-wide absolute GHG emission reduction targets, with flexibility for developing countries to move over time toward such targets.[7](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn7 "UNFCCC, Paris Agreement, Article 4, para 4.") NDCs are updated every five years. Each NDC update is a progression that reflects the "highest possible ambition" in light of each country's particular circumstances.[8](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn8 "UNFCCC, Paris Agreement, Article 4, para 3.")

In advance of the adoption of the PA at COP21 in Paris, Parties were invited to communicate their intended NDC (INDC).[9](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn9 "UNFCCC, Decision 1/CP.19 , para 2.") Upon a Party's ratification of the PA, the Party's INDC became their First NDC. Thus, each Party has submitted at least one NDC.[10](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn10 "UNFCCC, Decision 1/CP.21, para 22.") Thereafter, Parties are required to communicate their NDC every five years. The most recent NDCs were due in 2025 and cover the period to 2035.[11](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn11 "UNFCCC, Paris Agreement, Article 4, para 9; \"Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs): The Paris Agreement and NDCs ,\" UNFCCC, https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement/nationally-determined-contributions-ndcs .") Subsequent NDCs are to reflect a progression of contributions to collectively over time increase *ambition*.[12](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn12 "Article 4 of the Paris Agreement uses the term \"ambition\" to describe countries' emission reduction and adaptation plans. The use of \"ambition \" throughout this report reflects this language and does not imply judgment by CRS.") Article 4 of the PA states that,

Each Party's successive nationally determined contribution will represent a progression beyond the Party's then current nationally determined contribution and reflect its highest possible ambition, reflecting its common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, in the light of different national circumstances.[13](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn13 "UNFCCC, Paris Agreement, Article 4, para 3.")

Parties decided that those who submitted their first NDCs without quantifiable 2030 emission targets were required to submit new (Second) NDCs by 2020.[14](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn14 "UNFCCC, Decision 4/CMA.1 , paras 7 , 9.") Others were asked to submit updated versions of their First NDCs by 2020 that reflect increasing ambition. The majority of countries met this deadline in advance of COP26.[15](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn15 "COP26 was scheduled to be held in 2020 and was postponed until 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic.") According to the UN Climate Change Secretariat (Secretariat), all 194 Parties to the PA have submitted First, updated First, Second, or Third NDCs.[16](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn16 "\"NDC Registry,\" UNFCCC, https://unfccc.int/NDCREG .")

Conditional NDCs

When preparing NDCs, some countries specify conditions which reflect the country's domestic context and capabilities that would allow the implementation of certain measures.[17](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn17 "UNFCCC, Decision 4/CMA.1.") Parties to the PA recognized that countries have different capacities to mitigate GHG emissions, by stating that "enhanced support for developing country Parties will allow for higher ambition in their actions."[18](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn18 "UNFCCC, Paris Agreement, Article 4, para 5 . Note that the PA does not define \"developing country\" and does not set qualifications for which parties can submit conditional NDCs and which cannot .") Many countries requesting international support include two sets of targets: targets unconditional on international support, and more ambitious targets conditional on international support (financial, technical, or other support). Some countries combine these targets while others only include conditional GHG mitigation targets, indicating that without international support, goals to reduce GHG emissions will not be met.

[**Table 1**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#_Ref179991906) lists every Party with an NDC target conditional on international support. As of November 9, 2026, 129 of 194 Parties had submitted NDCs conditional on international support. NDC targets are extracted from countries' NDC submissions found in the UNFCCC's NDC Registry.[19](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn19 "\"NDC Registry,\" UNFCCC, https://unfccc.int/NDCREG .") [**Table 1**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#_Ref179991906) reflects the most recent submissions found in the NDC Registry. Thus, some of the NDC targets are from a Party's first or second NDC and have not been updated by the Party. To provide consistency throughout [**Table 1**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#_Ref179991906), the language used to describe the NDC targets may reflect slight modifications to the language used in the NDCs themselves.

Some countries' submissions may include targets for several years. However, in [**Table 1**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#_Ref179991906), the Congressional Research Service (CRS) reflects only the target for the period required by the pertinent NDC. For example, a country may include 2030, 2035, and 2050 targets in their 2025 NDC. However, CRS only listed the 2035 target in [**Table 1**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#_Ref179991906) as that is the period that the 2025 NDCs are to cover. Not all NDC submissions include quantifiable targets, and not all quantifiable targets encompass economy-wide reductions or include all greenhouse gases. Where applicable, the table notes these differences. Additional information on and analysis of NDCs can be found in CRS Report R46945, [*Greenhouse Gas Emission Reduction Pledges by Selected Countries: Nationally Determined Contributions and Net-Zero Legislation*](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/R46945) and UNFCCC's 2025 NDC Synthesis Report.[20](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R48248.html#fn20 "\"2025 NDC Synthesis Report,\" UNFCCC, 2025, https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement/nationally-determined-contributions-ndcs/2025-ndc-synthesis-report .")
