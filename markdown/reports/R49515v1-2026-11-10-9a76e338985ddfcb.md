Summary

The U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) reports that from 2017 to 2024, foreign holdings of U.S. agricultural land increased an average of more than 2.4 million acres annually. Some Members of Congress have expressed concerns relating to national security about investments in U.S. agricultural land by foreign adversaries, according to 15 C.F.R. §791.4 (e.g., the People's Republic of China), particularly investments near sensitive military sites. These concerns would be addressed by various bills introduced in the 119th Congress, some of which would amend the Agricultural Foreign Investment Disclosure Act (AFIDA; [P.L. 95-460](https://www.congress.gov/law/95/pub/460); 7 U.S.C. §§3501 et seq.) or restrict foreign investment in U.S. agricultural land.

USDA reports that as of December 31, 2024, foreign persons held interests in over 47 million acres of U.S. agricultural land, which accounted for 3.6% of total privately owned agricultural land. Forestland accounted for 47% of all foreign-owned acres, cropland accounted for 29%, and other agricultural land accounted for 22%. Five countries—Canada, the Netherlands, Germany, Italy, and the United Kingdom—accounted for 61% of all foreign-owned acres. USDA reports that "Chinese-primary investor filers" accounted for approximately 0.5% of foreign holdings.

Current federal law imposes no restrictions on the amount of private U.S. agricultural land that can be foreign-owned. However, AFIDA requires foreign persons who acquire or transfer interest in agricultural land to submit a report to USDA within 90 days of the transaction. Persons who fail to report may be subject to a civil penalty.

A 2024 Government Accountability Office report identified gaps among AFIDA data collection and information sharing with the interagency Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS). The report was initiated by some Members identifying foreign investments near U.S. military bases and purchases that could lead to foreign control of supply chains. In June 2026, USDA released a proposed rule to amend the AFIDA regulations. The rule proposes changes to define *agricultural land*to includeland used for animal slaughtering and processing and solar and wind electric power generation; a requirement to include digital, open-source geospatial map and property boundaries and a legal description of the land in reporting; and amendments to civil penalties based on transaction and whether the foreign person is associated with a foreign adversary.

In the 119th Congress, some Members have introduced legislation that would amend AFIDA, increase and improve data collection on foreign ownership, or restrict foreign ownership associated with foreign adversaries. Two of these proposals are the House-passed farm bill, the Farm, Food and National Security Act of 2026 ([H.R. 7567](http://www.congress.gov/bill/119/hr/7567)), and the Senate committee-reported Agricultural Act of 2026 ([S. 5526](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119/s/5526)). The House-passed bill would amend AFIDA disclosure requirements, amend civil penalties for AFIDA violations, restrict certain foreign ownership of agricultural land, and alter the relationship between USDA and CFIUS. [S. 5526](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119/s/5526) proposes amendments to AFIDA reporting requirements and the civil penalty structure.

Many states and localities have introduced proposals or enacted laws to restrict foreign ownership of agricultural land or require reporting similar to what is required under AFIDA, but there is no uniform approach across such legislation. For example, some local laws restrict the amount of land that can be owned by foreign persons or the duration of ownership, and some differ in their definition of agricultural land.

Congress, the executive branch, and state and local governments continue to deliberate foreign ownership of U.S. agricultural land and whether to amend related laws. Congress may consider whether issues or concerns relating to foreign ownership may require amendments to AFIDA or changes in interagency collaboration between USDA and CFIUS.

Existing Federal Reporting Requirements

Federal law imposes no restrictions on the amount of private U.S. agricultural land that can be foreign-owned but requires foreign persons to disclose transactions, investments, and ownership of agricultural land to USDA. Federal reporting requirements are authorized under AFIDA, and USDA regulations specify how AFIDA is to be implemented.[16](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn16 "Regulations for the Agricultural Foreign Investment Disclosure Act of 1978 (AFIDA; P.L. 95-460 ; 7 U.S.C. §§3501-3508) can be found in 7 C.F.R. §§ 781 .1 -781.6 .") The Farm Service Agency (FSA) also maintains an AFIDA handbook that provides guidance to state and county offices for administering AFIDA.[17](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn17 "USDA, FSA, FSA Handbook : Foreign Investment Disclosure for State and County Offices , 1-AFIDA (Revision 2), https://www.fsa.usda.gov/Internet/FSA_File/1-afida_r02_a02.pdf (hereinafter FSA Handbook: Foreign Investment Disclosure ) .") For example, the handbook includes information on how FSA offices are to handle incomplete AFIDA forms and check for errors in forms.

AFIDA establishes a nationwide set of requirements for the collection of information on foreign purchasers of U.S. agricultural land. AFIDA requires any foreign person who acquires or transfers interest, other than a security interest (e.g., a mortgage- or debt-securing interest), in U.S. agricultural land to submit a report to USDA within 90 days of the acquisition or transfer.[18](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn18 "7 U.S.C. §350 1 ( a ).")

AFIDA defines *agricultural land* as land that is used for agricultural, forestry, or timber production.[19](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn19 "7 U.S.C. §3508(1) .") The AFIDA regulations further define agricultural land to include land that was used for farming, ranching, or timber production within the last five years.[20](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn20 "7 C.F.R. §781.2(b).") An exception is made for land that does not exceed 10 acres if the gross sale receipts from the land are less than $1,000 annually. Land used for forestry production includes land exceeding 10 acres in which 10% is stocked by trees of any size, including land that formerly had this level of tree cover that can be naturally or artificially regenerated.[21](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn21 "7 C.F.R. §781.2(b).") The regulations reference the Standard Industrial Classification Manual (SIC manual), Division A, for activities that are generally considered farming, ranching, or timber production.[22](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn22 "A search of classification codes for agriculture can be found at Occupational Safety and Health Administration, \"Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) System Search,\" https://www.osha.gov/data/sic-search?field_sic_number_value=&title_and_body=agriculture .")

The definition of a *foreign person* broadlyincludes any foreign government or individual, corporation, company, association, partnership, society, joint stock company, trust, estate, or other legal entity created or organized under the laws of a foreign government or a principal place of business outside the United States.[23](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn23 "7 U.S.C. §3508(3) and 7 C.F.R. §781.2(g).") In addition, the AFIDA regulations state the level of interest or control that would qualify an individual, government, or business entity as a foreign person under AFIDA and require reporting on additional ownership tiers. "Significant interest or substantial control"[24](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn24 "7 U.S.C. §781.2(k).") under AFIDA is defined to include

"an interest of 10 percent or more held by [any single foreign individual, person, government, or legal entity]";

"an interest of 10 percent held by [a combination of foreign individuals, persons, government, or legal entity that are acting in concert with one another] even though no single individual, person, or government [or legal entity] holds an interest of 10 percent or more"; or

"an interest of 50 percent or more, in the aggregate, held by [foreign individuals, persons, governments, or entities even if they] may not be acting in concert."[25](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn25 "7 U.S.C. §781.2(k).")

The AFIDA handbook states that the first three tiers of ownership must report on form FSA-153.[26](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn26 "FSA Handbook : Foreign Investment Disclosure .") The handbook describes ownership tiers as follows:

* 1st Tier—"the entity, company, individual, or country having title to the land";
* 2nd Tier—"the entity, company, individual, or country owning an interest in the entity, individual, company, or country having title to the land" and having "significant interest or substantial control in the title holder of the land"; and
* 3rd Tier—"the entity, company, individual, or country owning an interest in the 2nd tier" and having "significant interest or substantial control in the 2nd tier."

AFIDA also requires reporting on transactions of individuals who become a foreign person following their acquisition of an interest in U.S. agricultural land and requires that a foreign person who converts land to agricultural uses submit a report to USDA within 90 days of the land conversion.[27](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn27 "7 U.S.C. §3501 .") Reports must also be submitted within 90 days in cases where agricultural land ceases being agricultural, such as when the land is transitioned to construct a residential neighborhood.[28](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn28 "7 C.F.R. §781.3(h).") AFIDA regulations provide reporting exemptions for certain transactions. These exemptions, specified under the term *any interest*, include "leaseholds of less than 10 years," "surface or subsurface easements and rights of way used for purposes unrelated to agricultural production," and interests "solely in mineral rights."[29](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn29 "7 C.F.R. §781.2(c).")

Filers may submit AFIDA information via an online portal or form FSA-153.[30](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn30 "AFIDA reports can be submitted at USDA, FSA, \"Agricultural Foreign Investment Disclosure Act (AFIDA) , \" https://www.fsa.usda.gov/resources/economic-policy-analysis/afida . Reports can also be submitted through the hard copy form FSA-153.") The report is to contain information such as the legal name, address, and citizenship of the foreign person, the legal description and acreage of the agricultural land, the purchase price of the land, and the intended use of the land. In the case that the foreign person is not an individual or government, the person (e.g., corporation or similar entity) must disclose its nature, country of establishment, and principal place of business.[31](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn31 "7 U.S.C. §3501 and 7 C.F.R. §781.3.")

Persons that fail to comply with AFIDA reporting requirements may be subject to a civil penalty.[32](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn32 "7 U.S.C. §3502 and 7 C.F.R. §781.4.") Penalties may also be incurred for a late filing or the inclusion of false information on an AFIDA filing. The penalty amount is not to exceed 25% of the fair market value of the land on the date the penalty is assessed. AFIDA also gives USDA discretion in taking investigative actions to monitor compliance and verify that information submitted in reports is accurate and fully reveals the ownership of the land.[33](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn33 "7 U.S.C. §3503.")

USDA compiles and publicly reports data submitted under AFIDA annually.[34](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn34 "A compilation of annual AFIDA reports can be found at USDA, FSA, \"Annual Reports,\" https://www.fsa.usda.gov/resources/economic-policy-analysis/afida/annual-reports .") The most recent report covers 2024; as of October 14, 2026, USDA has not published one for 2025. Congress has directed USDA, in appropriations laws enacted in the 117th ([P.L. 117-328](http://www.congress.gov/law/117/pub/328); codified in 7 U.S.C. §3501 note) and 118th ([P.L. 118-42](http://www.congress.gov/law/118/pub/42); codified in 7 U.S.C. §3501 note) Congresses, to report on foreign investments in agricultural land in the United States and the impact that foreign ownership has on U.S. family farms and rural communities and the domestic food supply. Congress also directed USDA to establish a streamlined process for electronic submission of disclosures under AFIDA and an internet database that contains disaggregated data from the disclosures submitted within two years of the enactment of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2024 ([P.L. 118-42](http://www.congress.gov/law/118/pub/42)). USDA announced the launch of the electronic disclosure submission portal on January 22, 2026.[35](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn35 "A link to online filing can be found at USDA, FSA, \"Agricultural Foreign Investment Disclosure Act (AFIDA) . \"")

Developments in AFIDA

Certain Members of Congress have requested that the Government Accountability Office (GAO) conduct a review of AFIDA regarding foreign investments near U.S. military bases and land purchases that could lead to foreign control of U.S. food supply chains.[36](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn36 "GAO, Foreign Investments in U.S. Agricultural Land , GAO-24-106337 .") GAO published the requested report in 2024 and in it identified issues with USDA's data collection and reporting with respect to foreign investments in farmland. GAO recommended that USDA reform the collection and sharing of AFIDA data; use AFIDA data to inform the decisionmaking of the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS); improve the quality of information gathered in AFIDA forms; sign memoranda of understanding (MOUs) with the U.S. Department of the Treasury (Treasury) and the U.S. Department of Defense (which is "using a secondary Department of War designation" under Executive Order 14347 of September 5, 2025) to share AFIDA transaction information; update the AFIDA handbook; develop an online submission system; develop ways to verify information received in AFIDA reporting and identify non-filers; and develop methods to capture information beyond the primary investor.[37](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn37 "For more on the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) , see CRS In Focus IF10177, Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) . For more on the relationship between CFIUS and USDA , see CRS In Focus IF13255, Foreign Investment in U.S. Agricultural Land: Current Issues .") USDA has since moved to implement several of the GAO recommendations (see "[USDA AFIDA Proposed Rule](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#_Ref240461694)").

In late 2025, the Trump Administration released its *National Farm Security Action Plan*.[38](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn38 "USDA, Farm Security Is National Security: National Farm Security Action Plan , https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/documents/farm-security-nat-sec.pdf .") The document laid out the Administration's plans to work alongside other agencies and state, local, tribal, and territorial governments on potential national security issues in agriculture. Among the stated goals was to "secure and protect American farmland." In the plan, the Administration identified that farmland owned by foreign nationals, particularly those from "countries of concern or other foreign adversaries," is a "potential threat to national security and future economic prosperity."[39](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn39 "USDA cites 15 C.F.R. § 231.102 to define a foreign country of concern .") The plan states that the Administration will implement AFIDA reforms, work with Congress and state governments to pass legislation impacting foreign land ownership, sign an MOU with Treasury and CFIUS to ensure coordination on certain transactions relating to agriculture and farmland, and launch a database for the submission of potential violations of AFIDA.[40](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn40 "For more information about CFIUS , see CRS In Focus IF10177, Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) .")

USDA AFIDA Proposed Rule

On June 25, 2026, USDA released a proposed rule to update AFIDA regulations, with a public comment period through August 10, 2026. The rule proposes to transfer the administration of AFIDA from FSA to the USDA Office of Homeland Security (OHS).[41](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn41 "USDA, O HS , \" AFIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315-38334.") It would also amend the definitions of terms such as agricultural land,the reporting requirements under AFIDA, the assessment of civil penalties, and the review process for civil penalties. The rule would also create a process to appeal civil penalties through OHS.[42](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn42 "Current regulations allow for AFIDA appeals in accordance with the general regulations for FSA appeals (7 C.F.R. §780.4(a)(iv) ) . These regulations would not apply to OHS under the proposed rule .")

The proposed rule would expand the scope of agricultural land defined and covered under AFIDA. The proposed rule would base its classification of farming, ranching, forestry, or timber production activities on selected codes from the 2022 version of the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS), which is different from the current regulations that use the SIC manual.[43](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn43 "More on the North American Industry Classification System codes can be found at U.S. Census Bureau, \"North American Industry Classification System,\" https://www.census.gov/naics/?58967?yearbck=2022 .") Under the proposed rule, USDA would require reporting on the basis of selected NAICS codes that include a broader list of agricultural supply chain activities and different uses of agricultural land than are currently included under AFIDA regulations. For example, these new categories would include the following uses of land: animal slaughtering and processing; farm product warehousing and storage; solar and wind electric power generation; pipeline transportation; and research and development in physical, engineering, and life sciences and biotechnology.[44](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn44 "USDA, OHS, \" AFIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 38329-38 330.") The proposed rule would also require that foreign persons with leases longer than one year submit an AFIDA report; though if the lessee is associated with a foreign adversary country, they would be required to submit an AFIDA report regardless of the lease length. Under current regulations, foreign persons must submit an AFIDA report if their lease is 10 years or longer, and no distinction is made for country affiliation.[45](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn45 "Requirements for leases are defined under the term any interest in the AFIDA regulations ( 7 C.F.R. §781.2(c)(2) ) .")

The proposed rule would define new terms such as *beneficial owner* and *shell corporation**.*[46](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn46 "The proposed rule defines beneficial owner as a \" foreign person who , directly or indirectly … exercises decision-making authority over the agricultural land or legal entity holding the land \" (91 F ederal Register 38330) . The proposed rule defines a shell corporation as \" any company, partnership, trust, or legal entity that has no or nominal operations and is used to hold an interest in agricultural land \" (91 Federal Register 38330).") It would amend the definition of "significant interest or substantial control"to reduce the threshold of aggregate foreign interest to 10% and to include any interest by a foreign person that qualifies as a beneficial owner or is associated with a foreign adversary.[47](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn47 "USDA, OHS, \" AFIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 3832 3 .")

The proposed rule would also change some of the reporting requirements under AFIDA. It would require that all reports be submitted through the online database that USDA established following congressional direction in appropriations acts.[48](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn48 "7 U.S.C. §3501 note. A link to online filing can be found at USDA, FSA, \"Agricultural Foreign Investment Disclosure Act (AFIDA) . \"") Other proposed changes include requiring a digital, open-source geospatial map with property boundaries and the legal description of the agricultural land in reports.[49](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn49 "USDA, OHS, \" AFIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 38331 .") The proposal would also require filers to include certain identifiers, such as a tax identification number and foreign passport number.[50](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn50 "USDA, OHS, \"A FIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 38331.") Additionally, filers would be required to submit any information that is necessary for USDA to verify the accuracy of the report, as requested by USDA.

The rule, if finalized in its proposed form, would differentiate the civil penalty structure by the type of transaction and whether the foreign person is associated with a foreign adversary.[51](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn51 "USDA proposes a temporary penalty scheme for acquisitions and holdings that would sunset after one year in § 5100.4(b)(3) , which does not differentiate between whether the person is associated with a foreign adversary. The scheme would also increase the penalty at a time scale for land acquired by inheritance or transfer.") In the case of a late report for an acquisition or holding, the penalty would begin to be assessed on the 91st day following the transaction date.[52](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn52 "USDA, OHS, \"A FIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 38332.") The new civil penalty scheme would assess an initial penalty and then increase it if the payment were delayed.[53](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn53 "USDA, OHS, \"A FIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 3833 2-38333 .") The penalty would increase at a higher rate for foreign persons associated with a foreign adversary country than for those who are not.[54](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn54 "USDA, OHS, \"A FIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 38332-38333.") The rule would also provide a different civil penalty scheme for different types of transactions, such as the transfer of inherited land.[55](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn55 "USDA, OHS, \"AFIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 38332-38333.") This would be a change from the current structure that assesses a penalty at 0.001% of the fair market value each week that the report is late regardless of transaction type or connection to any foreign country. The cap on penalties of 25% of the fair market value of the land would remain the same.[56](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn56 "USDA, OHS, \"AFIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 38332-38333.")

The proposed rule would also amend the penalty for reports that have mistakes, errors, or false information to be up to 25% of the fair market value at the discretion of USDA.[57](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn57 "USDA states that this proposed change is to align with the statutory language in 7 U.S.C. §3502(b) (91 Federal Register 38327) .") Current regulations state that a 25% penalty will be assessed in these cases.[58](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn58 "7 C.F.R. §781.4(b)(2).") The rule would also shorten the duration for which a penalty can be appealed from 60 days to 30 days and direct that all penalties are to be paid online.[59](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn59 "USDA, OHS, \"AFIDA of 1978,\" 91 Federal Register 38315, 38326.")

Discussion

The extent and implications of foreign ownership of agricultural land remain of interest to Congress, the Administration, and state and local governments. In the 119th Congress, both chambers of Congress have seen legislation that would amend foreign ownership disclosure requirements in AFIDA, including the House-passed farm bill ([H.R. 7567](http://www.congress.gov/bill/119/hr/7567)) and the Senate committee-reported farm bill ([S. 5526](http://www.congress.gov/bill/119/s/5526)). Some of these bills would also place restrictions on foreign ownership. In addition, the Trump Administration has proposed a new rule to amend the AFIDA regulations, and states continue to pass laws relating to foreign ownership.

Some approaches differ between legislative proposals in the 119th Congress and USDA's proposed rule. For example, USDA's proposed rule would require holders of leases longer than one year to submit an AFIDA report; the Senate committee-reported farm bill ([S. 5526](http://www.congress.gov/bill/119/s/5526)) would set the lease length at five years.[79](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn79 "As noted above, the rule would require foreign persons associated with foreign adversaries to submit an AFIDA report regardless of the length of the lease. Currently, \" leaseholds of less than 10 years \" are not required to submit a report (7 C.F.R. §781. 2(c)(2) ) .") Generally, the proposed rule would require AFIDA reporting from more entities engaged in other uses of land, more foreign persons and entities with interest in the land, and more types of transactions than the farm bill proposals. This is due in part to the proposed rule's expansion of the definition of agricultural land, changes to the definition of substantial control, and the lease length requirements. Both the proposed rule and the farm bill proposals include efforts to collect AFIDA reports in a digital database and amend the civil penalty amounts. These provisions may streamline the data collection process and provide an incentive for compliance with AFIDA. Other proposals may lead to more data on foreign ownership of U.S. agricultural land, such as by requiring geospatial data to be collected in AFIDA reporting. An increase in data could provide USDA with more information on the potential impacts of foreign investment on the agricultural economy. Additional reporting under AFIDA may also create administrative burdens for filers of AFIDA reports and potentially disincentivize foreign investment in agricultural land. Additionally, some legislation and provisions in the proposed rule differ in approach as to whether they would create new or additional reporting requirements for foreign ownership broadly or limit their scope to foreign adversaries.

These policy changes would have different effects. If a primary goal is to obtain additional reporting and data specifically regarding foreign adversaries, changes addressing foreign ownership broadly may create additional burdens on foreign investors from countries that are not considered foreign adversaries. Conversely, policy changes directed specifically at foreign adversaries may provide less data on foreign ownership in total, as other foreign persons would not be subject to the new provisions.

Federal law differs from some state laws regarding restrictions on foreign ownership of agricultural land. While amendments to AFIDA may provide more data on foreign ownership or more oversight from USDA on controversial agricultural land transactions, they may not quell all national security or food security concerns pertaining to certain foreign ownership issues, such as investment in agricultural land by persons associated with foreign adversaries.[80](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn80 "For more on some national security issues relating to foreign ownership , see CRS In Focus IF13255, Foreign Investment in U.S. Agricultural Land: Current Issues .") Resolving the national and food security issues associated with foreign agricultural land ownership may involve multiple agencies and government bodies, including CFIUS.[81](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn81 "For more on how CFIUS reviews transactions for national security risks , see CRS In Focus IF10177, Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) .") For example, as noted by GAO, AFIDA data are not regularly used in CFIUS reviews, in part because of a lack of information sharing between CFIUS and USDA.[82](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn82 "GAO, Foreign Investments in U.S. Agricultural Land , GAO-24-106337 .") Congress may consider proposals that include requirements for information sharing between USDA and CFIUS or codification of a specific role for USDA on CFIUS. Additionally, some stakeholders may favor placing prohibitions on foreign persons from certain foreign countries from purchasing agricultural land to address security concerns in a manner that goes beyond data reporting. Others may see no need for these restrictions, may identify or oppose the economic impacts of such policies on foreign persons or may assess the potential for negative effects on agricultural land prices. Some stakeholders may favor reducing the existing reporting requirements to lower administrative burdens or leaving the issue primarily to state governments.[83](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49515.html#fn83 "For more on legal considerations with state laws and foreign ownership , see CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11013, State Regulation of Foreign Ownership of U.S. Land: January 2023 to July 2024 .")
