The Paris Agreement (PA) and Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)

The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) has been the foundational multilateral treaty for cooperation among nations on greenhouse gas (GHG)-induced climate change since its adoption in 1992.1 The Paris Agreement (PA) is a multilateral treaty in pursuit of the UNFCCC's objective and guided by its principles.2 Out of 198 Parties to the UNFCCC, 194 are Parties to the PA as of November 9, 2026.3 The United States is no longer a Party to the PA, as of January 3, 2026.4 For more information about the United States withdrawal from the PA, see CRS Report R48504, U.S. Withdrawal from the Paris Agreement: Process and Potential Effects, by Jonathan D. Haskett.

The PA defines a collective, long-term objective of "holding the increase in the global average temperature to well below 2℃ [Celsius] above pre-industrial levels and pursuing efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5℃ above pre-industrial levels."5 In pursuit of that objective, the PA requires all parties, regardless of "developed" or "developing" country status, to maintain nationally determined contributions (NDCs) that specify by how much a party plans to reduce their GHG emissions, among other things.6

NDCs are the primary vehicle through which Parties to the PA communicate their intended contributions to achieving countries' collective temperature and climate goals. NDCs reflect each country's own national climate objectives and plans, including emission reduction goals, adaptation plans, and other elements. Under the terms of the PA, all NDCs must address GHG emissions reductions and all NDCs should list economy-wide absolute GHG emission reduction targets, with flexibility for developing countries to move over time toward such targets.7 NDCs are updated every five years. Each NDC update is a progression that reflects the "highest possible ambition" in light of each country's particular circumstances.8

In advance of the adoption of the PA at COP21 in Paris, Parties were invited to communicate their intended NDC (INDC).9 Upon a Party's ratification of the PA, the Party's INDC became their First NDC. Thus, each Party has submitted at least one NDC.10 Thereafter, Parties are required to communicate their NDC every five years. The most recent NDCs were due in 2025 and cover the period to 2035.11 Subsequent NDCs are to reflect a progression of contributions to collectively over time increase ambition.12 Article 4 of the PA states that,

Each Party's successive nationally determined contribution will represent a progression beyond the Party's then current nationally determined contribution and reflect its highest possible ambition, reflecting its common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, in the light of different national circumstances.13

Parties decided that those who submitted their first NDCs without quantifiable 2030 emission targets were required to submit new (Second) NDCs by 2020.14 Others were asked to submit updated versions of their First NDCs by 2020 that reflect increasing ambition. The majority of countries met this deadline in advance of COP26.15 According to the UN Climate Change Secretariat (Secretariat), all 194 Parties to the PA have submitted First, updated First, Second, or Third NDCs.16

Conditional NDCs

When preparing NDCs, some countries specify conditions which reflect the country's domestic context and capabilities that would allow the implementation of certain measures.17 Parties to the PA recognized that countries have different capacities to mitigate GHG emissions, by stating that "enhanced support for developing country Parties will allow for higher ambition in their actions."18 Many countries requesting international support include two sets of targets: targets unconditional on international support, and more ambitious targets conditional on international support (financial, technical, or other support). Some countries combine these targets while others only include conditional GHG mitigation targets, indicating that without international support, goals to reduce GHG emissions will not be met.

Table 1 lists every Party with an NDC target conditional on international support. As of November 9, 2026, 129 of 194 Parties had submitted NDCs conditional on international support. NDC targets are extracted from countries' NDC submissions found in the UNFCCC's NDC Registry.19 Table 1 reflects the most recent submissions found in the NDC Registry. Thus, some of the NDC targets are from a Party's first or second NDC and have not been updated by the Party. To provide consistency throughout Table 1, the language used to describe the NDC targets may reflect slight modifications to the language used in the NDCs themselves.

Some countries' submissions may include targets for several years. However, in Table 1, the Congressional Research Service (CRS) reflects only the target for the period required by the pertinent NDC. For example, a country may include 2030, 2035, and 2050 targets in their 2025 NDC. However, CRS only listed the 2035 target in Table 1 as that is the period that the 2025 NDCs are to cover. Not all NDC submissions include quantifiable targets, and not all quantifiable targets encompass economy-wide reductions or include all greenhouse gases. Where applicable, the table notes these differences. Additional information on and analysis of NDCs can be found in CRS Report R46945, Greenhouse Gas Emission Reduction Pledges by Selected Countries: Nationally Determined Contributions and Net-Zero Legislation and UNFCCC's 2025 NDC Synthesis Report.20
