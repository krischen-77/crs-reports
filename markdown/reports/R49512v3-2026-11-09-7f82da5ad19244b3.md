Summary

There are many federal civil service leadership and support positions in the legislative and executive branches of the federal government that may be subject to noncareer (political) appointments. The nature of these positions varies widely and includes positions such as agency heads and their immediate subordinates, policy executives and advisors, and aides who report to these officials. Since 1952, successive volumes of *United States Government Policy and Supporting Positions* have identified the positions in the federal government to which political appointments can be made. The book is often referred to as the "Plum Book" due to both the purple cover of the physical book and the list of positions found within its pages. In 2022, Congress passed legislation requiring the information in the Plum Book to be published in an online database.

Congress may be interested in identifying certain information about these positions. Such information could include, for each position, its title, type of appointment, name of individual(s) currently holding the role, location, salary, tenure (if applicable), and expiration date of the term (if applicable).

This report provides a brief history and overview of the Plum Book and [Plum Reporting website](https://www.opm.gov/about-us/open-government/plum-reporting/plum-data/), an explanation of the various political appointment types listed on the website, and examples of regional positions within federal agencies.

*United States Government Policy and Supporting Positions* (The "Plum Book")

After winning the presidential election in 1952, President Eisenhower requested a list of federal government positions to which he could make noncareer appointments. As a result, the *United States Government Policy and Supporting Positions* book[1](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn1 "Government Publishing Office, \" United States Government Policy and Supporting Positions (Plum Book) ,\" https://www.govinfo.gov/collection/plum-book .") was created. The book is often referred to as the "Plum Book" due to both the purple cover of the physical book and the list of positions found within its pages. Since 1960, the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs and the House Committee on GovernmentReform (and the committees' predecessors) alternately published new editions of the Plum Book every four years.

The major categories of positions listed in the Plum Book include the following:

* Executive Schedule and salary-equivalent positions paid at the rates established for levels I through V of the Executive Schedule
* Senior Executive Service "General" positions
* Senior Foreign Service positions
* Schedule C positions excepted from the competitive service by the President or by the director of the Office of Personnel Management (OPM) because of the confidential or policy-determining nature of the position duties
* Other positions at the GS-14 level and above excepted from the competitive civil service by law because of the confidential or policy-determining nature of the position duties[2](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn2 "U.S. Congress, Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs , United States Government Policy and Supporting Positions, 11 8 th Cong., 2 nd sess., committee print, November 12, 202 4 (GPO, 202 4 ), p. iii .")

The information on the website is self-reported by each agency, and OPM publishes updates. It is typically not updated in real time. Under Title 5, Section 3330f(4)(A), of the *U.S. Code*, agency heads are required to provide updates not less than once a year on the policy and supporting positions in the agency, appointees occupying such positions, and appointees formerly occupying the positions.

While agencies are required to certify that the information provided is complete and accurate,[11](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn11 "Each agency's most recent certification date is available on OPM's website. This issue of currency could result in discrepancies between an agency's real-time appointment numbers and those provided in the database.

Vacant Positions

In addition to the position titles and names of the incumbents, the Plum Reporting website also lists the vacancy status of a position.[18](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn18 "As of the Plum Reporting website update of June 15 , 202 6 .") As mentioned previously, because the Plum Reporting website's data is typically not updated in real time, some of the information in the database may not be current.

Those seeking vacancy information may wish to supplement their Plum Reporting website searches by consulting the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO) Vacancy Tracker,[19](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn19 "The GAO vacancy tracker is available at https://www.gao.gov/legal/other-legal-work/federal-vacancies-reform-act . T he vacancy tracker includes only information that federal departments and agencies have self-reported to GAO. For this reason, the results at this website may not be complete or current.") federal agency websites, and Congress.gov's nominations database[20](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn20 "Congress.gov's nomination s database presents information about nominations to presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed positions . While it does not explicitly identify the current status of a position, a user can determine whether the President has submitted a nomination to the Senate and what the status of the nomination is in the Senate. For more information on the nominations process, see CRS Report R44083, Appointment and Confirmation of Executive Branch Leadership:...") as additional resources. It can be difficult to establish with certainty whether an agency, without a formal announcement, either (1) filled a vacancy by designating an individual to serve in an acting capacity or (2) assigned the duties of a vacant position to a current employee who is still officially occupying his or her current position.

On the Plum Reporting website itself, positions listed as vacant do not display the appointment type associated with the position, as seen above in [**Figure 3**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#_Ref238804587). OPM notes that this is because "several categories of positions can be filled by more than one type of appointment, e.g., Senior Executive Service (SES) positions listed in this publication may be filled by using career, noncareer, limited emergency, or limited term appointment authorities."[21](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn21 "OPM , \"Data Information,\" https://www.opm.gov/about-us/open-government/plum-reporting/data-information/  .")

If a user, keeping this OPM treatment of the appointment type in mind, clicks the drop-down and searches for a specific "Type of Appointment," the database will display both the relevant filled and vacant positions matching those criteria, even if the listed vacant positions themselves do not actively display an appointment type on the screen.

Additionally, if the Plum Reporting website data is downloaded into a CSV file, vacant positions will display their associated types of appointment in Column E of the downloaded spreadsheet. An example can be seen in [**Figure 4**](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#_Ref238805006). This may be useful when attempting to identify vacant positions with presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed (PAS) and presidentially appointed (PA) appointment types. For such positions, the category of position cannot be filled by more than one appointment type, so the appointment type should appear in every case.

A Brief Overview of Select Political Appointment Types

The Plum Reporting website includes positions that can be filled through political (or noncareer) appointment. It does not include positions in the civil service that are filled through a competitive hiring process.[24](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn24 "For more information, see CRS Report R45635, Categories of Federal Civil Service Employment: An Overview of Appointment, Pay, and Adverse Action Entitlements , by Jon O. Shimabukuro et al. ; and CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11412, Schedule Policy/Career: 2026 Final Rule, Legal Challenges, and Issues for Lawmakers , by Jimmy Balser, Mainon A. Schwartz, and Jon O. Shimabukuro .")

A brief description of the position types listed in the database is provided below.

* **Presidentially****a****ppointed****, Senate****-****c****onfirmed** **(PAS)****.**These positions are often referred to as "advice and consent positions" or "PAS positions." PAS positions are filled through nomination of an individual by the President. The President [submits the nomination to the Senate for its consideration](https://www.crs.gov/Reports/R44083?source=author).[25](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn25 "For more information on this process, see CRS Report R44083, Appointment and Confirmation of Executive Branch Leadership: An Overview , by Henry B. Hogue and Maeve P. Carey .") When the Senate confirms a nomination, the President [may formally appoint the individual to the position](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/R44083). Under certain circumstances, the President can exercise constitutional recess appointment authority to [appoint an individual to an office while the Senate is in recess](https://www.crs.gov/Reports/RS21308?source=author).[26](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn26 "For more information on recess appointments, see CRS Report RS21308, Recess Appointments: Frequently Asked Questions , by Henry B. Hogue .") Most PAS appointees serve at the pleasure of the President.[27](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn27 "For more information on th is topic , see CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11448, Trump v. Slaughter and the Future of For-Cause Removal Protections , by Benjamin M. Barczewski, Todd Garvey, and Jonathan M. Gaffney ; and CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11449, Trump v. Cook and For-Cause Removal of Federal Reserve Governors , by Jonathan M. Gaffney and Todd Garvey .")
* The Plum Reporting website does not list positions in the judicial branch, such as U.S. circuit court and district judge positions, though these positions are presidentially appointed and Senate-confirmed.[28](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn28 "While the Plum Reporting website does not list district or circuit judicial positions, the United States Courts website provides a series of tables that lists recent judicial confirmations and current judicial vacancies at https://www.uscourts.gov/data-news/judicial-vacancies/confirmation-listing . For more information on the appointment process for appointing judges to the U.S. circuit and district courts, see CRS Report R43762, The Appointment Process for U.S. Circuit and District...")
* **Presidentially****a****ppointed.** These positions are often referred to as "PA" positions. A PA appointment requires the President to select and appoint an individual for the position. No Senate confirmation is required. Most PA appointees serve at the pleasure of the President.[29](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn29 "For more information on this topic, see CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11448, Trump v. Slaughter and the Future of For-Cause Removal Protections , by Benjamin M. Barczewski, Todd Garvey, and Jonathan M. Gaffney , and CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11449, Trump v. Cook and For-Cause Removal of Federal Reserve Governors , by Jonathan M. Gaffney and Todd Garvey .")
* **Schedule C.** Schedule C appointees serve in confidential or policy-determining positions.[30](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn30 "For more information on Schedule C appointments, see \"Position Descriptions: Schedule C Positions\" on the Plum Reporting website at https://www.opm.gov/about-us/open-government/plum-reporting/position-descriptions/  .") [Such appointmentsdo not go through a merit-based competitive hiring process](https://www.opm.gov/about-us/open-government/plum-reporting/position-descriptions/). Rather, in most cases, an agency submits a request to OPM to place a position in a Schedule C appointment category, and the authorization and granting of each position is considered on a case-by-case basis.[31](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn31 "Per OPM, \"I n addition to the Schedule C positions authorized by the OPM Director, a limited number of positions may be placed under Schedule C by Executive Order of the President or by legislation …. The only time when OPM approval is not required for a Schedule C position is when a position is filled by a temporary Schedule C appointment during a Presidential transition, a change of agency head, or establishment of a new agency. \" S ee \"Position Descriptions: Schedule C Positions\" on the P...") Schedule C appointees "serve at the pleasure of the appointing authority (usually the agency head) and may be removed at any time."[32](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn32 "OPM , \"Plum Reporting: Position Descriptions,\" October 8, 2026, https://www.opm.gov/about-us/open-government/plum-reporting/position-descriptions/#SCP .") OPM states that "Schedule C positions authorized by OPM are automatically revoked when the incumbent leaves the position (i.e., there is no such thing as a 'vacant' Schedule C position)." If an individual departs a role and a "vacancy" occurs, the agency must submit to OPM a new request to fill the position as a Schedule C appointment type.
* **Career Senior Executive Service (SES)** **a****ppointment****.** [Career SES appointees](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF11743) typically serve in top-level policy and supervisory roles.[33](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn33 "For more information, see CRS In Focus IF11743, The Senior Executive Service: An Overview , by Maeve P. Carey .") These positions are filled through a merit-based competitive hiring process. Generally, incumbents may be removed from the SES for less than fully successful executive performance.[34](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn34 "5 U.S.C. §3592(a)(2) .") Career appointees who are removed from the SES for performance reasons may be [placed in non-SES civil service positions](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:5%20section:3592%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section3592)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true).[35](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn35 "5 U.S.C. §3594(b)(1) .") Career appointees may also [be removed generally from federal service](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:5%20section:7543%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section7543)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true) for misconduct, neglect of duty, malfeasance, or failure to accept a directed reassignment or to accompany a position in a transfer of function.[36](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn36 "5 U.S.C. §7543 .")
* Most SES positions are filled through career appointments.[37](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn37 "See noncareer, limited term, and limited emergency appointment types in this report for additional details.") And, not all career SES positions are listed on the Plum Reporting website. For example, the website does not include SES positions that are "career reserved."[38](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn38 "Career reserved positions are those that involve day-to-day functions of government and require impartiality. By law (5 U.S.C. §3132(b)(1)) and regulation (5 C.F.R. §214.402), certain types of positions must be removed from political influence to ensure public confidence in the impartiality of certain government functions. Examples include audits, inspections, civil or criminal law enforcement, grants administration, and security matters, the principal duties of which involve day-to-day govern...") Approximately half of federal SES positions fall into this category. Career-reserved positions may be filled only through career appointments and therefore do not meet the criteria for inclusion in the Plum Book or Plum Reporting website. OPM publishes an annual list of "career reserved" SES positions in the *Federal Register*.[39](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn39 "The most recent Federal Register notice lists career reserved SES positions for calendar year 2024. It can be found at https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/05/20/2025-08853/senior-executive-service-positions-that-were-career-reserved-during-calendar-year-2024 .")
* **Noncareer** **SES** **a****ppointment.** Noncareer SES appointees are not hired through a competitive process. However, [they must meet the position qualifications](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:5%20section:3394%20edition:prelim)%20OR%20(granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section3394)&f=treesort&edition=prelim&num=0&jumpTo=true),[40](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn40 "5 U.S.C. §3394 .") and OPM must generally approve an agency's use of noncareer hiring authority. A noncareer appointee serves at the pleasure of the appointing authority (usually the agency head) and [may be removed at any time](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF12721).[41](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn41 "For more information, see CRS In Focus IF12721, Presidential Transitions: Executive Branch Political Appointment Status , by Henry B. Hogue .") Up to [10% of SES positions](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF11743) across the executive branch may be filled by noncareer appointees at any given time.[42](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn42 "5 U.S.C. §3 134(b). For more information, see CRS In Focus IF11743, The Senior Executive Service: An Overview , by Maeve P. Carey .") The proportion of noncareer appointees may vary by agency, however, so noncareer appointments at some agencies may exceed 10%, while such appointments at other agencies may be fewer than 10%. [Generally, no agency may exceed a 25% limit](http://www.crs.gov/Reports/IF11743).[43](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn43 "5 U.S.C. §3 134(d).")
* **Limited** **t****erm** **SES** **a****ppointment (TA).** Limited term appointees typically serve in short-term project-related or presidential transition roles. [Their service may not exceed a period of three years](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/senior-executive-service/reference-materials/guidesesservices.pdf).[44](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn44 "5 U.S.C. §3 132(a)(5).") Such an appointee serves at the pleasure of the appointing authority (usually the agency head) and may be removed at any time. By law, limited appointments may not exceed 5% of all SES positions government-wide.[45](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn45 "5 U.S.C. §3 134(e). This 5% cap encompasses positions in both limited term and limited emergency appointments. For more information, see CRS In Focus IF11743, The Senior Executive Service: An Overview , by Maeve P. Carey .")
* **Limited** **e****mergency** **SES** **a****ppointment (EA).** Limited emergency appointees typically serve in short-term roles to meet urgent and unanticipated needs. [Their service may not exceed a period of 18 months](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/senior-executive-service/reference-materials/guidesesservices.pdf).[46](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn46 "5 U.S.C. §3 132(a)(6).") Such an appointee serves at the pleasure of the appointing authority (usually the agency head) and may be removed at any time.
* **Appointment** **e****xcepted by** **s****tatute (XS).** [These appointments are authorized by the parent agencies' governing legislation](https://chcoc.gov/sites/default/files/Periodically%20Listing%20Updates%20to%20Management%20%28PLUM%29%20Guidance%20March%202024.pdf) and are subject to the terms of said legislation. Sometimes these positions are referred to as excepted service positions.[47](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49512.html#fn47 "For more information, see CRS Report R45635, Categories of Federal Civil Service Employment: An Overview of Appointment, Pay, and Adverse Action Entitlements , by Jon O. Shimabukuro et al. .")
