## Summary

According to the Office of Personnel Management (OPM), the executive branch of the federal government consists of an estimated two million civilian employees. Federal law categorizes these employees into three types of service—the competitive service, the excepted service, and the Senior Executive Service (SES)—that may be distinguished by different selection, compensation, and other standards. 5 U.S.C. §§ 2101a-2103. Title 5 of the U.S. Code (Title 5) contains most of the standards governing federal employment for employees subject to Title 5, and OPM is generally responsible for implementing these requirements.

The competitive service largely consists of all civil service positions in the executive branch, other than (1) positions excepted from the competitive service by statute; (2) positions appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate; and (3) the SES. *Id*. § 2101. Traditionally, OPM has administered examinations for entrance into the competitive service. 5 C.F.R. § 332.101(a). These examinations are meant to be "practical in character" and relate to "matters that fairly test the relative capacity and fitness of the applicants for the appointment sought." 5 U.S.C. § 3304(b)(1). Title 5 also authorizes OPM to prescribe rules allowing agencies to hire candidates directly under specified circumstances. *Id*. § 3304(b)(3).

The excepted service includes designated civil service positions that are not in the competitive service or the SES and are not subject to competitive examination. *Id*. § 2103(a). OPM maintains authority to exempt a position from the competitive service when it determines that an appointment through competitive examination is not practicable, or the recruitment of students or recent graduates would be better achieved through alternate recruitment and assessment processes. 5 C.F.R. § 213.102(b).

The pay structure for the competitive service and the excepted service is similar. Both services are typically paid in accordance with the General Schedule, a schedule of annual basic pay rates that consists of 15 grades, designated "GS-1" through "GS-15." 5 U.S.C. § 5332. This fixed pay scale is generally designed to reflect, among other things, equal pay for substantially equal work within a local pay area. *Id*. § 5301. Additionally, employees in the competitive service and those meeting specified tenure requirements in the excepted service have similar notice and appeal rights for adverse personnel actions. *Id*. § 7511(a)(1). For example, before a removal, a suspension for more than 14 days, a reduction in grade or pay, or a furlough of 30 days or less, the agency must provide at least 30 days' advance written notice to the affected employee. *Id*. § 7513(b)(1). The employee must also be given a reasonable time to respond to the notice and provide affidavits and other evidence to support the answer. *Id*. § 7513(b)(2). Some adverse actions may also be appealed to the Merit Systems Protection Board (MSPB or Board), an agency that reviews and adjudicates specified personnel actions taken against qualifying federal employees. *Id*. § 7701.

The SES is a corps of high-level government employees who manage major programs and projects within most federal agencies. *Id*. § 2101a. In these leadership roles, SES members may serve as a link between top-level political appointees of an agency and career civil servants within the agency. The SES is governed by a regulatory structure separate from the competitive and excepted services. *Id*. §§ 3131-3136. While SES members are primarily career appointees chosen through a merit-based competitive hiring process, others are noncareer, limited term, or limited emergency appointees (commonly political appointees) selected by agency leadership. *Id*. §§ 3393-3394. To shield certain SES roles from political influence, some SES positions (career reserved positions) must be filled with career appointees, and Title 5 limits the number of noncareer and limited term appointees who may serve in SES positions. *Id*. § 3134.

The SES pay structure is distinct from the rest of the civil service. *Id*. § 5382. Title 5 specifies that SES members are paid within a particular range based on an executive's individual performance or contribution to agency performance (or both), as measured under a performance appraisal system. *Id*. In addition, Title 5 articulates special conditions and procedures for removing, suspending, or taking other adverse actions against a member of the SES. *Id*. §§ 7541-7543. For example, career SES appointees who have successfully completed a one-year probationary period may be removed or subject to adverse action only for specified reasons, including misconduct and substandard performance. *Id*. § 7543(a). Career appointees must receive advance written notice of these actions, and an opportunity to appeal the action. *Id*. § 7543(b), (d). In comparison, noncareer, limited term, and limited emergency appointees are generally not subject to the same protections and may be removed from the SES at any time. *Id*. § 3592(c).

## Introduction

The federal government is the nation's largest employer,[1](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn1) with more than two million executive branch employees in the United States, U.S. Territories, and foreign countries.[2](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn2) A majority of these employees work in the competitive service.[3](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn3) Applicants for competitive service positions compete with other applicants and are evaluated according to objective standards before they may be appointed.[4](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn4) The executive branch includes two other service classifications—the excepted service and the Senior Executive Service (SES)—with hiring and removal standards that diverge from those prescribed for the competitive service. Positions in the excepted service are specifically excepted from the competitive service by statute, by the President, or by the Office of Personnel Management (OPM).[5](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn5) SES positions are also not in the competitive service.[6](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn6) The SES includes senior managerial, supervisory, and policy positions that are subject to a different pay scale, as well as different hiring and removal standards.[7](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn7) This report examines the three service classifications, and reviews some of the central features and notable differences among these classifications.

## Competitive Service

The competitive service consists of all civil service positions in the executive branch, except the following:

* positions that are specifically excepted from the competitive service by or under statute;
* positions to which appointments are made by presidential nomination for confirmation by the Senate, unless the Senate otherwise directs; and
* positions in the SES.[8](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn8)

The competitive service also includes non-executive branch positions and positions in the District of Columbia government that are specifically included in the competitive service by statute.[9](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn9)

### Appointment Process

OPM administers examinations for entrance into the competitive service.[10](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn10) These examinations are meant to be "practical in character" and relate to "matters that fairly test the relative capacity and fitness of the applicants for the appointment sought[.]"[11](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn11) OPM identifies the relative weights for the subjects in an examination, and assigns numerical ratings on a 100-point scale.[12](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn12) Applicants who meet the minimum requirements for entrance to an examination, such as citizenship and residence requirements, and are rated 70 or more in the examination are eligible for appointment in the competitive service.[13](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn13) These individuals are placed on registers or lists of eligibles.[14](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn14) When an agency seeks to fill a competitive service position, it requests a certificate of eligibles from OPM.[15](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn15) This certificate is to include enough names from the top of the relevant register to allow an agency appointing official to consider at least three individuals for every position to be filled.[16](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn16)

The competitive service includes several types of appointments. An individual selected for a continuing position is generally appointed as a career-conditional employee subject to an initial one-year probationary period.[17](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn17) After three continuous years of service in a career-conditional appointment, an employee will be converted to a career appointment.[18](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn18) A term appointment is a nonpermanent appointment for a period of more than one year, but fewer than four years.[19](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn19) An agency may make a term appointment when the need for an employee's services is not permanent, but involves a special project, extraordinary workload, or reorganization.[20](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn20) A temporary appointment is a time-limited appointment for a period not to exceed one year.[21](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn21) An agency may make a temporary appointment to fill a short-term position or meet an employment need that is scheduled to end within a specified timeframe.[22](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn22)

### Pay Structure

Employees in the competitive service are generally paid in accordance with the General Schedule, a schedule of annual basic pay rates that consists of 15 grades, designated "GS-1" through "GS-15."[23](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn23) The grades include 10 steps that provide for increasing rates of pay. An employee may receive a periodic step-increase, commonly referred to as a within-grade increase, if the employee is paid less than the maximum rate for his or her position and the employee's work is "at an acceptable level of competence," as determined by the head of an agency.[24](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn24) The increase may be made annually at steps 1, 2, and 3; every two years at steps 4, 5, and 6; and every three years at steps 7, 8, and 9 of the General Schedule.[25](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn25)

General Schedule salaries are based on the principles that there is equal pay for substantially equal work within a local pay area, that any pay distinctions are based on work and performance, that federal pay rates are comparable with non-federal pay rates for the same level of work, and that any pay disparities between federal and non-federal employees should be eliminated.[26](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn26)

### Adverse Action Protections and Procedures

Employees in the competitive service who are not serving a probationary or trial period, or have completed one year of current continuous service in a position other than a temporary appointment limited to one year or less, maintain specified notice and appeal rights for adverse personnel actions.[27](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn27) Before an agency may suspend such a qualifying employee for up to 14 days, the employee must be given an advance written notice that identifies the specific reasons for the suspension.[28](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn28) The employee must also be provided a reasonable time to answer the notice and furnish affidavits and other evidence to support the answer.[29](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn29)

Similar notice is required before an agency may subject a qualifying employee to other adverse personnel actions. Before a removal, a suspension for more than 14 days, a reduction in grade or pay, or a furlough of 30 days or less, the agency must provide at least 30 days' advance written notice to the employee.[30](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn30) The employee must also be given a reasonable time to respond to the notice and provide affidavits and other evidence to support the answer.[31](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn31) Unlike suspensions for up to 14 days, these adverse actions may be appealed to the Merit Systems Protection Board (MSPB or Board), an agency that reviews and adjudicates specified personnel actions taken against qualifying federal employees.[32](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn32)

In general, an agency must establish three factors to withstand an individual's challenge of his or her adverse personnel action.[33](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn33) First, the agency must prove, by a preponderance of the evidence, that the charged conduct occurred.[34](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn34) Second, it must establish a nexus between that conduct and the efficiency of the civil service.[35](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn35) Finally, the agency must show that the penalty imposed on the employee is reasonable.[36](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn36) An agency's action may not be sustained if the appellant shows: (1) harmful error in applying the agency's procedures in arriving at its decision; (2) the decision was based on a prohibited personnel practice; or (3) the decision was not in accordance with law.[37](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn37)

## Excepted Service

About one-third of all federal workers in the executive branch are employed in the excepted service.[38](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn38) The excepted service consists of those civil service positions that are not in the competitive service or the SES.[39](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn39) Positions in the excepted service may be designated by statute or by OPM, and are not subject to competitive examination.[40](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn40) OPM will exempt a position from the competitive service when it determines that an appointment through competitive examination is not practicable, or the recruitment of certain students or recent graduates would be better achieved through alternate recruitment and assessment processes.[41](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn41) For example, OPM may determine that a position should be excepted from the competitive service because it is impracticable to examine the knowledge, skills, and abilities required for a position.[42](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn42)

### Appointment Process

Positions in the excepted service are categorized into seven schedules or categories of appointment authority. Schedule A includes positions that are not of a confidential or policy-determining character for which it is not practicable to examine applicants.[43](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn43) Attorneys, chaplains, and short-term positions for which there is a critical hiring need are examples of schedule A positions.[44](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn44)

Schedule B also includes positions that are not of a confidential or policy-determining character for which it is not practicable to examine applicants.[45](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn45) Unlike Schedule A positions, however, these positions require an applicant to satisfy basic qualification standards established by OPM for the relevant occupation and grade level.[46](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn46) Individuals appointed to schedule B positions engage in a variety of activities, including policy analysis, teaching, and technical assistance.[47](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn47)

Positions in schedule C are policy-determining or involve a close and confidential working relationship with the head of an agency or other key appointed officials.[48](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn48) These positions include most political appointees below the cabinet and subcabinet levels.[49](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn49) Political appointees are generally appointed by the President or his delegates and have no expectation of continued employment beyond the presidential administration in which the appointment is made. An agency may have a variety of titles for jobs designated as schedule C.[50](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn50)

Schedule D includes positions that are not of a confidential or policy-determining character for which competitive examination makes it difficult to recruit a sufficient number of certain students or recent graduates.[51](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn51) Examples of schedule D positions include those involving science, technology, engineering, or mathematics (STEM) occupations and positions in the Presidential Management Fellows Program.[52](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn52) Schedule D positions generally require an applicant to satisfy basic qualification standards established by OPM for the relevant occupation and grade level.[53](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn53)

Schedule E includes administrative law judges (ALJs) who are appointed under 5 U.S.C. § 3105.[54](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn54) This section provides that each agency shall appoint as many ALJs as are necessary for proceedings required to be conducted. The establishment of Schedule E followed the U.S. Supreme Court's decision in *Lucia v. Securities and Exchange Commission* *(SEC)*, a 2018 decision involving the appointment of that agency's ALJs.[55](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn55) In *Lucia*, the Court determined that the SEC's ALJs are "Officers of the United States," who must be appointed in accordance with the Appointments Clause of the U.S. Constitution.[56](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn56) In Executive Order (EO) 13843, President Donald Trump, in excepting ALJs from the competitive service, observed that *Lucia* may "raise questions about the method of appointing ALJs, including whether competitive examination and competitive service selection procedures are compatible with the discretion an agency head must possess under the Appointments Clause in selecting ALJs."[57](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn57) President Trump maintained that placing ALJs in the excepted service "will mitigate concerns about undue limitations on the selection of ALJs, reduce the likelihood of successful Appointments Clause challenges, and forestall litigation in which such concerns have been or might be raised."[58](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn58)

Schedule Policy/Career includes career positions of a "confidential, policy-determining, policy-making, or policy-advocating character that are not normally subject to change as a result of a presidential transition."[59](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn59) EO 14171, which established Schedule Policy/Career, directed agency heads to identify existing positions in the competitive and excepted services that meet this criterion.[60](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn60) When reviewing positions, agency heads considered whether a position involves specified duties, such as supervising attorneys or viewing, circulating, or otherwise working with proposed regulations.[61](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn61) Once the relevant positions were identified, agency heads petitioned the OPM Director and recommended that the President place these competitive service, Schedule A, Schedule B, or Schedule D positions in Schedule Policy/Career. In June 2026, President Trump reclassified approximately 8,000 positions for placement in Schedule Policy/Career.[62](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn62) In general, an employee occupying a Schedule Policy/Career position is subject to different adverse action procedures from competitive service employees and employees in the other excepted service schedules.

Schedule G includes positions of a policy-making or policy-advocating character that are normally subject to change as a result of a presidential transition.[63](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn63) According to an executive branch fact sheet, the Trump Administration intends for Schedule G to "fill the gap" between Schedule C and Schedule Policy/Career, and "facilitate[] appointments of non-career federal employees who will serve temporarily and implement the policy agenda prescribed by the American people . . ."[64](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn64) Because of the policy-making or policy-advocating character of Schedule G positions, employees occupying these positions would also be subject to different adverse action rules from those that govern competitive service appointments, ALJs, and employees in Schedule A, Schedule B, and Schedule D positions.

OPM is required to publish "at least annually" a list of excepted service positions.[65](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn65) A consolidated listing of all Schedule A, B, C, and Policy/Career authorities, current as of June 30 of each year, is also required to be published annually in the *Federal Register*.[66](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn66) Additionally, pursuant to the Periodically Listing Updates to Management Act of 2022,[67](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn67) OPM established a website that contains information on policy and supporting positions in the executive branch and other federal entities.[68](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn68) The data may be arrayed by agency, component, position, type of appointment, including Schedule C excepted appointment or excepted by statute, and position status (whether filled or vacant). Each entry provides information on position title, the name of the incumbent, location, and pay plan.

### Pay Structure

Like employees in the competitive service, excepted service employees are generally paid in accordance with the General Schedule.[69](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn69) Some excepted service employees, however, may be paid under administratively determined (AD) pay schedules or agency specific pay systems.[70](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn70)

### Adverse Action Protections and Procedures

Most excepted service employees maintain the same notice and appeal rights as competitive service employees for removals, suspensions for more than 14 days, reductions in grade or pay, and furloughs of up to 30 days.[71](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn71) These rights do not apply to employees in positions that have a "confidential, policy-determining, policy-making or policy-advocating character."[72](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn72) Thus, employees in Schedule C positions do not maintain these rights.[73](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn73)

In addition, some employees in the excepted service must satisfy different durational requirements before the notice and appeal rights become available. So-called "preference eligibles" in an executive agency, the Postal Service, or the Postal Rate Commission must complete one year of current continuous service to avail themselves of the relevant notice and appeal rights.[74](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn74) The term "preference eligible" refers to specified military veterans and some of their family members, such as an unmarried widow, and the spouse of a service-connected disabled veteran.[75](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn75) Employees in the excepted service who are not preference eligibles and (1) are not serving a probationary or trial period under an initial appointment pending conversion to the competitive service, or (2) have completed two years of current or continuous service in the same or similar position, have the same notice and appeal rights as qualifying employees in the competitive service.[76](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn76)

## Senior Executive Service

The SES is a cadre of high-level government employees who manage major programs and projects within most federal agencies.[77](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn77) While they are considered federal employees within the civil service system, the SES is governed by a regulatory structure separate from the competitive and excepted services. As defined in statute, SES positions are generally managerial or supervisory positions that are classified above the GS-15 grade (or certain equivalent positions) and need not be appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.[78](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn78) In these leadership roles, SES members may serve as intermediaries between top-level political appointees of an agency who seek to carry out the objectives of a particular President and career civil servants with institutional experience relating to relevant issues.[79](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn79) According to OPM data, as of April 2026, the SES had approximately 6,647 members.[80](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn80)

### Appointment Process

There are two types of SES positions: (1) career reserved and (2) general.[81](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn81) Career reserved positions must be filled with career appointees and are designated in accordance with OPM criteria and regulations.[82](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn82) The criteria and regulations provide that a position will be designated as a career reserved position "only if the filling of the position by a career appointee is necessary to ensure impartiality, or the public's confidence in the impartiality, of the Government."[83](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn83) Agency heads are responsible for designating career reserved positions at their agencies in accordance with the criteria and regulations.[84](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn84) OPM regulations reflect the types of SES roles in which this designation is appropriate, including those involving adjudication and appeals, auditing, and law enforcement duties.[85](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn85) General positions may be filled by career appointees, as well as other noncareer and limited term (i.e., political) appointees.[86](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn86)

There are four types of SES appointments: career, noncareer, limited term, and limited emergency appointees. The SES mainly consists of "career appointees" chosen through a merit-based competitive hiring process.[87](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn87) As part of this process, each agency must maintain a recruitment program for career appointees, as well as at least one executive board that reviews qualifications and makes recommendations regarding SES candidates.[88](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn88) An OPM-convened Qualification Review Board (QRB) must certify the executive and managerial qualifications of a selected candidate before a career appointment may be made to an SES position.[89](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn89)

Unlike career appointees, noncareer appointees are not subject to the competitive selection process, but agency heads must determine that these appointees meet the qualifications of the SES position.[90](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn90) While noncareer appointees are not QRB-certified, OPM must approve these appointees.[91](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn91) Limited term and limited emergency appointees make up a small subset of the SES, and their terms are non-renewable.[92](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn92) These appointments are used when a position is needed for a specified period (such as to manage a special project), or a position is established to meet a "bona fide, unanticipated, urgent need."[93](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn93) Limited term and limited emergency appointments are also subject to OPM approval.[94](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn94) Title 5 of the U.S. Code (Title 5) limits the number of noncareer and limited term appointees who may serve in SES positions.[95](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn95)

### Pay Structure

The SES pay structure is also distinct from the rest of the civil service.[96](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn96)Title 5 specifies that the pay rate of each senior executive is based on the executive's individual performance or contribution to agency performance (or both), as measured under a "rigorous" performance appraisal system.[97](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn97) Each federal agency must maintain at least one of these appraisal systems, subject to OPM standards, review, and approval.[98](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn98) Performance appraisals of SES members may consider factors such as improvements in efficiency, productivity, and quality of work or service, cost efficiency, and performance timeliness.[99](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn99)

Title 5 also sets out different pay rates for the SES, with a minimum rate of basic pay equal to 120 percent of the rate for GS-15, step 1, and a maximum rate of basic pay equal to the rate for Level III of the Executive Schedule.[100](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn100) SES members' annual aggregate pay (that includes additional compensation such as bonuses, awards,[101](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn101) and other payments in addition to basic pay) is capped at the rate for Level I of the Executive Schedule.[102](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn102) If a senior executive's total compensation exceeds the aggregate limitation, the executive receives the overage in the following calendar year.[103](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn103) Agencies with performance appraisal systems that are certified by OPM as "making meaningful distinctions based on relative performance" have a higher range of SES pay.[104](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn104) These agencies apply a maximum annual rate of basic pay for senior executives equal to the rate for level II of the Executive Schedule or apply an annual aggregate limit equal to the salary of the Vice President.[105](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn105)

### Adverse Action Protections and Procedures

Title 5 also articulates conditions and procedures for removing, suspending, or taking other adverse actions against a member of the SES. Career SES appointees who have successfully completed a one-year probationary period may be removed or subject to adverse action only for specified reasons.[106](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn106) For example, an SES career appointee may be removed from the civil service or suspended for more than 14 days only for misconduct, neglect of duty, malfeasance, or failure to accept a directed reassignment or to accompany a position in a transfer of function.[107](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn107) SES members must receive advance written notice about the action and opportunity to provide an answer or receive hearing, subject to exception.[108](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn108) The senior executive may also appeal the employment action to the MSPB.[109](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn109)

An agency's performance appraisal system is to provide for annual summary ratings at the following levels: one or more fully successful levels; a minimally satisfactory level; and an unsatisfactory level.[110](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn110) A career appointee receiving a single unsatisfactory performance rating may be reassigned or transferred within the SES or removed from the SES.[111](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn111) A career SES member who receives two unsatisfactory ratings in any period of five consecutive years must be removed from the SES.[112](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn112) A career SES member who receives less than fully successful ratings twice in any period of three consecutive years will also be removed from the SES.[113](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn113) Affected SES career appointees must receive advance written notice of these actions. While these appointees may not appeal these actions to the MSPB, they may request an informal hearing before the Board.[114](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn114)

In comparison, noncareer, limited term, and limited emergency appointees are generally not subject to the same removal protections and may be removed from the SES at any time.[115](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn115) The procedures for removal of noncareer and limited term appointees are largely not addressed in federal statute, and the terms and procedures for their removal are mainly at the discretion of the agency head.[116](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn116)

On occasion, Congress has adopted alternate removal procedures for senior executives at some agencies. For example, Congress established special removal requirements for SES members employed by the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) in response to concerns about performance and accountability at the agency. [117](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn117) In 2017, Congress passed the Department of Veterans Affairs Accountability and Whistleblower Protection Act, which amended an existing provision concerning removal procedures for these covered senior executives.[118](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn118) Under the 2017 Act, the VA Secretary has discretion to suspend, demote, remove, or take other actions against SES career appointees or other high-level executives if the Secretary determines that the individual's misconduct or performance warrants such action.[119](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn119) To address SES job performance issues more expeditiously, SES employees at the VA Department are entitled to abbreviated notice and appeals rights, as compared to the removal procedures in place in other federal agencies.[120](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn120)

## Considerations for Congress

While Congress has provided the President with broad authority to prescribe rules for the competitive service and identify positions in the excepted service, it has established a variety of standards that govern the employment of individuals in these positions, as well as those employed in the SES.[121](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn121) The appointment, compensation, and adverse action standards discussed in this report illustrate how Congress has legislated to govern civil service employment. The Civil Service Reform Act of 1978 (CSRA)[122](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn122) generally established these standards in response to a civil service system described at the time as "an outdated patchwork of statutes and rules built up over almost a century."[123](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn123) In considering the act, the Senate Committee on Governmental Affairs observed that "[a]lthough the civil service system has largely succeeded in safeguarding merit principles, there have been frequent attempts to circumvent them, some of which have been successful."[124](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn124)

Congress could maintain the status quo regarding regulation of the civil service, potentially allowing the executive branch to continue to pursue reforms within existing statutory authorities. Depending on the nature of these reforms, however, some may question whether they reflect the CSRA's original goals. For example, if a greater number of employees are placed in the excepted service and are subject to different adverse action procedures, questions could arise about the continued application of merit principles.

Congress could consider new legislation that would establish additional standards for federal employees, including measures to allow for compensation outside of the General Schedule[125](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn125) or bills that would limit or expand the President's ability to except positions from the competitive service within constitutional bounds.[126](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-26_R45635_4524bed367514e9e5e2dd1d8df94140d34e66c77.html#fn126) A review of the statutory and regulatory authorities for the excepted service schedules could also be part of the oversight agenda for the 120th Congress. One focus could involve strengthening the current reporting requirements to Congress for positions placed in the excepted service, potentially providing Congress with current information about changes in which Congress would concur or to which it would object.

Congress could also seek more information and guidance that might inform future legislation on the civil service. For example, Congress could require OPM to provide a report to the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform and the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs on executive agency use of the excepted service schedules and on OPM's procedures for oversight of the excepted service. Congress could direct OPM to convene a task force of the Chief Human Capital Officers Council, of which OPM is the chair, to review agency practices with regard to excepted service positions, including any needed enhancements to training for agency employees responsible for overseeing excepted service appointments to ensure that procedures of import to Congress are followed. Congress could also require the National Academy of Public Administration, a congressionally chartered nonprofit organization that conducts research on public administration, to conduct a study and report to Congress on the civil service, including any recommendations for congressional action.
