Summary

Biofuels—fuels made from biomass—have been used in the United States as a transportation fuel for decades. Ethanol produced from corn starch and biodiesel produced from soybean oil are two examples of biofuels used in vehicles. In most cases, these biofuels when used as transportation fuel in vehicles are blended with their petroleum fuel counterpart at a low level. In general, low-blend fuel (LB fuel) includes ethanol blended at a rate of 10% or lower (e.g., E10) and biodiesel blended at a rate of 5% or lower (e.g., B5). High-blend fuel (HB fuel) includes biofuel blended at a higher rate, such as ethanol blended at a rate greater than 10% (e.g., E15, a fuel blend of gasoline blended with 10.5-15% ethanol).

The Higher Blends Infrastructure Incentive Program (HBIIP) is an initiative created by the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) in 2020 to increase the sales and use of higher blends of ethanol and biodiesel. HBIIP funding has mostly come from the USDA Commodity Credit Corporation. However, under the law commonly known as the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 ([P.L. 117-169](https://www.congress.gov/law/117/pub/169)), Congress provided $500 million to HBIIP for "installing, retrofitting, or otherwise upgrading fuel dispensers or pumps," among other investments.

One HB fuel that HBIIP supports, E15, receives considerable attention in the 119th Congress:

* In May 2026, the House passed [H.R. 1346](http://www.congress.gov/bill/119/hr/1346), the Nationwide Consumer and Fuel Retailer Choice Act of 2025, which would, in part, allow for the year-round sale of E15.
* In September 2026, the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry reported out of committee [S. 5526](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119/s/5526), the Agricultural Act of 2026, which would also allow for the year-round sale of E15.

The second Trump Administration has taken several actions related to HBIIP, including the freezing of funds for HBIIP and releasing obligated funding for HBIIP for projects. Regarding E15, in the June 2026 supplemental funding request to Congress, the Trump Administration requested "an urgent and needed fix that codifies the permanent, year-round sale of E-15."

Congress may have questions about HBIIP and choose to conduct oversight of the program. For example, Congress may want to know more about the value received from HBIIP, program funding and implementation going forward, if there are environmental impacts associated with the use of HB fuel, monitoring nationwide HB fuel use, transportation fuel projections that could impact biofuel markets, and more. Alternatively, Congress may choose to take no action.

Biofuels

Biofuels are liquid fuels and blending components produced from biomass feedstocks, used primarily for transportation.[15](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn15 "U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) , Glossary , a ccessed October 22 , 2026 , https://www.eia.gov/tools/glossary/index.php?id=B .") The two leading biofuels produced and used for transportation in the United States are ethanol and biomass-based diesel.[16](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn16 "Bio mass- based diesel includes biodiesel and renewable diesel. EIA, Monthly Energy Review , DOE/EIA-0035(2026/9), September 2026 ; USDA Economic Research Service, U.S. Bioenergy Statistics , accessed October 19, 2026, https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/us-bioenergy-statistics .") LB fuel is approved for use in light-duty vehicles by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and vehicle manufacturers.[17](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn17 "For example, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency ( EPA ) reports that almost all gasoline s are E10 blends — fuel blend s of 10% ethanol and 90% gasoline . For more information, see EPA , \" Ethanol Waivers (E15 and E10) , \" a ccessed October 22 , 2026 , https://www.epa.gov/gasoline-standards/ethanol-waivers-e15-and-e10 .") HB fuels include higher blends of "ethanol (i.e., greater than 10 percent ethanol) and biodiesel (greater than 5 percent biodiesel)."[18](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn18 "USDA , \"Notice of Funds Availability for the Higher Blends Infrastructure Incentive Program (HBIIP) for Fiscal Year 2020,\" 85 Federal Register 26657, May 5, 2020.") The approval to use HB fuels in light-duty vehicles can vary by fuel type, vehicle model year, and vehicle manufacturer.[19](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn19 "For example, EPA has approved the use of E15 in model year 2001 and newer vehicles. See EPA , \" E15 Partial Waivers , \" a ccessed October 22 , 2026 , https://www.epa.gov/gasoline-standards/e15-fuel-partial-waivers . The Renewable Fuels Association reports that for the 202 6 model year, most vehicle manufacturers have approved their vehicles to use E15. See the table \" E15 Approval Status for U.S. Light-Duty Vehicles \" in Renewable Fuels Association, Ethanol Unleashed: 2026 Etha...")

In general, infrastructure required for HB fuel is the same type of infrastructure required for LB fuel (e.g., fuel dispenser, underground storage tank), but the HB fueling infrastructure is constructed specifically to endure the composition of HB fuel.[20](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn20 "Researchers and other s have examined the infrastructure needs for HB fuels . See, for example, Kristi Moriarty and Janet Yanowitz, E15 and Infrastructure , NREL/TP-5400-64156, National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL), May 2015 , https://afdc.energy.gov/files/u/publication/e15_infrastructure.pdf (NREL was renamed the National Laboratory of the Rockies in 2025) ; and USDA , Assessing Future Market Opportunities and Challenges for E15 and Higher Ethanol Blends , May 2022....") In short, not all vehicles on the road are certified by their vehicle manufacturer to use HB fuel, and not all fueling stations have the required infrastructure to sell HB fuel.

Both corn-starch ethanol and biomass-based diesel are established biofuels that can be blended into conventional fuel (i.e., petroleum fuel). For several years, production, consumption, and logistics for these fuels have been traditionally centered on low percentage levels (i.e., 10% ethanol and 5% biodiesel). In current LB fuel markets, industry knows how to produce the biofuel, the infrastructure exists to get the LB fuel to market, and consumers know that their vehicle manufacturer has approved the use of the LB fuel. Corn-starch ethanol and biomass-based diesel may be blended into conventional fuel at a higher rate. Using these biofuels at higher-blend rates could require different infrastructure and may raise some questions by lawmakers, consumers, and others.[21](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn21 "For example, m ultiple parties have expressed their concerns about the use of biofuels , biofuels' potential impacts , and ho w those impacts sh o uld be measured and accounted for . For example, see California Air Resources Board, Biofuels and Land Use Change Public Forum , November 6, 2025, https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/low-carbon-fuel-standard/lcfs-meetings-and-workshops .")

The predicament, mainly for biofuel supporters, is adapting the existing infrastructure—potentially nationwide—to use HB fuel.[22](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn22 "In 2015, when implementing the Biofuels Infrastructure Partnership ( BIP ) , the predecessor to HBIIP, USDA reported that \"[i]nfrastructure constraints and other barriers currently limit the market for biofuels and thereby the commodities used to produce them, contributing to lower commodity prices. In particular, the nation's fueling infrastructure is not sufficiently flexible to accommodate large additional quantities of higher ethanol blends that could enable biofuels to fill a signifi...") This might require, at a minimum, legislative and regulatory changes (depending on the HB fuel), funding (e.g., private funding, federal appropriations), administrative support (e.g., program staff to issue federal funding, technical expertise to install the infrastructure), and a willingness by industry and consumers to do so (e.g., a station owner who will upgrade their equipment, a consumer who will purchase an HB fuel). It remains unclear whether federal involvement and investment would encourage fueling station owners and others to upgrade their infrastructure to handle HB fuel and motivate consumers to purchase HB fuel.

HB fuel brings opportunities and challenges. Increased production of biomass feedstocks and biofuels could stimulate certain rural economies and segments of the agricultural sector. Conversely, if consumers were to purchase additional amounts of HB fuels, the increased demand could cause competition for feedstocks, interrupt feedstock pricing, and raise environmental concerns, among other issues.

Some HB fuels face circumstances unique to the biofuel blend type. For example, for ethanol, consumers may consider the energy content and engine performance of the HB fuel (e.g., E15). Ethanol has a lower energy content than gasoline yet a higher octane rating than gasoline.[23](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn23 "DOE reports that denatured ethanol (98% ethanol) contains about 30% less energy than gasoline per gallon. DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center , \" Ethanol Fuel Basics , \" accessed October 22 , 2026, https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/ethanol-fuel-basics . One study reports , \"In road trials, cars running on E15 get 1% to 2% fewer miles per gallon than those burning E10.\" Aaron Smith, \" California Now Allows More Ethanol i n Gasoline: Is This Going to Save Drivers Money? , \" Giannini Fou...") Consumers choosing to use HB fuel may consider fuel economy and performance, among other factors.

HB Fuel Consumption and Infrastructure

It is not clear how much HB fuel is consumed in the United States annually.[24](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn24 "The federal government collects and reports biofuel data. For instance, EIA provides data on fuel ethanol, biodiesel, renewable diesel, and other biofuels. EIA , Monthly Energy Review , June 25, 2026.") Due to variations in fuel mixtures (e.g., E15, E20, E85, B20), infrastructure capacity, vehicle manufacturer requirements, and state regulations—and for other reasons—it is difficult to ascertain nationwide consumption trends for HB fuel using publicly available data.[25](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn25 "B20 is a biodiesel blend consisting of 6% to 20% biodiesel. See DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center, \" Biodiesel B lends ,\" accessed November 3, 2026, https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/biodiesel-blends .")

Some states and stakeholders monitor HB fuel consumption, but their observations are generally focused on specific HB fuel(s) and/or a particular region. For example, the Iowa state government prepares an annual report of fuel gallons sold or dispensed in Iowa.[26](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn26 "Iowa Department of Revenue, 2025 Retailers Fuel Gallons Annual Report , April 2026.") The report contains a table that provides Iowa gasoline, diesel, and biofuel sales for 2025, as reported by retailers, including 870.7 million gallons of E10, 410.1 million gallons of E15, 0.9 million gallons of E20, and 24.0 million gallons of E85.[27](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn27 "Iowa Department of Revenue, 2025 Retailers Fuel Gallons Annual Report , April 2026, p . 5.") The annual report also states that "ethanol blends of E15 or higher constituted 27 percent of all gasoline sold or dispensed in 2025."[28](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn28 "Iowa Department of Revenue, 2025 Retailers Fuel Gallons Annual Report , April 2026 , p . 4 .")

Some data are available for certain HB fuels, but comprehensive data are not available. For example, DOE reports Illinois has 490 such public biodiesel fueling stations.[31](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn31 "DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center, Biodiesel Fueling Station Locations by State , interactive map, accessed October 21, 2026, https://afdc.energy.gov/data/10365 .") The fueling stations that sell HB fuel are a small fraction of the total number of fueling stations located in the United States. For example, the Oak Ridge National Laboratory reports there were 142,000 conventional refueling stations in 2020.[32](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn32 "Stacy C. Davis and Robert G. Boundy , Transportation Energy Data Book : Edition 40 , ORNL/TM-2022/2376, Oak Ridge National Laboratory, June 2022, https://tedb.ornl.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/TEDB_Ed_40.pdf .")

The cost of equipping a fueling station to sell HB fuel varies.[33](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn33 "This report focuses on the potential fueling station infrastructure needed to sell HB fuel because this is where USDA has appeared to place its focus for HBIIP . Infrastructure costs are facility-specific depending on what HB fuel will be sold and what equipment the station may need to upgrade to sell HB fuel. DOE reports there are approximately 60 pieces of equipment at a fueling station that handle fuel and vapor, some of which are below ground.[34](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn34 "Kristi Moriarty and Janet Yanowitz , E15 and Infrastructure , NREL/TP-5400-64156, NREL, May 2015, https://afdc.energy.gov/files/u/publication/e15_infrastructure.pdf . Station owners interested in selling an HB fuel might inspect their fueling station equipment to determine what equipment may be compatible and what equipment would need to be upgraded, and may also take into consideration the design life of the existing equipment and consumer demand, among other things, before making a decision to upgrade or not upgrade their equipment to sell an HB fuel.

Using ethanol as an example, USDA reports that "[w]hile virtually all stations have equipment necessary for storing and dispensing E10, it is difficult to discern how many have fueling equipment compatible with ethanol blends above E10."[35](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn35 "USDA , Assessing Future Market Opportunities and Challenges for E15 and Higher Ethanol Blends , May 2022, https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/documents/e15-market-opportunities.pdf .") The Higher Blends Infrastructure Incentive Program

Program Overview

HBIIP, a USDA initiative created in 2020, aims to "increase significantly the sales and use of higher blends of ethanol and biodiesel" and "is intended to encourage a more comprehensive approach to marketing higher blends by sharing the costs related to building out biofuel-related infrastructure."[42](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn42 "USDA , \"Notice of Funds Availability for the Higher Blends Infrastructure Incentive Program (HBIIP) for Fiscal Year 2020,\" 85 Federal Register 26657, May 5, 2020. For more information on HBIIP, see the USDA HBIIP web page at https://www.rd.usda.gov/HBIIP . USDA reports that HBIIP \"will build on biofuels infrastructure investments and experience gained through the Biofuels Infrastructure Partnership (BIP). USDA administered BIP from 2016–2019 through state and private partners to expand...") HBIIP awards cost-share grants that assist transportation fueling and biodiesel distribution facilities with upgrading or installing new infrastructure to be compatible with high-blend ethanol and biodiesel (e.g., E15 fuel dispensers, B20 fuel dispensers, ethanol storage tanks, and biodiesel storage tanks).[43](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn43 "For more information, see USDA , Projects Receiving Funding T hrough t he Higher Blends Infrastructure Incentive Program (HBIIP) and t he Rural Energy for America Program (REAP) Renewable and Energy Efficiency Program (Total: $14,000,418) , January 13 , 202 5 , https://www.rd.usda.gov/media/file/download/reap-and-hbiip-grant-awards-1-13-2025-0.pdf ; and USDA , \"Announcement of Future Competitive Grant Funds Availability for Higher Blends Infrastructure Incentive Program (HBIIP) fo...") USDA reports that consideration will also be given to biodiesel terminal operations and home heating oil distribution centers.[44](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn44 "USDA , \"Announcement of Future Competitive Grant Funds Availability for Higher Blends Infrastructure Incentive Program (HBIIP) for Fiscal Year 2020,\" 85 Federal Register 11946, February 28, 2020.")

Issues for Congress

The Supreme Court has established that Congress has the power to conduct oversight of federal programs.[66](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn66 "For more information, see CRS In Focus IF10015, Congressional Oversight and Investigations , by Todd Garvey, Mark J. Oleszek, and Ben Wilhelm .") As such, Congress may or may not choose to assess past, current, and potentially future HBIIP administration by USDA. And Congress may or may not choose to consider HB fuel consumption rates, infrastructure costs, potential environmental impacts, and other issues related to HBIIP. Congress could choose to take no action. Alternatively, Congress could decide to explore the above-mentioned areas, and may consider the following questions and data requests, which are presented in no order of priority.[67](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/HTML/R49508.html#fn67 "This is not a comprehensive list .")
