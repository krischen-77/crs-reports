Understanding Electricity Rates

Electricity rates (prices) are not set like the prices for most other goods and services.

Electricity rates are set by state or local utility regulators based on utility costs

(the revenue requirement) and sales, as discussed below.

What is the revenue requirement?

The revenue requirement is determined by the utility regulator and

mainly reflects the costs to build, operate, and maintain equipment for

generating electricity (e.g., power plants, fuels) and delivering electricity

(e.g., power lines, transformers).

Additionally, the revenue requirement might include costs for

implementing state energy policies, taxes paid by the utility, and a

reasonable return on the utility's capital investment (i.e., profit), as

determined by the utility regulator.

Revenue requirements change over time. For example, if a utility

needs to replace or expand its grid, the revenue requirement goes up.

If fuel costs go down, the revenue requirement goes down.

If costs increase, revenue requirement increases

If costs decrease, revenue requirement decreases

How are most rates set?

The utility regulator projects the utility's sales for the next few

years and sets a rate equal to the revenue requirement divided by

estimated sales. If sales are different than expected, the regulator might

direct the utility to change the rate (up or down) so that the utility's

actual revenue closely matches its revenue requirement.

Not all of a utility's customers pay the same electricity rate. Rates

for different types of customers (customer classes) reflect their differing

contributions to the utility's revenue requirement. Generally, large customers

that may be densely located (e.g., industrial customers) pay a lower rate

than small customers that may be less densely located (e.g., residential

customers). Utility regulators typically follow several common principles in

setting rates, including fairly allocating customers' contributions to the

revenue requirement, avoiding overly complex administrative procedures,

and keeping rates relatively stable and simple to understand.

Utility regulators may use variations on the process described above to

incentivize certain customer behaviors or utility performance.

When sales are high relative to the

revenue requirement, rates are low.

When sales are low relative to the

revenue requirement, rates are high.

Information as of November 24, 2026.
