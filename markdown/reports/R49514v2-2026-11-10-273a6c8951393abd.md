resources regarding federal funding for programs that may support local emergency
communications projects. The report provides a brief overview of selected grant and loan
programs organized by agency. Agencies covered include the U.S. Department of Homeland Security’s (DHS’s) Federal
Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), the U.S. Department of Commerce
(DOC), the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), and the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT). For each program
identified, information is included on eligible applicants and allowable costs. Table A-1 includes links to each program’s
website and most recent Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO). Additionally, the report includes selected resources from
the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), in DHS, on identifying emergency communications programs
and funding and sustaining emergency communications projects.

Introduction

This report is intended to provide Members of Congress and their staffs with information and
resources regarding federal funding programs to support local emergency communications
projects. According to the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), during an incident,
“emergency communications may include alerts and warnings; directives about evacuation,
curfews, and other self-protection actions; and information about response status, family
members, available assistance, and other matters that impact response and recovery.”1

This report provides a brief overview of selected grant and loan programs organized by agency.
Agencies covered include the U.S. Department of Homeland Security’s (DHS) Federal
Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), the U.S.
Department of Commerce (DOC), the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), and the U.S.
Department of Transportation (DOT). For each program identified, this report includes the
program’s website, its Assistance Listing Number,2 and information on program eligibility. Table
A-1 includes links to each program’s website and most recent Notice of Funding Opportunity
(NOFO).

This report also highlights resources from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency
(CISA), within DHS, on identifying emergency communications programs and funding and
sustaining emergency communications projects.

Information in this report is intended to assist with a variety of potential emergency
communication circumstances. Thus, the report identifies and describes an array of programs and
funding sources that interested parties could assess in the context of particular situations and
needs. The listed programs and funding sources typically detail the purposes and availability of
the grants to foster such assessments.

Grants vary in funding levels and mechanisms. For some grants, FEMA allocates funds to states
and territories on a formula basis. In these instances, every state and territory receives funding,
but funding varies across jurisdictions based on an assessment of risk of terrorism or disaster. For
other grants, applicants compete for funding based on certain pre-set criteria.

Eligibility also varies across programs. In some cases, FEMA awards grants directly to states.
States then award funding to subrecipients, typically local entities and agencies. For certain
grants, FEMA pre-selects or pre-determines eligible subrecipients. In other cases, tribal or local
entities (e.g., fire departments, transit agencies) apply directly. Typically, these grants are
reviewed, rated, ranked, and selected on a competitive basis.5

FEMA’s Preparedness Grants Manual provides interested parties with information on “policies
and procedures for managing preparedness grants; guidance specific to each grant; [and] rules
and regulations that guide the proper management of FEMA grants.”6

Below are high-level descriptions of various FEMA preparedness grants that could potentially be
used to fund emergency communications projects. The cited sources contain additional details on
the purposes and availability of the grants.

U.S. Department of Commerce (DOC)

The National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA) in DOC has
administered public safety grant programs in the past, including the $1 billion Public Safety
Interoperable Communications grant.73 Previously, NTIA also administered two 911 grant
programs with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.74 NTIA does not currently
administer any public-safety-specific grant programs. However, NTIA does administer several
large broadband programs, including the Tribal Broadband Connectivity Program described
below, which may be used to support public safety communications and connectivity.

DOC, through NTIA, also oversees the First Responder Network Authority (FirstNet) network, a
nationwide broadband network for public safety use.75 While there are no grants affiliated with
FirstNet, revenue earned from subscriber fees is reinvested back into FirstNet.76 Public safety
agencies using FirstNet can work with state officials to convey coverage and service needs to
FirstNet and its partner, AT&T.77

For additional information on FirstNet, see CRS Report R48923, First Responder Network
(FirstNet) Authority: Reauthorization and Selected Issues, by Colby Leigh Pechtol.
