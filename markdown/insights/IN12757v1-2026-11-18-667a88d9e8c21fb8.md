Autonomous Warfare Command: Background
and Considerations for Congress


November 18, 2026

On September 30, 2026, Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth issued a memorandum directing the Direct
Reporting Portfolio Manager for Unmanned Systems (DRPM-UxS) to lead Project Agincourt, an effort to
create a new combatant command (CCMD) known as Autonomous Warfare Command
(AUTOWARCOM). (The Secretary is using “Secretary of War” as a “secondary title” under Executive
Order [EO] 14347 dated September 5, 2025.) According to the memo, the Secretary tasked Project
Agincourt with “[establishing] the pathway to the Autonomous Warfare Command, a 4-star functional
combatant command that will lead the Joint Force in unmanned and autonomous warfare.” The memo
states the project is charged with “[executing] the existing DRPM-UxS mission while prototyping a new
acquisition model for unmanned and autonomous systems.” The memo states the project is to “collaborate
directly with Congress to secure necessary legislation [for the CCMD] with a target establishment date of
October 1, 2027.”

Prior to the Secretary’s issuance of the memo, the Senate Armed Services Committee (SASC) reported to
the Senate its version of a National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2027 (NDAA; S. 4784)
with a provision that would allow the Department of Defense (DOD) to establish a functional CCMD for
robotic and autonomous systems. (The department is “using a secondary Department of War designation”
under EO 14347). The House-passed version of an FY2027 NDAA (H.R. 8800) did not contain a similar
provision. Congress may consider whether or not to enact the Senate committee proposal, the DOD
proposal, or an amended version of the proposals.

Unified Combatant Commands

DOD uses unified CCMDs to conduct continuing, joint (i.e., multiservice) operations. There are
regionally focused and functional CCMDs; functional CCMDs include U.S. Strategic Command
(STRATCOM), Special Operations Command (SOCOM), Transportation Command (TRANSCOM), and
Cyber Command (CYBERCOM). Title 10, Chapter 6, of the U.S. Code generally prescribes the
authorities of CCMDs and commanders. Section 161(a) grants the President, acting through the Secretary
of Defense, the authority to establish unified CCMDs and to prescribe the force structure of those
commands.

Title 10, Chapter 6, generally does not grant CCMDs dedicated personnel or acquisition authorities

Autonomous Warfare Command Concept

Secretary Hegseth announced in a September 30, 2026, speech that AUTOWARCOM is to have “service-
like authorities.” Similarly, the Secretary’s memorandum notes the new command is to “possess directed
manpower, budget, acquisition authorities and create dedicated military career pathways for Officers and
Enlisted personnel.” Per the memo, the DRPM-UxS, with the support of the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs
of Staff (CJCS), was to submit a plan of action and milestones (POAM) for Project Agincourt for
Secretary Hegseth’s approval within 30 days. The POAM was to

         identify priority operational efforts, Service force contributions, force-generation responsibilities,
      command  relationships, DRPM-UxS absorption, test-and-update support, resources, required
          authorities, and milestones for command establishment … assign responsible officials, define initial
         operating capability, and identify measures of adaptation and Service adoption.

The Secretary’s memo does not specify which authorities DOD may request that Congress amend or add,
or potential organizational or operational changes related to its proposal to establish AUTOWARCOM.

The memorandum directed the Secretaries of the military departments, coordinated by the Under
Secretary of Defense for Personnel and Readiness, to include in the POAM “phased manning plans and
proposals to establish or adapt military occupational specialties (MOSs), ratings, or equivalent career
fields for unmanned and autonomous systems.” (The Under Secretary is using “Under Secretary of War”
as a “secondary title” under EO 14347.)

Proposed Robotic and Autonomous Systems Command

Section 917 of S. 4784 would amend Title 10, Chapter 6, of the U.S. Code to authorize, with the advice
and assistance of the CJCS, the President, through the Secretary of Defense, to establish a unified CCMD
for robotic and autonomous systems called the “United States Robotic and Autonomous Systems
Command,” or RASCOM. The RASCOM commander would be responsible for “all functions relating to
robotic and autonomous systems assigned by the Secretary of Defense” and not “space systems;
autonomous cyber capabilities; nuclear command, control, and communications systems; integrated air
and missile defense sensing; or Integrated Tactical Warning and Attack Assessment capabilities.” The
provision would require the military services to establish service component commands and to create
dedicated career paths, MOSs or ratings, and training pipelines for RASCOM personnel. The provision
would require the RASCOM commander to establish a joint subordinate unified command to generate
requirements for robotic and autonomous systems; ensure interoperability; plan and conduct joint
exercises; and develop tactics, techniques, and procedures, among other duties.

Section 917 would provide RASCOM with operational test and evaluation authorities, as well as limited
acquisition authorities. Under the provision, the “Secretary of Defense may delegate contracting
authorities to the Commander of RASCOM sufficient to enable the Commander … to procure limited
quantities of robotic and autonomous systems equipment and associated capabilities” for certain purposes
and to procure, operate, and sustain specified robotic and autonomous systems equipment and
capabilities. This authority would be limited to contracts not to exceed $20 million.

Considerations for Congress

SASC Chair Senator Roger Wicker, who supports the SASC provision, has asserted that RASCOM
“would standardize the way the entire armed services use unmanned systems in combat, and … help
service branches work effectively with each other when using robotics and drones.” A retired Air Force
lieutenant general, who opposes the proposal, has argued that a separate command would “risk building a
bureaucracy around technology rather than accelerating its practical application in combat.”

Congress may consider whether or not to provide AUTOWARCOM with DOD’s request for “service-like
authorities,” including dedicated personnel and acquisition authorities. It may consider whether or not to
provide the command with the SASC-proposed operational test and evaluation authorities and limited
acquisition authorities. Congress may consider whether or not to authorize and appropriate funding for the
command. It may consider the sufficiency of information available to assess the implications of the
command’s establishment.
