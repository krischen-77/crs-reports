## Summary

Under Section 18 of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act, as amended (OCSLA; 43 U.S.C. §1344), the Secretary of the Interior must prepare and maintain forward-looking plans—typically referred to as *national programs* or *five-year programs*—that indicate proposed public oil and gas lease sales in U.S. waters. In doing so, the Secretary must balance national interests in energy supply and environmental protection. The lead agency within the Department of the Interior (DOI or Interior) responsible for the program has been the Bureau of Ocean Energy Management (BOEM). Under a July 2026 order issued by the Secretary, BOEM and a sister agency are being merged into a new agency within DOI to be called the Marine Minerals Administration.

BOEM's development of a five-year program typically has taken place over two or three years, during which successive drafts of the program are published for review and comment. At the end of the process, the Secretary must submit each program to the President and to Congress for a period of at least 60 days, after which the proposal may be approved by the Secretary and may take effect with no further regulatory or legislative action. In the past, BOEM also developed a programmatic environmental impact statement (PEIS) for the leasing program under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA; 42 U.S.C. §4321). The PEIS examined the potential environmental impacts from oil and gas exploration and development on the U.S. outer continental shelf and considered a reasonable range of alternatives to the proposed plan.

Congress typically has been actively involved during the planning phases of BOEM's five-year leasing programs. For example, some Members of Congress have conveyed their views on BOEM's proposals by submitting public comments on draft versions of programs during formal comment periods. Congress also has evaluated programs in committee oversight hearings. Further, although Congress does not play a direct role under the OCSLA in the approval or disapproval of a leasing program, Congress has sometimes influenced the terms of five-year programs through legislation, such as legislation to mandate or prohibit lease sales in certain offshore areas. For example, in the 119th Congress, P.L. 119-21 mandated 12 lease sales (9 for the Gulf of America region and 3 for the Alaska region) in the 2024-2029 period, in addition to the 3 sales already scheduled by the Biden Administration in its 2024-2029 leasing program.

Since 1980, 10 distinct five-year programs and a revised version of one program have been submitted to Congress. The five-year programs have reflected the offshore oil and gas leasing policies of different presidential Administrations, along with input from states, Members of Congress, and other stakeholders. Most recently, in September 2023, DOI submitted BOEM's offshore oil and gas leasing program for 2024-2029, and then-Secretary of the Interior Deb Haaland issued a record of decision approving the program in December 2023. In November 2025, under the second Trump Administration, BOEM published a draft proposed program for 2026-2031 that would replace some years in the current 2024-2029 program. BOEM stated that it would not prepare a PEIS in conjunction with this program. For discussion of recent activities and issues for Congress, see CRS Report R44692, *Five-Year Offshore Oil and Gas Leasing Program: Status and Issues in Brief*.

## Introduction

Under the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (OCSLA), as amended,[1](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn1) the Department of the Interior (DOI or Interior) must prepare and maintain forward-looking five-year plans—referred to by DOI as *national programs* or *five-year programs*—that indicate proposed public oil and gas lease sales on the U.S. outer continental shelf (OCS) over a five-year period.[2](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn2) In preparing each program, DOI must balance national interests in energy supply and environmental protection.[3](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn3) Since 2011, the lead agency within DOI responsible for the program has been the Bureau of Ocean Energy Management (BOEM).[4](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn4) In July 2026, the Secretary of the Interior directed the merger of BOEM and a sister agency, the Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement (BSEE), in a new agency within DOI to be called the Marine Minerals Administration, which will administer offshore energy leasing along with safety and enforcement functions.[5](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn5)

The development of a five-year program typically takes place over two or three years, during which successive drafts of the program are published for review and comment.[6](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn6) All available leasing areas are initially examined,[7](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn7) and the selection may then be narrowed based on economic and environmental analysis to arrive at a final leasing schedule. At the end of the process, the Secretary of the Interior must submit each program to the President and to Congress for a period of at least 60 days, after which the proposal may be approved by the Secretary and may take effect with no further regulatory or legislative action.[8](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn8)

The planning process has typically included preparation of a programmatic environmental impact statement (PEIS) under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA).[9](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn9) The PEIS examines the potential environmental impacts from oil and gas exploration and development on the OCS and considers a reasonable range of alternatives to the proposed plan. Public comments from stakeholders, including state governors, companies, individuals, and public interest organizations, are addressed in both the PEIS and the five-year program itself. For the five-year program currently in preparation—a program for the years 2026-2031 that would replace some years in the most recent (2024-2029) program—BOEM announced that the program process would not include NEPA analysis, based on judicial rulings "that found that NEPA was unripe at the National OCS Program stage."[10](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn10)

Because of the stages of review and comment required under the OCSLA, BOEM generally could not revise a finalized program—for example, to add new sales—without restarting the program development process.[11](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn11) However, scheduled sales could potentially be cancelled (but not added) during implementation of the program, based on requirements for environmental review that are associated with each individual sale.

The leasing decisions in BOEM's five-year programs may affect the economy and environment of individual coastal states and of the nation as a whole. Accordingly, Congress often has been involved in the planning process for the five-year programs. Under the OCSLA, Congress's review of BOEM's final program does not include approval or disapproval of the program. However, Members of Congress may influence the program in other ways. Members may convey their views on the Administration's proposals by submitting public comments on draft versions of the program during formal comment periods, and they may evaluate the program in committee oversight hearings.[12](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn12) More directly, Members may introduce legislation to set or alter a program's terms, such as by prohibiting or mandating specific sales. For example, in the 119th Congress, P.L. 119-21 mandated 12 lease sales (9 for the Gulf of America region and 3 for the Alaska region) in the 2024-2029 period in addition to the 3 lease sales already scheduled by the Biden Administration in its 2024-2029 leasing program.[13](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn13)

This report discusses the history and legal framework for DOI's five-year leasing programs, outlines the program development process, summarizes previous programs, and discusses the role of Congress. The five-year program currently in force, which was prepared during the Biden Administration and covers the years 2024-2029, was approved by then-Secretary of the Interior Deb Haaland in December 2023.[14](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn14) This program scheduled three lease sales for the 2024-2029 period, located in specified planning areas of the Gulf of Mexico region of the OCS. (BOEM renamed this region as the Gulf of America region in 2025, pursuant to Executive Order 14172.)[15](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn15) Prior to the December 2023 approval of the 2024-2029 program, a gap occurred between leasing programs, the first prolonged gap since publication of the programs began in 1980.[16](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn16) In November 2025, under the second Trump Administration, BOEM published a draft proposed program for 2026-2031.[17](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn17) For further discussion of the 2026-2031 program, recent actions, and issues for Congress, see CRS Report R44692, *Five-Year Offshore Oil and Gas Leasing Program: Status and Issues in Brief*.

## Historical Background[18](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn18)

In 1953, Congress enacted two laws that addressed jurisdiction and rights off the coasts of the United States, including rights to regulation of subsurface oil and natural gas exploration and production. The first of these acts, the Submerged Lands Act,[19](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn19) provides that coastal states are generally entitled to an area extending 3 geographical miles[20](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn20) from their officially recognized coasts (or baselines).[21](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn21) The second, the OCSLA, defined the OCS as "all submerged lands lying seaward and outside of" state coastal waters that are subject to the jurisdiction and control of the United States.[22](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn22) The OCSLA has as its primary purpose "expeditious and orderly development [of OCS resources], subject to environmental safeguards, in a manner which is consistent with the maintenance of competition and other national needs."[23](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn23)

As offshore activities expanded in the years following adoption of the OCSLA, Congress sought a means by which to allow for expedited exploration and production in order to achieve national energy goals while also providing for environmental protection, opportunities for state and local governments affected by offshore activity to have their voices heard, and a competitive bidding and leasing process.[24](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn24) The product was the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act Amendments of 1978.[25](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn25) This legislation added a number of new provisions to the OCSLA, including Section 18, which mandates the creation and maintenance of an OCS leasing program to "best meet national energy needs for the five-year period following its approval or reapproval."[26](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn26) These five-year programs, which include schedules for lease auctions, have provided the framework for OCS oil and gas exploration and production since the first one was adopted by DOI in 1980.

Although the 1978 amendments were the last major overhaul to the OCSLA, Congress has taken other actions since that time that have altered the scope of offshore oil and gas exploration and production. For example, the Deep Water Royalty Relief Act of 1995 attempted to encourage exploration and production in deep water by providing relief from otherwise applicable royalty payment requirements for some deepwater oil and natural gas production.[27](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn27) The Gulf of Mexico Energy Security Act of 2006 directed the leasing of certain regions of the Gulf of Mexico for oil and gas exploration and production and placed a moratorium on leasing in other regions.[28](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn28) It also created a mechanism for sharing revenues from leasing in the region with Gulf states and the Land and Water Conservation Fund.[29](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn29) Also, starting in 2008, Congress removed language from annual Interior appropriations legislation that had been in place to bar leasing and related activities in certain OCS regions.[30](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn30) These legislative actions helped to shape subsequent five-year programs.

## Legal Framework[31](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn31)

The statutory framework governing development of a five-year offshore oil and gas leasing program includes the OCSLA as well as other federal statutes, particularly NEPA and the Coastal Zone Management Act (CZMA).[32](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn32)

### Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act

Section 18 of the OCSLA provides:

The Secretary [of the Interior] ... shall prepare and periodically revise, and maintain an oil and gas leasing program to implement the policies of this subchapter. The leasing program shall consist of a schedule of proposed lease sales indicating, as precisely as possible, the size, timing, and location of leasing activity which he determines will best meet national energy needs for the five-year period following its approval or reapproval.[33](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn33)

Section 18 further provides that the OCS is to be managed in a manner "which considers economic, social, and environmental values" of the resources of the OCS as well as the potential impact of oil and gas exploration on the marine, coastal, and human environments.[34](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn34)

Specifically, Section 18 directs the Secretary of the Interior to schedule the timing and location of oil and gas exploration and production among the regions of the OCS based on consideration of a variety of factors, including existing geographical, geological, and ecological characteristics of the regions; relative environmental and other natural resource considerations of the regions; the relative interest of oil and natural gas producers in the regions; and the laws, goals, and policies of the states that would be affected by offshore exploration and production in the region. In addition to striking this balance, leasing under the five-year program must "be conducted to assure receipt of fair market value for the lands leased and the rights conveyed by the Federal Government."[35](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn35) The OCSLA also requires that the five-year program include estimates on appropriations and staffing needs.[36](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn36)

The OCSLA also imposes a number of consultation requirements. During preparation of the five-year program, the Secretary of the Interior must "invite and consider suggestions for such program from any interested Federal agency, including the Attorney General, in consultation with the Federal Trade Commission, and from the Governor of any State which may become an affected State under such proposed program."[37](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn37) In addition to these mandatory consultation requirements, the Secretary may choose to consult with local government officials in affected states.[38](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn38)

Once the Secretary has satisfied these consultation and other requirements and prepared a proposed program, that program must be submitted to the governor of each affected state for further comments at least 60 days prior to publication of the proposed program in the *Federal Register*.[39](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn39) The OCSLA also authorizes the Attorney General, in coordination with the Federal Trade Commission, to submit comments regarding potential effects of the proposed program on competition.[40](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn40) At least 60 days prior to the program's approval, the Secretary must submit the proposed program to Congress and the President, along with an explanation as to why any specific recommendation of the Attorney General or a state or local government was not accepted.[41](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn41) Once these steps have been completed, the Secretary is free to approve a final five-year program. The OCSLA also authorizes the Secretary to revise the five-year program at any time pursuant to a mandated review, but any revision that is "significant" must go through the process for the initial five-year program described above.[42](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn42)

DOI regulations applicable to preparation of the five-year program include details regarding these consultation requirements. For example, DOI is required to send letters to governors of affected states requesting that they identify specific laws, goals, and policies that they would like DOI to consider during preparation of the five-year program.[43](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn43) The regulations also outline requirements for publication of the proposed program in the *Federal Register*.

### Other Federal Statutes

While the OCSLA and the applicable regulations guide the five-year planning process, other federal statutes also play a role in the program's formation. Two federal statutes that have played a prominent role in the preparation of the five-year program are NEPA and the CZMA.

#### National Environmental Policy Act

NEPA generally requires federal agencies to consider the environmental impacts (i.e., through an environmental review) of their actions prior to finalizing certain decisions.[44](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn44) How a federal agency demonstrates compliance with NEPA depends on the level of the proposed action's impacts.[45](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn45) A proposed action that has a "reasonably foreseeable significant effect on the human environment" typically requires the preparation of an environmental impact statement (EIS).[46](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn46) If the proposed action does not have a reasonably foreseeable significant effect on the quality of the human environment, or if the significance of the effects is unknown, an agency typically prepares an environmental assessment (EA).[47](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn47) BOEM follows DOI regulations and departmental guidance to carry out the NEPA environmental review process.[48](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn48)

Historically, BOEM has prepared a PEIS[49](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn49) concurrently with preparation of the five-year program. However, when it published the most recent draft proposed program for 2026-2031, BOEM noted that it would not be preparing a PEIS for the program.[50](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn50) The agency described past reviews as "discretionary," and cited recent judicial decisions that it described as concluding that the five-year program "does not constitute an irreversible and irretrievable commitment of resources" and that "the obligation to fully comply with NEPA does not mature until the lease sale stage."[51](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn51) The agency stated that it would, instead, prepare "an environmental analysis outside of the NEPA framework to provide the relevant environmental and predictive information for different areas of the OCS as required by Section 18" of the OCSLA, as discussed above.[52](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn52)

#### Coastal Zone Management Act[53](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn53)

Under the CZMA,[54](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn54) states are encouraged to enact coastal zone management plans to coordinate protection of habitats and resources in coastal waters.[55](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn55) The CZMA establishes a policy of preservation alongside sustainable use and development compatible with resource protection.[56](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn56) State coastal zone management programs that are approved by the Secretary of Commerce are eligible to receive federal monetary and technical assistance. State programs must designate conservation measures and permissible uses for land and water resources,[57](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn57) and must address various sources of water pollution.[58](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn58)

The CZMA also requires that the federal government and federally permitted activities comply with these state programs.[59](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn59) To that end, the regulations governing the five-year program provide that "[i]n development of the leasing program, consideration shall be given to the coastal zone management program being developed or administered by an affected coastal State."[60](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn60) The regulations require DOI to request information concerning the relationship between a state's coastal zone management program and OCS oil and gas activity from the governors of affected coastal states and the Secretary of Commerce prior to development of the leasing program.[61](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn61)

## Five-Year Program Development Process[62](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn62)

The development of a five-year program typically takes place over two or three years, during which three successive versions of the program proposal are published for review. The process can sometimes take longer; for the 2024-2029 program, program development spanned a six-year period over two presidential Administrations.[63](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn63) Conversely, BOEM projected a shorter development period for its proposed 2026-2031 program, with an initial request for information for the program published in April 2025 and the first lease sales projected in 2026.[64](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn64) The program drafts are submitted to state governors and federal agencies and, in the final stage, to Congress and the President (see discussion of consultation requirements in the "[Legal Framework](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#_Ref445913527)" section, above). The second and final program proposals have typically been released concurrently with draft and final versions of the PEIS prepared under NEPA, which also are opened to public comments (although, as described previously, BOEM is not preparing a PEIS for the 2026-2031 program). After the program takes effect, individual lease sales also undergo NEPA compliance, as do companies' exploration and development plans on leased tracts.[65](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn65) [**Figure** **1**](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#_Ref421021224) outlines the steps from development of the five-year program to actual oil and gas production in an individual well.

Because of the analysis and review undertaken at each stage of drafting the five-year program, the successive drafts represent a winnowing process. The initial draft proposed program (DPP) examines all of the agency's available planning areas for oil and gas leasing,[66](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn66) analyzing them according to factors in Section 18 of the OCSLA and considering public input, to develop an initial schedule of proposed lease sales. In subsequent versions of the program, BOEM might remove proposed sales on the basis of further analysis, but could not add new sales without reverting to an earlier stage of the process and undertaking new environmental reviews.

The steps of the process are discussed in greater detail below, with examples from the 2024-2029 program, the most recent five-year program to complete the development process. This summary refers to BOEM because it was the DOI agency that published the 2024-2029 program, but as discussed above, as of July 2026, DOI is merging BOEM and its sister agency BSEE into a new Marine Minerals Administration.

* **Step 1. Request for Information****.** BOEM initiates development of a new five-year program by publishing in the *Federal Register* a request for information (RFI) from interested parties concerning regional and national energy needs for the next five-year period; leasing interests of possible oil and gas producers; environmental concerns; and concerns of state and local governments, tribes, and the public, among other issues. For example, the RFI for what became the 2024-2029 leasing program was published on July 3, 2017, and was followed by a comment period during which the agency received more than 800,000 comments.[67](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn67) At the time, the leasing program was envisioned as covering a five-year period from 2019 through 2024.
* **Step 2. Draft Proposed Program/Notice of Intent for PEIS****.** BOEM publishes a DPP, the first of three program decision documents.[68](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn68) The DPP analyzes all OCS planning areas available for leasing, taking into account comments received on the RFI, and identifies a preliminary lease schedule. BOEM published its DPP for what became the 2024-2029 program (at that time planned as a 2019-2024 program) on January 8, 2018, with a comment period that closed on March 9, 2018.[69](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn69) The DPP was published under the Trump Administration. The DPP's preliminary lease schedule consisted of 47 proposed oil and gas lease sales spanning all four offshore planning regions (Gulf of Mexico [now Gulf of America], Alaska, Atlantic, and Pacific).[70](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn70) BOEM received more than 2 million comments on the DPP.[71](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn71)

When the DPP is published, BOEM also typically has issued a notice of intent (NOI) to publish a PEIS for the proposed lease areas and has sought public input (through a scoping process) on the issues that should be analyzed in the PEIS. The NOI to prepare a PEIS for what became the 2024-2029 program (at that time planned as a 2019-2024 program) was published on January 8, 2018, along with the DPP.[72](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn72) As discussed, for its proposed 2026-2031 program, BOEM announced its intent not to prepare a PEIS.[73](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn73)

* **Step 3. Proposed Program/Draft PEIS****.** After further analyzing the lease sale areas proposed in the DPP according to the required factors in Section 18 of the OCSLA, and taking into account the public comments received on the DPP, BOEM publishes a proposed program (PP). This second version refines the proposed locations and timing for OCS oil and gas lease sales. BOEM submits the PP to Congress, state governors, and relevant federal agencies and also solicits public comment on the program.[74](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn74) BOEM published the PP for what became the 2024-2029 program on July 8, 2022, with a comment period that closed on October 6, 2022.[75](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn75) At that time, the PP was envisioned to cover the years 2023-2028. The PP, published under the Biden Administration, revised the draft leasing schedule from the DPP to narrow it to a maximum of 10 proposed lease sales spanning two offshore planning regions (Gulf of Mexico [now Gulf of America] and Alaska).

The PP has typically been accompanied by a draft PEIS analyzing the OCS areas that were identified for leasing at the DPP stage (although BOEM has stated that the 2026-2031 program will not include a PEIS). The draft PEIS for what became the 2024-2029 program (at that time covering the 2023-2028 period) was released along with the PP, with a comment period that closed on October 6, 2022.[76](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn76) BOEM received more than 750,000 comments on the PP and draft PEIS.[77](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn77)

* **Step 4. Proposed Final Program/Final PEIS****.** The final document published by BOEM is the proposed final program (PFP), which is based on additional analysis of the factors in Section 18 of the OCSLA, along with analysis of the public comments received on the PP and draft PEIS. The PFP is announced in the *Federal Register* and submitted to the President and Congress for a period of at least 60 days. Although Congress does not have an approval role for the PFP, the 60-day review period could allow for legislation to be introduced that would influence the outcome of the program. BOEM published the PFP for 2024-2029 on September 29, 2023.[78](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn78) It contained a final refinement of the proposed lease schedule, narrowing it to three sales in one offshore planning region (the Gulf of Mexico [now Gulf of America] region).

Along with the PFP, BOEM typically has published a final PEIS that concludes the analysis of the areas proposed for leasing (although BOEM has stated that the 2026-2031 program will not include a PEIS). The final PEIS is submitted to the President and Congress along with the PFP. BOEM released the final PEIS for the 2017-2022 program on September 29, 2023, along with the PFP.[79](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn79)

* **Step 5. Approval of PFP by Secretary** **of the Interior****.** At least 60 days after the PFP is submitted to the President and Congress, the Secretary of the Interior may approve the PFP, which then becomes final. The Secretary publishes a record of decision (ROD) for the final program.[80](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn80) Secretary of the Interior Deb Haaland issued a record of decision approving the final program for 2024-2029 on December 14, 2023.[81](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn81)

## Role of Congress

Congress can influence the Administration's development of a five-year program in a number of ways. Members of Congress may convey their views on the Administration's proposals by submitting public comments on program drafts during the formal comment periods, and they may evaluate the program in committee oversight hearings. More directly, Members may introduce legislation to set or alter a program's terms. Congress also has a role under the OCSLA of reviewing each five-year program once it is finalized, but as discussed above, the OCSLA does not require that Congress directly approve the final program in order for it to be implemented.

As examples of these types of participation, this section summarizes congressional action on the 2024-2029 program, the most recent completed program. For developments related to the upcoming 2026-2031 program, see CRS Report R44692, *Five-Year Offshore Oil and Gas Leasing Program: Status and Issues in Brief*.

### Public Comment

Members of Congress, along with other stakeholders such as state governors, interested agencies and organizations, and members of the public, may submit comments on draft versions of five-year programs. For the 2024-2029 program, BOEM received 12 comments from Members of Congress on its initial RFI, 49 comments from Members on the DPP, and 3 comments from Members on the PP. Some of these comments came from one or a few Members, and others had many signers (in some cases, 130 Members or more).[84](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn84) Some comments opposed the inclusion of certain regions in the program, whereas others supported the proposed lease sales or sought an expansion of lease areas and a higher number of sales.[85](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn85) The comments also addressed related issues such as seismic testing in the Atlantic.[86](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn86)

DOI takes the public comments into account when developing successive drafts of a five-year program. Each draft contains an appendix summarizing the substantive comments received on the previous version, including those from Members of Congress, and explaining the agency's response to each.[87](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn87) The agency may revise the program to partially or fully adopt a suggestion, or may explain why it declined to do so.

### Oversight Hearings

The House or Senate may hold oversight hearings to evaluate a proposed five-year oil and gas leasing program. Such hearings help to inform Members in their legislative decisionmaking concerning the program and provide an opportunity for agency officials to hear Members' views. For example, during development of the 2024-2029 program, committees in both chambers held hearings on the program and the offshore oil and gas strategies of both the Biden and Trump Administrations.[88](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn88) Members and witnesses addressed issues such as the Biden Administration's temporary pause on development of the leasing program, the optimal number of sales for the program, and economic and environmental impacts of offshore oil and gas development.

### Legislation

Under the OCSLA, the final version of each five-year program must be submitted to Congress for a period of 60 days before the Secretary of the Interior can approve and implement the program.[89](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn89) During or outside the 60-day period, Congress could consider legislation to alter the program. Legislation could, for example, remove scheduled lease sales, add new lease sales, or make broader changes to the program. The 118th and 119th Congresses both considered legislation to alter the 2024-2029 program, and one enacted measure, P.L. 119-21, mandated 12 lease sales in the 2024-2029 period in addition to the program's 3 scheduled lease sales. Other bills in the 119th Congress include measures to change the terms of program development or to prohibit leasing consideration for certain areas.[90](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-14_R44504_4085ccd0c184f23921426e1c72f798e4dbfadb1c.html#fn90)
