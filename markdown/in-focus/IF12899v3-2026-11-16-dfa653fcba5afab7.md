Because chips in an AI data center require massive amounts
of power to run, the facility’s maximum computing
capacity is directly bound by its available power supply. In
turn, how much electric power the data center can draw at
its peak workload—measured in megawatts (MW; 1 MW =
1 million watts) or gigawatts (GW; 1 GW = 1,000 MW)—
has become the primary metric when referring to its scale.
For example, according to some industry analysts, to be
considered a “hyperscale data center,” the facility should be
able to draw over 100 MW of power at any moment, house
at least 5,000 computer servers, and occupy at least 10,000
square feet. A data center running at a continuous demand
of 100 MW for 24 hours would consume 2,400 MW-hours
(MWh) of energy. That single-day electricity consumption
