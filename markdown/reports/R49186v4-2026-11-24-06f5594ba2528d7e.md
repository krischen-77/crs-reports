Secretary Rubio said that “the United States has significant influence over the direction that these talks go and the results that
we get from them”; Secretary Rubio further stated that sanctions could be used to ensure that the acting government fulfills
its commitments. The October 25, 2026, surprise return of opposition leader María Corina Machado to Venezuela—after 10
months in exile and several failed attempts to enter the country—has put pressure on U.S. and acting officials to hasten the
transition process. Backed by the Unitary Platform of opposition parties, Machado is advocating for elections in 2027. U.S.
officials have not called for a specific date but have indicated that elections could be held in late 2027 or early 2028.

Following Maduro’s arrest, some in Congress have praised the Trump Administration for pressuring the acting government
to cut ties with U.S. adversaries and follow U.S. guidance in opening its economy and confronting illegal armed groups.
Others have sought to shape how democracy and human rights aims are balanced against other U.S. goals in Venezuela and
pressed for transparency in U.S. economic deals with the acting government through legislation and hearings and other
oversight measures. Congress may consider legislation to shape U.S. policy toward Venezuela and conduct oversight of U.S.
engagement—such as reaching economic and military deals—with the acting government.

Congress appropriated $50 million in democracy funding for programs in Venezuela in the National Security, Department of
State, and Related Programs (NSRP) Appropriations Act, 2026 (P.L. 119-75, Division F). The House-passed version of the
FY2027 NSRP appropriations bill (H.R. 8595) would direct $50 million for democracy programs for Venezuela. The act and
accompanying report, H.Rept. 119-631, would require a report from the Secretary of State within 30 days of the bill’s
enactment on the strategy needed to advance a democratic transition in Venezuela with benchmarks and timelines. The House
Foreign Affairs Committee ordered reported legislation (H.R. 7674) that would require the Secretary of State to submit a
strategy to support a democratic transition in Venezuela. A bipartisan Senate-introduced resolution (S.Res. 829) states that
U.S. policy aims to support a peaceful transition to democracy in Venezuela through free and fair elections. A Senate-
introduced bill (S. 4809) would, among other provisions, require a multipronged U.S. strategy to support elections in
Venezuela. A Senate-introduced resolution (S.Res. 932), pursuant to Section 502B of the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961, as
amended (22 U.S.C. §2304), would require the State Department to provide a report within 30 days of the resolution’s
adoption on human rights practices in Venezuela.

Stability

forcibly removed from power.33 Nearly a year since Maduro’s ouster, those predictions have not

response to an October U.S.-backed lethal strike on an ELN camp near Colombia.34 A June U.S.
strike reportedly killed a leader of TdA.35 In November 2026, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth

intelligence-sharing, and possibly the provision of defense equipment to help Venezuela confront

Prospects for Elections

Machado’s surprise return from exile in October has arguably given new urgency for the talks to
result in further reforms; the Unitary Platform has endorsed her candidacy as the opposition
candidate for presidential elections, and Machado has called for elections to occur in 2027.117 In
September, Acting President Rodríguez told the UN General Assembly that “there will be
elections” in Venezuela, but that the country is already being “reborn” under her leadership.118
Prior to Machado’s first press conference on October 29 after returning from exile, Rodríguez
gave a national address in which she said that elections would occur under a new CNE but
refrained from predicting when they would occur.119

Congressional Considerations

Since the removal of Maduro, observers have debated governance challenges, including the
prospects for a return to democracy in Venezuela. On July 15, 2026, the House passed its version
of the NSRP Appropriations Act (H.R. 8595); the accompanying report, H.Rept. 119-631, would
require a report from the Secretary of State within 30 days of the bill’s enactment on the strategy
needed to advance a democratic transition in Venezuela. Congress may continue to address these
issues, as well as how to balance democracy and human rights concerns against other U.S. goals
in Venezuela through legislation, such as appropriations and sanctions statutes, as well as through
letters, hearings, investigations, and other oversight measures. Congress could amend or enact
broad legislation to shape U.S. policy, like the VERDAD Act of 2019 (P.L. 116-94, Division J,
Title I), or consider targeted measures to address specific issues.120 Experts have urged U.S.
policymakers to consider issues including, but not limited to, how to reform and professionalize
Venezuela’s security forces and restructure and diversify its economy.121

Secretary of State to update, with input from relevant congressional committees, the State
Department’s March 2020 Democratic Transition Framework for Venezuela; officials in the
second Trump Administration have not publicly mentioned this framework.122 The bill would,
